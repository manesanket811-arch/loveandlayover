Dubai with Family: A Relaxed 5-Day Itinerary

A paced plan for kids and grandparents alike, with visa tips, costs in rupees and plenty of Indian food along the way.

8 min readUpdated November 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: Guilhem Vellut from Annecy, France, CC BY 2.0

Dubai is one of the easiest international trips for an Indian family: short flights, Indian food everywhere, very safe streets and attractions that work for every age. The trick is pacing, because Dubai is spread out and the afternoons can be hot. This plan keeps mornings and evenings busy and afternoons easy.

Before you go

Visa

Indian passport holders need a visa for the UAE. Most families apply for a tourist e-visa online through their airline (Emirates or flydubai, for example), a licensed travel agent or the official immigration portal. Every family member, including babies, needs their own visa. Indians with a valid US visa or green card, or a UK or EU residence permit or visa, may be eligible for a visa on arrival. Check the official website before you travel.

When to go

November to March is the best time, with warm days and pleasant evenings. Global Village and the Miracle Garden are only open in the cooler months, roughly from autumn to spring. Summer (June to September) is very hot, so outdoor sightseeing is limited to early mornings and evenings.

Where to stay

  • Downtown Dubai: walk to the Burj Khalifa, Dubai Mall and the fountains. Great for a first visit.
  • Dubai Marina or JBR: beach, promenade and lots of family restaurants.
  • Bur Dubai or Al Karama: cheaper, close to the souks and full of Indian restaurants.
Our tipWith grandparents, pick a hotel right next to a metro station or book a family room in Downtown. Less walking in the heat makes every day better.

5-day family itinerary

Day 1: Old Dubai Easy arrival day

Settle in, then head to Al Fahidi historic district in the late afternoon. Take an abra (wooden boat) across Dubai Creek for a small fare and wander the gold and spice souks. Dinner in Bur Dubai or Karama, where Indian restaurants are everywhere.

Low walkingGood for all ages

Day 2: Downtown and the Burj Khalifa The big one

Spend the morning in Dubai Mall: the Dubai Aquarium tunnel is free to see from outside, and kids love the ice rink. Book Burj Khalifa's At the Top for late afternoon to see the city in daylight and at sunset, then watch the Dubai Fountain show from the promenade (free, every 30 minutes in the evening).

Book aheadStroller friendly

Day 3: Desert safari Afternoon and evening

Keep the morning free for the pool. In the afternoon, a desert safari picks you up from your hotel: dune drive, camel ride, sandboarding, then a barbecue dinner with shows at a desert camp. Most operators offer vegetarian food. With grandparents or small kids, ask for a gentle dune drive or a camp-only option.

Hotel pickupVeg dinner on request

Day 4: A theme park day Pick one

Choose by age: IMG Worlds of Adventure (indoor, good on hot days), Dubai Parks and Resorts (Legoland is great for under-10s), or Atlantis Aquaventure on the Palm for older kids who love water slides. In the cooler months, end the day at Global Village for food from around the world and shows.

Day 5: Marina, beach and the Frame Slow finish

Morning at JBR beach or Kite Beach, then the Dubai Frame for views of old and new Dubai. In season, add the Miracle Garden. Finish with a Dubai Marina dhow dinner cruise, a relaxed last evening that grandparents usually love.

How much it costs

In our Dubai guide, a mid-range day (hotel shared by two, food, transport and one big attraction) comes to about ₹9,200–16,000 per adult. Children usually cost less, since many attractions have reduced child tickets and small kids often get in free. Flights and visas are extra.

Save moneyBook big attractions online in advance, as walk-up prices are usually higher. Eat in Karama or Bur Dubai, where a filling Indian meal costs a fraction of mall prices, and use the metro instead of taxis for city sightseeing.

Food for Indian families

Food is the easy part. Dubai has a huge Indian community, so you'll find South Indian breakfasts, Gujarati thalis, Jain food on request and North Indian restaurants across the city, especially in Karama, Bur Dubai and Al Barsha. Malls have Indian chains in their food courts too.

Family tips

  • Carry water and caps everywhere, even in winter. The sun is strong.
  • Dress modestly in souks, malls and mosques: cover shoulders and knees.
  • The metro has a Gold Class carriage and a women and children carriage, both useful at busy times.
  • Most attractions have lifts and ramps, so strollers and wheelchairs are easy to manage.

FAQs

Is Dubai good for a family trip from India?

Yes. Flights are short, Indian food is everywhere, the city is very safe and there are attractions for every age, from the Burj Khalifa and desert safaris to theme parks and beaches.

Do children need a visa for Dubai?

Yes. Every Indian family member, including babies, needs their own UAE visa. Most families apply for a tourist e-visa online through their airline, a licensed travel agent or the official portal.

How many days are enough for Dubai with family?

Five days lets you see the main sights at a relaxed pace, with a pool or beach break built in. Three days covers the highlights if you're short on time.

What is the best month to visit Dubai with kids?

November to March, when the weather is warm but comfortable and seasonal attractions like Global Village are open.
