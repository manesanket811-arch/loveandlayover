Healthcare in Singapore for Indian Expats

Doctors, hospitals and costs for foreigners, what employer insurance covers, and how to protect your family.

7 min readUpdated November 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: Mustang Joe, CC0

Singapore's healthcare is among the best in the world, but as a foreigner you pay full, non-subsidised rates. Here's how it works, what it costs, and how to make sure you and your family are covered.

As of November 2026Rules change, and this is a general guide from our experience, not legal, tax or immigration advice. Always confirm with your insurer, your employer's HR team and the clinic or hospital for your own case.

How the system works

  • GP clinics: your first stop for most illnesses. There are clinics in every neighbourhood, many open late.
  • Polyclinics: government clinics. Foreigners pay non-resident rates, so they're not always cheaper than a private GP.
  • Public hospitals (e.g. SGH, NUH, Tan Tock Seng) and private hospitals (e.g. Mount Elizabeth, Gleneagles): for specialists, surgery and inpatient care.
  • A&E (emergency): at hospitals, for genuine emergencies. Call 995 for an ambulance.

What it costs (approximate)

ServiceTypical cost for foreigners
GP consultationAbout S$50–125, plus medicine
Polyclinic consultationNon-resident rates, often similar to a private GP
A&E visit (public hospital)Often S$130–200+ before tests and treatment
Private hospital stayHundreds to over a thousand dollars a night, plus doctor and procedure fees

Prices vary widely, so ask the clinic upfront. Foreigners don't get MediShield Life or MediSave, which is why insurance matters so much.

Employer insurance: check what it covers

Most employers provide group medical insurance. Check:

  • Outpatient GP visits: often at panel clinics with little or no payment.
  • Hospitalisation and surgery limits
  • Specialists and dental: dental is often limited or not covered.
  • Your spouse and children: many policies cover only the employee unless you pay to add family.
  • Maternity: often excluded or capped, which matters if you're planning a baby here.
Fill the gapsIf your family isn't covered, buy an individual or family health plan soon after you arrive. Pre-existing conditions may be excluded, so the earlier the better. For visiting parents, buy travel insurance with medical cover before they fly.

Medicines

  • Pharmacies like Guardian and Watsons sell common over-the-counter medicines; stronger ones need a doctor's prescription.
  • When moving or travelling, carry personal medicines in their original packaging with a prescription. Some medicines, especially controlled ones, need approval from the Health Sciences Authority (HSA) before you bring them in.

Sick leave

If you're unwell, see a doctor and get a medical certificate (MC). It's the standard way to take sick leave in Singapore, and most employers ask for one.

FAQs

Is healthcare free for foreigners in Singapore?

No. Foreigners pay non-subsidised rates and don't get MediShield Life. Most rely on employer insurance and private health insurance.

How much does a doctor visit cost in Singapore?

A GP consultation typically costs about S$50–125 for foreigners, plus medicine. A&E visits at public hospitals often start around S$130–200.

Does employer insurance cover my family in Singapore?

Not always. Many group policies cover only the employee unless family is added. Check with HR and buy a family plan if needed.
