Singapore First-Timer Guide

Must-see sights, neighbourhoods, hawker food, getting around and money tips: everything we tell friends visiting Singapore for the first time.

5 min readUpdated October 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: Basile Morin, CC BY-SA 4.0

Singapore is an ultra-modern city-state that feels like the future of Southeast Asia: spotless, safe, efficient and full of incredible food. It's pricier than its neighbours, but most of the best things to do are cheap or free, and almost everything is a short MRT ride away.

Three to five days is perfect for a first visit. A classic route: Marina Bay → Gardens by the Bay → Chinatown → Little India → Kampong Glam → Sentosa.

Must-see attractions

Marina Bay Icon · free to S$$

The skyline you've seen in photos. Walk the waterfront promenade past the Merlion, the Esplanade and Marina Bay Sands, then watch the free Spectra light and water show in the evening. The SkyPark Observation Deck on top of Marina Bay Sands costs about S$32–40.

Free light showBest at night

Gardens by the Bay Nature · free to S$

The outdoor gardens and Supertree Grove are free. The two cooled conservatories, the Flower Dome and Cloud Forest, cost about S$45–55 together. Stay for the free Garden Rhapsody light show at the Supertrees in the evening (usually 7:45 PM and 8:45 PM).

Free Supertree showAir-conditioned domes

Sentosa Island Beaches & theme parks · S$–S$$$

Singapore's playground. Take the Sentosa Express monorail from VivoCity (about S$4) or walk across the Sentosa Boardwalk from HarbourFront MRT in about 15 minutes. Relax on Siloso, Palawan or Tanjong beach, or spend the day at Universal Studios Singapore (about S$85–90). Our Universal Studios and Sentosa guide has the details.

Full dayWalk or monorail

Chinatown Heritage · mostly free

Temples, shophouses, markets and some of the city's best food. Visit the Buddha Tooth Relic Temple and Thian Hock Keng Temple, then eat at Maxwell Food Centre, home of the famous Tian Tian chicken rice stall.

Free templesHawker food

Kampong Glam Culture · mostly free

Singapore's historic Malay-Muslim quarter around the golden-domed Sultan Mosque (free to visit outside prayer times; dress modestly). Wander Haji Lane's murals and boutiques and try murtabak or biryani on Arab Street.

Free mosque visitStreet art

Neighbourhoods to explore

  • Marina Bay: glamorous and expensive, so come in the evening for the lights. Satay by the Bay and Lau Pa Sat nearby are far cheaper than the hotel restaurants.
  • Chinatown: red lanterns, temples and food. Allow 3–4 hours; Chinatown Food Street and Maxwell Food Centre are good for dinner.
  • Little India: temples, flower stalls and Indian food on Serangoon Road. Visit Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple and have a dosa or thali at Komala Vilas. See our Indian restaurants guide.
  • Kampong Glam: Haji Lane cafés, independent shops and the Sultan Mosque.
  • Katong and Joo Chiat: colourful Peranakan shophouses and the original Katong laksa.

Hawker food: the real Singapore meal

Hawker centres are open-air food courts where each stall specialises in one or two dishes. They're how Singaporeans eat, and a meal usually costs about S$5–8. Must-tries:

  • Hainanese chicken rice: poached chicken with fragrant rice cooked in chicken stock. Try Maxwell Food Centre.
  • Laksa: spicy coconut curry noodle soup; Katong laksa comes with short noodles you eat with a spoon.
  • Satay: grilled skewers with peanut sauce, about S$1 a stick. Lau Pa Sat's satay street comes alive in the evening.
  • Chilli crab: a restaurant splurge (often S$80+ per crab, priced by weight) that's worth it once.
  • Fried carrot cake: radish cake fried with egg, "white" (savoury) or "black" (sweet dark soy).
  • Kaya toast: toast with coconut jam and butter, plus soft-boiled eggs and kopi for breakfast.

Getting around

  • MRT and buses: fast, clean and cheap; most rides cost about S$1–2.50. Tap in and out with a contactless Visa or Mastercard (check your card's foreign transaction fee), or buy a Singapore Tourist Pass for unlimited rides.
  • Grab: the main ride-hailing app, handy late at night after the MRT stops (around midnight) or with luggage.
  • Walking: central areas are walkable. Marina Bay to Chinatown is about 20 minutes on foot, but carry water: it's hot and humid.
  • From Changi Airport: the MRT costs about S$2.50 and takes around 45 minutes to the city; a Grab is about S$25–35.

Best time to visit

Singapore is warm all year (about 25–32°C). February to April is usually the driest and most comfortable stretch. November to January is the wettest, with the northeast monsoon bringing heavy afternoon showers, though rarely all-day rain. Carry a small umbrella whenever you go.

Money tips

  • Budget roughly S$80–150 per person a day for a budget stay, food and transport; mid-range trips cost more.
  • Cards work almost everywhere, but keep a little cash for hawker stalls.
  • Tipping isn't expected; restaurants usually add a service charge.
  • Many top sights are free: Supertree Grove, both light shows, Merlion Park, the Botanic Gardens, Jewel's Rain Vortex and the heritage neighbourhoods.

Visa and arrival for Indian travellers

Indian passport holders need a visa for Singapore, applied for through an authorised visa agent (usually around ₹2,500–3,500 all-in, a few working days). Every visitor must also submit the free SG Arrival Card within 3 days before arrival on the official ICA website or app. Our Singapore visa guide walks through it step by step.

SIM cards and staying connected

Tourist SIMs from Singtel, StarHub and M1 are sold at Changi Airport and convenience stores, typically S$12–30 for several days of data. A travel eSIM set up before you fly works well too, and free Wi-Fi is common in malls and MRT stations.

Our tipAfter a busy day, eat late (around 9–10 PM) when hawker queues are shorter, then take a night walk through Chinatown or Kampong Glam. The city is very safe after dark.

FAQs

How many days do you need in Singapore for a first trip?

Three to five days covers Marina Bay, Gardens by the Bay, Chinatown, Little India, Kampong Glam and a day on Sentosa at a comfortable pace.

Do Indians need a visa for Singapore?

Yes. Indian passport holders need a Singapore visa, applied for through an authorised visa agent, plus the free SG Arrival Card within 3 days before arrival. Check the ICA website for the latest rules.

When is the best time to visit Singapore?

February to April is usually the driest and most comfortable period. November to January is the wettest, with heavy afternoon showers during the northeast monsoon.

Is Singapore expensive for first-time visitors?

Hotels and big attractions are pricey, but hawker meals cost about S$5 to S$8, the MRT is cheap and many top sights, including the Supertree light show and Marina Bay, are free.
