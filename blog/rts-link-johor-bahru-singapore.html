JB–Singapore RTS Link: Opening Date, Fares and What to Know

The 5-minute cross-border train between Woodlands and Johor Bahru is almost ready. Here's when it opens, how it works and what Indian travellers need.

News · Transport6 min readUpdated 26 November 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: ZKang123, CC BY-SA 4.0

The Johor Bahru–Singapore RTS Link, a short cross-border train between Woodlands and JB, is close to opening. It's set to change how people cross one of the world's busiest borders. Here's what we know so far, and what it means if you're an Indian living in or visiting Singapore.

Updated 26 November 2026Based on announcements from Singapore's Land Transport Authority (LTA) and news reports. Fares and the exact opening date haven't been announced yet; we'll update this page when they are.

Key facts

RouteWoodlands North (Singapore) ↔ Bukit Chagar (Johor Bahru), about 4 km
Journey timeAbout 5 minutes
OpeningPassenger service targeted for February 2027, after trial runs due to finish by 31 December 2026 (some reports say January)
CapacityUp to 10,000 passengers per hour in each direction
ImmigrationClear both Singapore and Malaysia immigration at your departure station
FaresNot announced yet. Malaysia's transport minister has estimated S$5–7 one way; one report suggested RM15.50–21.70
Singapore connectionWoodlands North station on the Thomson-East Coast Line (TEL), linked by an underground concourse

How it will work

Unlike the Causeway buses, where you stop at two checkpoints, the RTS Link has co-located immigration: you clear both Singapore's departure and Malaysia's arrival checks at the station you leave from, then step off the train straight into the other country. That's what makes the 5-minute journey possible, and it should end the long queues of the morning and evening rush.

  • From Singapore: take the TEL to Woodlands North, walk to the RTS station, clear immigration, board the train, and arrive at Bukit Chagar in central JB.
  • From JB: Bukit Chagar is close to JB Sentral and the city centre.

What it means for Indians in Singapore

  • You'll still need a Malaysia visa. From 1 January 2027, Indian passport holders need a visa approved before entering Malaysia, so a quick RTS hop to JB still needs one. See our Malaysia visa guide for 2027.
  • The MDAC still applies: fill in the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card before each entry.
  • Coming back to Singapore: work pass and Dependant's Pass holders re-enter on their pass. Tourists need a Singapore visa valid for another entry, so check yours allows multiple entries.
  • Cheaper weekend trips: JB's malls, food and Legoland Malaysia get much easier to reach, especially if fares land in the expected range.

What happens to the buses and KTM shuttle?

Causeway buses and taxis will keep running. The KTM Shuttle Tebrau train between Woodlands and JB Sentral is expected to be phased out after the RTS Link starts, under the agreement between the two countries. Until then, our Singapore to Malaysia by bus or train guide covers today's options.

FAQs

When will the JB–Singapore RTS Link open?

Passenger service is targeted for February 2027, after trial runs due to finish by 31 December 2026. Some reports mention January; check LTA for the confirmed date.

How much will the RTS Link fare be?

Fares haven't been announced. Malaysia's transport minister has estimated S$5–7 one way, and one report suggested RM15.50–21.70.

Do I clear immigration twice on the RTS Link?

No. Immigration for both countries is done at your departure station, so you walk straight out when you arrive.

Do Indians need a visa to take the RTS Link to Johor Bahru?

Yes, from 1 January 2027 Indian passport holders need a Malaysia visa approved before entry, plus the MDAC arrival card.
