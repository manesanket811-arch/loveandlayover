Bali vs Thailand: Which Should Indians Pick?

Two of the most popular beach trips from India, compared honestly: visa, cost, food, flights and when each one is at its best.

8 min readUpdated November 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: Vyacheslav Argenberg, CC BY 4.0

Bali and Thailand are the two trips our Indian friends ask us about most. Both are warm, beautiful and good value, but they suit different travellers. Here's how they compare, and which one we'd pick for different kinds of trips.

Quick answer

  • Pick Thailand if you want the easiest visa, more direct flights from India, a mix of city, nightlife and beaches, and lots of vegetarian and Indian food.
  • Pick Bali if you want a slower, scenic holiday: rice terraces, temples, villas with private pools, cafes and sunsets. It's a favourite for honeymoons.
  • Timing decides a lot: Thailand's best months (November to February) are Bali's rainy season, and Bali's dry season (April to October) overlaps Thailand's hot and rainy months. So you can do one in winter and the other in summer.

Visa and entry

Thailand is currently visa-free for Indian tourists, though the permitted length of stay has changed several times, so check the current limit with the Royal Thai Embassy before you fly. Everyone must fill in the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card online before arrival.

Bali (Indonesia) gives Indians a visa on arrival, which you can also buy in advance online as an e-VoA on the official Indonesian immigration website. Bali also charges a foreign tourist levy, paid online or on arrival. Your passport needs at least 6 months' validity for both.

Our tipBuy Bali's e-VoA online before you fly. The arrival queue at Denpasar can be long, and having it sorted makes the first hour of your holiday much nicer.

Cost

Day to day, the two are close. Thailand's street food and Bangkok's public transport make it slightly cheaper for budget travellers, while Bali gives you more for your money at the top end: a villa with a private pool in Ubud or Seminyak can cost what a standard hotel room costs in Phuket in peak season.

The bigger difference is usually the flight. Thailand has more direct routes and more competition from India, so fares are often lower and the journey shorter. Bali fares vary a lot depending on your city, so compare both before you decide.

Food for Indian travellers

Thailand is easier. Bangkok, Phuket and Pattaya have plenty of Indian restaurants, and many Thai dishes can be made vegetarian (ask for jay food, which skips meat, fish sauce and eggs). See our Thailand food guide.

Bali is very good for vegetarians too, with lots of cafes, smoothie bowls and Indonesian dishes like gado-gado and tempeh. Indian restaurants exist in Kuta, Seminyak and Ubud, but there are fewer of them.

Beaches and things to do

Bali

  • Rice terraces and jungle around Ubud
  • Cliff-top temples like Uluwatu and Tanah Lot at sunset
  • A day trip to Nusa Penida for its famous viewpoints
  • A sunrise trek up Mount Batur

Thailand

  • Bangkok's temples, markets, malls and rooftop bars
  • Island hopping from Phuket or Krabi: Phi Phi, James Bond Island, Railay
  • Chiang Mai's old city, night markets and elephant sanctuaries
  • Clearer, calmer beach water in season than Bali's surf beaches

Which one for which trip

Honeymoon Bali, just

Private-pool villas, sunsets and a slower pace make Bali hard to beat. Thailand's Krabi and Koh Samui are great alternatives in December and January.

First trip abroad Thailand

Visa-free entry, short direct flights, easy food and good transport make Thailand the simpler first international trip.

Family with kids or parents Thailand

Shorter flights, easy Indian food and attractions like Safari World in Bangkok. Bali works well too if you book a villa and a private driver.

Friends' trip Thailand

Bangkok nights, Phuket's beaches and island hopping with a group are hard to beat.

FAQs

Is Bali or Thailand cheaper for Indians?

Day to day they are close: about ₹3,200–5,300 per person a day in Bali and ₹3,800–6,300 in Thailand, including a shared mid-range hotel. Flights to Thailand are often cheaper and shorter because there are more direct routes from India.

Do Indians need a visa for Bali or Thailand?

Thailand is currently visa-free for Indian tourists, with a free online arrival card. Bali gives Indians a visa on arrival (about IDR 500,000) plus a tourist levy of IDR 150,000. Rules change, so check official websites before you fly.

Which is better for a honeymoon, Bali or Thailand?

Bali is the classic honeymoon pick for its private-pool villas, scenery and slower pace. Thailand's Krabi, Phuket and Koh Samui are great alternatives, especially from November to February.

When is the best time to visit Bali and Thailand?

Bali is best from April to October, during the dry season. Thailand is best from November to February, when it's cooler and dry.
