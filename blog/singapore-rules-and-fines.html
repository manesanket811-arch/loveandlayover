Singapore Rules and Fines You Should Know

The strict rules that keep Singapore clean and safe, and the fines that catch newcomers out.

7 min readUpdated November 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: Marufish from Alor Setar, Malaysia, CC BY-SA 2.0

What surprised us most about moving to Singapore was how well planned everything is, and how strict the rules are about everything. The good news: the rules are clear, signs are everywhere, and they're a big reason the city is so clean and safe. Here's what to know, whether you're visiting or moving here.

As of November 2026Rules change, and this is a general guide from our experience, not legal, tax or immigration advice. Always confirm with official sources such as the Singapore Police Force, LTA and NEA for your own case.

On the MRT and buses

  • No eating or drinking, not even water, on trains, buses or inside stations. Fines can be up to S$500.
  • No durians on public transport, because of the smell.
  • No smoking or vaping anywhere on public transport.
  • Give up reserved seats for elderly, pregnant and less mobile passengers. It isn't a fine, but people notice.
  • Stand on the left on escalators and let people walk past on the right.

Vaping is illegal

Buying, owning or using vapes and e-cigarettes is illegal in Singapore, and the penalties were raised sharply from 1 May 2026, with fines for users of up to S$10,000. Don't bring a vape into Singapore, even for personal use; it can be confiscated at the border and you can be fined.

Smoking

Smoking is only allowed in designated smoking areas, marked with yellow boxes or signs. It's banned in most public places, including bus stops, covered walkways, parks, beaches and outside many buildings, and fines can run to hundreds of dollars. Cigarettes brought into Singapore must be declared and taxed.

Littering and cleanliness

  • Littering, even a tissue or cigarette butt, can bring a fine, and repeat offenders can be ordered to do community cleaning work.
  • Return your tray after eating at hawker centres and food courts. It's required, and you can be fined for not doing it.
  • Spitting in public is an offence.
  • Feeding pigeons and other wild animals is prohibited.

On the road

  • Jaywalking: cross at pedestrian crossings or overhead bridges if one is nearby; crossing elsewhere can be fined.
  • Traffic drives on the left, like in India, but rules are strictly enforced with cameras.
  • Cycling and e-scooters have their own rules on where you can ride. Check before you buy or rent one.

Other rules that surprise newcomers

  • Chewing gum isn't sold in shops, and you can't bring it in to sell. Small amounts for personal use are generally tolerated, but leave it at home to be safe.
  • Drinking alcohol in public is banned from 10.30pm to 7am, and Little India and Geylang have stricter weekend zones.
  • Drugs: penalties are extremely severe, including long jail terms and, for trafficking, the death penalty.
  • Medicines: some common medicines need approval to bring in. Carry prescriptions and keep to personal amounts.
  • Pirated goods and illegal downloads can get you into trouble.

Unwritten rules and etiquette

  • Queue properly: everyone does, for buses, food stalls and lifts.
  • "Chope" a seat at a hawker centre with a tissue packet. Don't take a seat that's been choped.
  • Keep it down: loud phone calls on the MRT get looks.
  • Be on time: meetings and appointments start on time.

It can feel like a lot at first, but within a few weeks it becomes second nature, and you'll appreciate how calm and clean everything is. For more on settling in, see our first 30 days checklist.

FAQs

Can you eat on the MRT in Singapore?

No. Eating and drinking, including water, aren't allowed on trains, buses or inside MRT stations. Fines can be up to S$500.

Is vaping illegal in Singapore?

Yes. Buying, owning or using vapes is illegal, and since 1 May 2026 users can be fined up to S$10,000. Don't bring a vape into Singapore.

Is chewing gum banned in Singapore?

Chewing gum isn't sold in Singapore and can't be brought in to sell. Small amounts for personal use are generally tolerated, but it's safest not to bring any.

Do you have to return trays at hawker centres?

Yes. Returning your tray and cleaning up after eating at hawker centres and food courts is required, and you can be fined for not doing it.
