How to Get a Job in Singapore from India

Work passes, the 2027 salary changes, COMPASS, how to job hunt from India and how much you need to earn, from a couple who did it.

16 min readUpdated November 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: Basile Morin, CC BY-SA 4.0

"How did you get a job in Singapore?" is the question we get most in our comments and DMs. Sanket moved here from India for a cloud engineering role, with no referral and no agent, so we've been through it ourselves. This guide covers everything we wish someone had told us: which work pass you need, the salary rules (including the changes coming in January 2027), how employers decide, how to job hunt from India, and how much you really need to earn to live well here.

Rules as of November 2026Work pass rules in Singapore change often. Everything below is based on the Ministry of Manpower (MOM) rules and announcements as of November 2026. Always confirm your own case on mom.gov.sg or with MOM's Self-Assessment Tool before you make decisions. This is a guide from our experience, not legal or immigration advice.

The short answer

  • You need a job offer first. Singapore has no job-seeker visa for most people. Your employer applies for your work pass, not you.
  • Most Indian professionals get an Employment Pass (EP). The minimum salary is S$5,600 a month today and rises to S$6,000 from 1 January 2027. It's higher if you're older or work in financial services.
  • Salary alone isn't enough. Your application also has to score 40 points under COMPASS, which looks at your salary, qualifications and the employer's workforce.
  • The job market is competitive. Tech, finance, healthcare and engineering hire the most foreigners. Specialised skills and certifications make a big difference.
  • To bring your family, you need a fixed salary of at least S$6,000 a month for a Dependant's Pass, and about S$12,000 to bring your parents.

Singapore work passes explained

Singapore has a pass for each level of job. Here are the ones that matter for most Indians:

Employment Pass salary rules for 2026 and 2027

The EP minimum qualifying salary is the lowest fixed monthly salary your employer can offer for your application to be considered. It depends on your age, because MOM expects experienced candidates to earn more.

Now (applications from 1 Jan 2025)From 1 Jan 2027
Most sectors, youngest applicantsS$5,600S$6,000
Most sectors, age 45 and aboveS$10,700S$11,500
Financial services, youngest applicantsS$6,200S$6,600
Financial services, age 45 and aboveS$11,800S$12,700

Between those ages the amount rises gradually each year. The new 2027 figures apply to new applications from 1 January 2027 and to renewals of passes expiring from 1 January 2028.

If you're applying nowIf you already have an offer, ask your employer to submit the EP application before 31 December 2026, while the current salary table applies. If your offer is close to S$5,600, it may not qualify after January.

The S Pass minimum is also rising, from S$3,300 to S$3,600 (S$3,800 to S$4,000 in financial services) for new applications from 1 January 2027 and renewals from 1 January 2028. We explain all the 2027 changes in Marathi in our video Singapore Work Visa Changes 2027.

COMPASS: the points test for the EP

Since September 2023, EP applications also go through COMPASS (Complementarity Assessment Framework). You need 40 points. Two criteria are about you and two are about the company hiring you, with two bonus criteria:

CriterionWhat it measuresPoints
C1 SalaryYour fixed salary compared with local professionals in the same sector and age group20 if at or above the 90th percentile, 10 if at or above the 65th, 0 below
C2 QualificationsYour highest qualification20 for a top-tier institution, 10 for a degree or equivalent, 0 otherwise
C3 DiversityHow many of the company's professionals share your nationalityHigher when your nationality is a smaller share
C4 Support for local employmentHow many locals the company employs compared with similar firmsHigher for firms that hire more locals
C5 Skills bonusWhether your job is on MOM's Shortage Occupation ListBonus points (up to 20)
C6 Strategic economic prioritiesWhether the company is part of a government-backed growth or innovation programmeBonus points

A few things this means in practice:

  • Salary matters twice: once for the minimum, and again for COMPASS points. An offer that only just meets the minimum often scores 0 on C1.
  • Some of it is out of your hands. C3 and C4 depend on the employer. Indians are a large share of professionals at many tech firms, which can lower C3. Smaller firms with fewer than 25 professionals get default points on C3 and C4.
  • Shortage roles get a boost. The Shortage Occupation List mainly covers certain tech, healthcare, green economy and advanced manufacturing roles. Check whether your role is on it.
  • Exemptions: candidates earning at least S$22,500 a month fixed, intra-company transfers and short-term roles of up to one month don't need COMPASS.

MOM has a free Self-Assessment Tool where you can check an offer before your employer applies.

How the hiring process works from India

Here's the typical journey, based on how it went for us:

  1. Apply and interview, usually 2 to 4 online rounds with technical tests or case studies.
  2. Get the offer and check that the fixed monthly salary meets the EP minimum for your age.
  3. The employer advertises the role on MyCareersFuture for at least 14 days, under the Fair Consideration Framework, unless the role is exempt (for example, small firms with fewer than 10 employees or very high salaries). This can delay your application, so ask your recruiter about it.
  4. The employer applies for your EP online. Most applications are decided within about 10 working days, though some take longer.
  5. In-Principle Approval (IPA): you get an IPA letter, which lets you enter Singapore to start work.
  6. Arrive, do the medical check-up if required, and the employer requests your pass card. You may need to register your fingerprints and photo at MOM.
  7. Collect your pass card, then set up Singpass, a bank account and a mobile number.

How to find a job in Singapore from India

Our story 3–4 months, all on LinkedIn

Sanket's job hunt took about 3 to 4 months. He applied only through LinkedIn, with no referral and no agent. He went after one clear role (cloud and DevOps), and his AWS, Terraform and Kubernetes certifications backed up his profile. It shows that a focused profile on one portal can be enough, as long as you keep going.

3–4 monthsLinkedIn onlyNo referralNo agent

Where to look

  • LinkedIn: the biggest source of professional roles, and the one that worked for us. Set your job location to Singapore, turn on "Open to work" for recruiters, use job alerts so you apply in the first few days, and message the recruiter or hiring manager after applying.
  • MyCareersFuture: the government job portal. Every EP role that needs advertising appears here, so it's a good signal of which companies are hiring foreigners.
  • JobStreet, Indeed and Glassdoor: useful for volume and for salary research.
  • Specialist sites: eFinancialCareers for finance and NodeFlair for tech salaries and roles.
  • Company career pages: many MNCs, banks and tech firms have Singapore hubs and post roles on their own sites first.
  • Recruitment agencies: firms like Michael Page, Robert Walters, Hays and Randstad place many foreign professionals. A licensed agency should never ask you to pay for a "guaranteed job".

Make your profile Singapore-ready

  • Keep your CV to 2 pages and lead with measurable results. Skip the photo, date of birth and family details that are common on Indian CVs.
  • State your notice period clearly. Indian notice periods of 60 to 90 days put many Singapore employers off. If you can negotiate a buyout or early release, say so.
  • Show global certifications. They're easy for Singapore recruiters to trust. For Sanket, AWS, Terraform and Kubernetes (CKA) certifications were what got interviews moving.
  • Apply to roles that match your experience closely. Employers have to justify hiring a foreigner, so a strong, specific match beats a broad CV.
  • Be ready for timezone-friendly interviews: Singapore is 2.5 hours ahead of India.

Sanket went from a call centre job to a cloud engineering role in Singapore. He shares the certifications, self-study plan and interview prep that worked in From Call Center to Singapore Tech Job.

Industries that hire foreigners

  • Technology: cloud, DevOps, cybersecurity, data and AI, and software engineering. Singapore is a regional hub for many global tech companies.
  • Banking and finance: technology, risk, compliance and operations roles at global banks.
  • Healthcare: nurses, doctors and allied health professionals, with registration requirements.
  • Engineering, semiconductors and advanced manufacturing
  • Consulting, logistics and supply chain

Your take-home pay

Foreigners don't pay into CPF, Singapore's pension scheme, and income tax is low. Once you're a tax resident (usually after 183 days in Singapore in a year), your approximate tax before reliefs looks like this:

Monthly salaryAnnual salaryApprox. income tax a yearApprox. take-home a month
S$6,000S$72,000S$2,800about S$5,770
S$8,000S$96,000S$5,200about S$7,570
S$10,000S$120,000S$7,950about S$9,340

Tax is paid once a year rather than deducted monthly, so set money aside. In your first or last year you may be taxed as a non-resident if you're here for fewer than 183 days. Check IRAS for your case.

Compare offers properlyAt about ₹70 per S$1, S$6,000 sounds like ₹4.2 lakh a month. But rent alone can take S$2,000–4,000. Compare offers on what's left after rent, and ask about housing allowance, health insurance for your family, annual bonus and relocation support.

Bringing your family

  • Spouse and children: a Dependant's Pass needs a fixed monthly salary of at least S$6,000. Your employer applies for it, usually with your EP or later.
  • Your spouse working: DP holders can't work on the DP itself. They need an employer to apply for their own EP, S Pass or Work Permit, which means meeting the same criteria as everyone else. See below for why this is harder than it sounds.
  • Parents: EP holders earning at least about S$12,000 fixed a month can apply for a Long-Term Visit Pass for their parents. Otherwise, parents usually visit on a regular tourist visa; see our Singapore visa guide for Indians.

The Dependant's Pass reality: why spouses find it hard to get a job

This is the part nobody tells you before you move. Many spouses assume that once they're in Singapore on a DP, finding a job will be easy. In our experience and that of most friends here, it's difficult, and the reason is visa sponsorship:

  • Being in Singapore doesn't change the rules. A DP holder is treated like any foreign candidate. The employer still has to apply for an EP or S Pass, meet the minimum salary and pass COMPASS or the S Pass quota.
  • Many employers prefer candidates who don't need sponsorship. Job adverts often say "Singaporeans and PRs only" or "must not require sponsorship", and recruiters screen for this early.
  • S Pass roles are limited. Companies pay a monthly levy and can only hire a set share of S Pass holders, so mid-level roles are harder to get.
  • Career breaks add up. The longer the gap, the harder it gets, so start early.

What helps:

  • Start the job hunt before you move, or as soon as the DP is approved, and treat it like a full job search from India.
  • Target employers that sponsor regularly: MNCs, banks, tech companies and hospitals hire foreigners every year. MyCareersFuture listings are a good sign that a company considers EP candidates.
  • Aim for roles that clear the EP bar, ideally in fields on the Shortage Occupation List, and add certifications to stand out.
  • Use your network in Singapore: community groups, alumni and LinkedIn connections here often hear about openings first.
  • Check before taking any work. Freelancing, part-time work or working remotely while on a DP can still need a work pass. Check MOM's rules first.
Plan as a coupleIf you both want to work, budget for living on one salary for the first few months, and don't assume the second job will come quickly. We talk about the DP reality in our job video.

Recent and upcoming changes

  • 1 September 2025: S Pass minimum rose to S$3,300 (S$3,800 in financial services), and the S Pass levy became S$650 a month.
  • 1 January 2025: EP minimum rose to S$5,600 (S$6,200 in financial services).
  • 1 January 2027: EP minimum rises to S$6,000 (S$6,600 in financial services) and S Pass to S$3,600 (S$4,000) for new applications. COMPASS salary benchmarks are also updated.
  • From January 2027: a new ONE Pass (AI and Tech) track for senior tech talent replaces the Tech.Pass.
  • 1 January 2028: the 2027 salary figures apply to renewals of passes expiring from this date.

Avoid job scams

  • Real employers don't charge you for a job offer or an EP. The employer pays the EP fees.
  • Check that any agency is licensed in MOM's Employment Agency Directory.
  • You can check whether an EP or IPA is genuine using MOM's online pass check.
  • Be wary of "guaranteed EP" or "visa first, job later" promises on WhatsApp or Telegram.

Our tips if you're starting now

  1. Pick one clear target role and build a CV and LinkedIn profile around it.
  2. Add one or two respected certifications in your field.
  3. Check your expected salary against the EP minimum for your age, and against 2027's higher table.
  4. Plan for 3 to 6 months of steady applying. It took Sanket 3 to 4 months, and many applications won't get a reply.
  5. Ask about notice period buyout, relocation, and when the employer will file your EP.
  6. Budget on take-home pay after rent before you accept.

FAQs

What is the minimum salary for an Employment Pass in Singapore?

As of November 2026, the EP minimum qualifying salary is S$5,600 a month (S$6,200 in financial services), rising with age to S$10,700 (S$11,800) at 45 and above. From 1 January 2027 it rises to S$6,000 (S$6,600 in financial services) for new applications. You must also pass the COMPASS points test.

Can I go to Singapore to look for a job?

Most Indians can't get a job-seeker visa for Singapore. You need a job offer first, and your employer applies for your work pass. Many people job hunt online from India and interview remotely.

How long does it take to find a job in Singapore from India?

It varies by field, but plan for 3 to 6 months of steady applying. Sanket's job hunt took about 3 to 4 months, applying only through LinkedIn with no referral or agent.

What is COMPASS in Singapore?

COMPASS is the points-based framework for Employment Pass applications. You need 40 points, based on your salary compared with locals, your qualifications, the employer's nationality mix and local hiring, plus bonus points for shortage roles and strategic projects. Candidates earning at least S$22,500 a month are exempt.

How much salary is good to live in Singapore?

From our experience, around S$5,000 a month is comfortable for a single person sharing a flat, S$8,000 or more for a couple on one income renting a whole flat, and S$12,000 or more for a family paying international school fees.

Can my wife or husband work in Singapore on a Dependant's Pass?

Not on the Dependant's Pass itself. A spouse who wants to work needs an employer to apply for their own work pass, such as an EP or S Pass, and must meet its criteria.

Is it hard to get a job in Singapore on a Dependant's Pass?

Often, yes. A Dependant's Pass holder still needs the employer to sponsor an EP or S Pass and meet the same salary and COMPASS rules, and many employers prefer candidates who don't need sponsorship. Start early and target employers that regularly hire foreigners.

How long does it take to get an Employment Pass?

Most online EP applications are decided within about 10 working days, but it can take longer if MOM needs more information. Employers may also need to advertise the job for 14 days on MyCareersFuture first.

What changes to Singapore work passes are coming in 2027?

From 1 January 2027, the EP minimum salary rises to S$6,000 and the S Pass minimum to S$3,600 for new applications, with higher figures for financial services. A new ONE Pass (AI and Tech) track also opens for senior tech talent.
