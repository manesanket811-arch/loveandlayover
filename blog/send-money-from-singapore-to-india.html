How to Send Money from Singapore to India

Wise, banks and remittance apps compared, which Indian account to send to, and how to get more rupees for every dollar.

7 min readUpdated November 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: Marcin Konsek, CC BY-SA 4.0

Almost every Indian in Singapore sends money home, whether it's savings, EMIs or support for family. The difference between a good and a bad transfer can be thousands of rupees a year, so here's how the options compare and what we use.

As of November 2026Rules change, and this is a general guide from our experience, not legal, tax or immigration advice. Always confirm with your bank, your remittance provider and a tax adviser for your own case.

What we use Wise app

We send money to India with the Wise app. What we like is that it shows the exchange rate and the fee upfront, and the exact rupee amount that will arrive, so it's easy to compare with what a bank offers. How to compare: look at the rupees that arrive

A "zero fee" transfer isn't free if the exchange rate is worse. The cost of a transfer has two parts:

  • The fee: a fixed amount, a percentage, or both.
  • The exchange rate markup: the gap between the rate you get and the real mid-market rate you see on Google.

The simple way to compare: enter the same Singapore dollar amount in each app or bank and see how many rupees will arrive. The highest number wins. Check it each time, because rates and promotions change.

Quick mathsAt about ₹70 per S$1, sending S$2,000 should land around ₹1.4 lakh. If a provider's quote is ₹2,000–3,000 lower, that's what the "free" transfer really costs.

Which Indian account should you send to?

Once you live abroad, you'll usually be an NRI for Indian rules, and your bank accounts in India should reflect that:

  • NRE account: for money you earn abroad. The money is fully repatriable (you can move it back out), and the interest is tax-free in India. Most people send their savings here.
  • NRO account: for income earned in India, such as rent, dividends or interest. Your old savings account is usually converted into an NRO account.

Many remittance services pay into both. More on this in our NRI guide for Indians in Singapore.

Tips to get more rupees

  • Compare the final amount every time, not the advertised fee.
  • Send fewer, larger transfers if your provider charges a fixed fee per transfer.
  • Use rate alerts: many apps can tell you when the rate hits a level you want.
  • Double-check the recipient details (name, account number, IFSC). Mistakes are slow and sometimes costly to fix.
  • Keep records: save transfer receipts in case your bank or tax filing asks for proof of the source of funds.

Is the money taxed?

Money you earned in Singapore and send to India isn't income in India, so it isn't taxed again just because you transfer it. Interest on an NRE account is tax-free in India, while interest on an NRO account is taxable. Gifts to family are a separate topic; ask a tax adviser for large amounts.

FAQs

What is the cheapest way to send money from Singapore to India?

It changes, so compare the final rupee amount each time. Wise, bank remittances and apps like Instarem and Remitly are all common. We use Wise because it shows the rate and fee upfront.

Is there tax on money sent from Singapore to India?

Your Singapore salary isn't taxed again in India just because you send it home. Interest on an NRE account is tax-free in India; interest on an NRO account is taxable.

Should I send money to an NRE or NRO account?

Most people send savings from their foreign salary to an NRE account, which is repatriable and has tax-free interest. Income earned in India goes into an NRO account.

How long does a transfer from Singapore to India take?

Many app-based transfers arrive the same day or within one working day. Bank transfers can take longer, especially over weekends and holidays.
