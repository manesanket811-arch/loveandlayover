NRI Guide for Indians in Singapore

What changes in India when you move: NRI status, taxes, NRE and NRO accounts, ITR filing and your first-year checklist.

9 min readUpdated November 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: Frank Schulenburg, CC BY-SA 4.0

When you move to Singapore for work, your status in India changes too. Most people become NRIs (non-resident Indians), which affects your taxes, bank accounts and investments back home. Here's what changes, in plain words, and what to sort out in your first year.

As of November 2026Rules change, and this is a general guide from our experience, not legal, tax or immigration advice. Always confirm with a chartered accountant, your bank and the Income Tax Department for your own case.

Are you an NRI?

For Indian income tax, your status depends on how many days you spend in India in a financial year (April to March):

  • Resident: generally if you're in India for 182 days or more in the year.
  • NRI: if you're in India for less than 182 days. For Indian citizens who leave India for a job abroad, the 182-day test is the main one.
  • Exception for high Indian income: if your income from India (excluding foreign income) is above ₹15 lakh a year, staying 120 days or more can change your status, sometimes to "resident but not ordinarily resident" (RNOR).

In practice, if you move to Singapore early in the financial year and visit India for a few weeks a year, you'll usually be an NRI from that year on.

India and Singapore have a tax treaty (DTAA), which helps you avoid being taxed twice on the same income. For your Singapore side, see what you'll pay in our salary guide.

Do you still need to file an ITR in India?

You need to file if your taxable income in India (interest, rent, capital gains and so on) is above the basic exemption limit. Many NRIs also file to claim a refund, because banks often deduct more TDS on NRO interest than you actually owe. When filing, declare your status as non-resident. A Tax Residency Certificate from IRAS can help you claim treaty benefits.

Your bank accounts

Under Indian foreign exchange rules (FEMA), once you become an NRI you shouldn't keep using regular resident savings accounts:

  • Convert your savings account to an NRO account. Your bank will do this when you inform them. Indian income (rent, dividends, interest) goes here.
  • Open an NRE account for money you earn abroad. It's repatriable, and the interest is tax-free in India.
  • FCNR deposits let you hold fixed deposits in foreign currency, if you want to avoid rupee movements.
  • Keep your Indian mobile number active for OTPs, or update it to an international number where your bank allows.

Investments and other things to update

  • Mutual funds and shares: update your status to NRI with your fund houses and broker. For shares you'll typically need NRI demat and trading accounts linked to NRE or NRO accounts.
  • PPF: an existing PPF account can usually continue until maturity, but NRIs can't open a new one.
  • Insurance and loans: inform your insurers and lenders of your new address and status.
  • KYC: update your address, contact details and residential status with your bank, broker and fund houses. Keep PAN and Aadhaar linked where required.

Your first-year checklist

  1. Tell your bank you've moved and convert savings accounts to NRO.
  2. Open an NRE account for your foreign earnings.
  3. Update KYC and status with your broker, mutual funds and insurers.
  4. Keep track of your days in India each financial year.
  5. File your ITR in India if you have taxable Indian income or TDS to claim back.
  6. Keep salary slips and transfer receipts as proof of income abroad.

FAQs

When do I become an NRI after moving to Singapore?

For income tax, generally when you spend less than 182 days in India in a financial year (April to March). Indian citizens leaving for a job abroad are mainly tested on the 182-day rule; a 120-day rule applies if your Indian income is above ₹15 lakh.

Is my Singapore salary taxed in India?

Not if you're an NRI. NRIs are taxed in India only on income earned or received in India, such as interest, rent or capital gains.

What is the difference between NRE and NRO accounts?

An NRE account holds money earned abroad; it's repatriable and the interest is tax-free in India. An NRO account holds income earned in India, such as rent or interest; that interest is taxable.

Do NRIs need to file income tax returns in India?

Yes, if your taxable Indian income is above the basic exemption limit, or if you want to claim a refund of excess TDS deducted by your bank.
