Singapore with Parents or Kids

A relaxed family plan: air-conditioned attractions, easy transport, vegetarian food and pacing tips that keep both grandparents and kids happy.

4 min readUpdated October 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: Jakub Hałun, CC BY 4.0

Singapore might be the easiest family destination in Asia. It's spotless, safe, English-speaking, and almost everything is step-free with lifts, clean toilets and air-conditioning a few steps away. That makes it just as good for parents in their 60s as for kids under 10.

This guide is built around one idea: do less, enjoy more. Plan one main outing a day, keep afternoons indoors and leave room for naps and long dinners.

Why Singapore works so well for families

  • Short flights from most Indian metros (about 4–6 hours), often overnight
  • Familiar food: South and North Indian vegetarian food is easy to find all over the island
  • Accessible transport: every MRT station has lifts, and buses kneel for wheelchairs and strollers
  • Safe at night, so evening light shows and walks are relaxed

Best attractions for parents and grandparents

Gardens by the Bay Marina Bay

The Flower Dome and Cloud Forest are cool, air-conditioned conservatories with gentle paths and lots of benches. Stay for the free Garden Rhapsody light show at the Supertrees in the evening.

Marina Bay and the river City centre

Watch the free Spectra light and water show at Marina Bay Sands, take a relaxing river cruise from Clarke Quay, or ride the Singapore Flyer for seated city views.

Seated sightseeingEvening favourite

Little India and temples Serangoon Road

Parents often love this most: Sri Veeramakaliamman and Sri Srinivasa Perumal temples, flower garlands, sweet shops and familiar thalis. Go in the morning when it's cooler and calmer.

Singapore Botanic Gardens Free entry

A UNESCO World Heritage garden with shaded paths and the National Orchid Garden. Early morning walks here are lovely.

Best attractions for kids

Mandai Wildlife Reserve Zoo, River Wonders, Night Safari, Bird Paradise

Singapore Zoo is one of the best in the world, with open enclosures and trams. The Night Safari is a magical evening tram ride. Pick one or two parks; trying all in one day is exhausting.

Getting around with strollers, seniors and wheelchairs

  • MRT and buses are step-free. Children below 0.9 m travel free; older kids and adults can tap in with a contactless card.
  • Grab is easy for door-to-door trips. Choose a family option if you need a child seat, or a larger car for luggage.
  • Wheelchairs: many attractions, including the zoo and Gardens by the Bay, lend wheelchairs or mobility scooters; ask at the entrance or book ahead.
  • Strollers are welcome almost everywhere, and most malls have nursing rooms.

Food: vegetarian, Jain and kid-friendly

Little India has pure-vegetarian classics like Komala Vilas and Ananda Bhavan, and Indian vegetarian restaurants are spread across the island. Food courts in malls are great with kids: everyone picks something different. For Jain meals, call ahead; many Indian restaurants can make no-onion, no-garlic dishes. See our Indian restaurants guide.

Where to stay

Choose a hotel next to an MRT station; it saves more energy than anything else. Bugis, City Hall, Orchard and Little India are central and well connected. Families of four or more should look at serviced apartments or connecting rooms; standard Singapore hotel rooms are compact.

Money-saving tips for families

  • Book attraction combos online in advance, often cheaper than at the gate
  • Mix paid days with free ones: Botanic Gardens, light shows, Merlion Park, Jewel Rain Vortex
  • Eat at hawker centres and food courts for most meals
  • Carry refillable water bottles; tap water is safe to drink

Health and travel insurance

Medical care is excellent but expensive for visitors, so buy travel insurance that covers seniors' pre-existing conditions if you can. Carry prescriptions and enough medicine for the trip. Pharmacies like Guardian and Watsons are everywhere for basics.

FAQs

Is Singapore good for elderly parents?

Yes. Public transport and attractions are step-free with lifts, many attractions lend wheelchairs, vegetarian Indian food is easy to find and the city is very safe. Plan one main outing a day and avoid the midday heat.

How many days in Singapore with kids?

Four to five days lets you do Gardens by the Bay, the zoo or Night Safari, Sentosa and Little India at a relaxed pace.

Do kids pay on Singapore MRT?

Children below 0.9 m travel free. Older children pay a fare like adults unless they have a local concession card.

Which area is best to stay in Singapore with family?

Pick a hotel next to an MRT station in a central area such as Bugis, City Hall, Orchard or Little India. Larger families should consider serviced apartments.
