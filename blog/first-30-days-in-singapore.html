Your First 30 Days in Singapore: A Checklist

Everything to sort out after you land, week by week, from the SIM card and Singpass to your bank, rent and sending money home.

10 min readUpdated November 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: kallerna, CC BY-SA 4.0

Your pass is approved and the flight is booked. Now what? The first month in Singapore is full of small setup tasks, and doing them in the right order saves a lot of running around. Here's the checklist we wish we'd had, with what we actually use.

What surprised us most Our first month

How well planned everything is, and how strict the rules are about everything. Transport, government services and housing all just work, but there's a rule for almost everything, from eating on the MRT to where you can smoke. Learn them early and life here is very easy.

As of November 2026Rules and prices in Singapore change. We check official sources (HDB, URA, IRAS, MOM), but always confirm the latest before you sign or pay.

Before you fly

  • In-Principle Approval (IPA) letter: print it and keep a copy on your phone. You'll show it on arrival.
  • SG Arrival Card: submit it online in the 3 days before you arrive.
  • Documents: passport, degree certificates, and for family passes, marriage and birth certificates. Carry originals and scans.
  • Temporary stay: book a hotel or serviced apartment for 2–4 weeks. Airbnb-style short stays in flats and condos are illegal here.
  • Money: carry some Singapore dollars and a card that works abroad. A Wise account is handy for moving money before your local bank account is ready.
  • Indian bank and KYC: keep your Indian mobile number active for OTPs, and plan to update your Indian bank accounts to NRI status (see below).

Week 1: get connected and moving

SIM card

We use Singtel. You can buy a prepaid SIM at Changi Airport or any 7-Eleven on arrival (bring your passport), then switch to a monthly plan once you have your pass card. Singtel, StarHub and M1 are the big three; budget SIM-only brands like GOMO, Giga, Circles.Life and SIMBA offer cheaper data plans if you don't need extras.

Getting around

  • MRT and buses: just tap your contactless Visa or Mastercard (or phone) at the gates. You don't need a separate travel card.
  • Apps: Google Maps for routes, Grab or Gojek for taxis and food delivery, and MyTransport.SG for bus arrival times.
  • No eating or drinking on the MRT: fines can be up to S$500.

Medical check-up and pass card

  • If your IPA asks for a medical examination, do it at a registered clinic in your first days.
  • Your employer then requests the pass card. MOM's notification letter will tell you whether you need to register your fingerprints and photo at the MOM Services Centre, which is usually within 2 weeks.
  • The card is delivered to your address, and your digital pass appears in the SGWorkPass app.

Week 2: ID, bank and money

Singpass

Singpass is your digital ID for almost every government service, from tax to healthcare. Once you have your FIN (on your pass), you can register; for many EP holders it's set up during the MOM visit. Download the Singpass app and set up face verification.

Bank account

  • The main local banks are DBS/POSB, OCBC and UOB. Most let you open an account with your pass, and some allow it with your IPA and passport before the card arrives.
  • Set up PayNow: it links your mobile number to your account for instant free transfers, and almost everyone here uses it, from rent to splitting bills.
  • Give your account details to HR for your salary.

Sending money home

We use the Wise app to send money to India (full comparison in how to send money from Singapore to India). It shows the exchange rate and fee upfront, so it's easy to compare with your bank's remittance rate. New users who sign up with our Wise invite link get a welcome perk, and we may earn a referral reward.

Your Indian bank accounts (NRI status)

Once you move abroad for work, you usually become an NRI under Indian rules. Your regular savings accounts should then be converted to NRO accounts, and many people open an NRE account for money earned abroad. Update your bank and your KYC (address, mobile, PAN) early. Speak to your bank or a tax adviser for your situation, and see our NRI guide for Indians in Singapore.

Weeks 3–4: settle in

  • Find a long-term home: HDB or condo, deposits, agents and what to check, in our guide to renting in Singapore.
  • Home internet: fibre broadband is fast and cheap; installation usually takes a few days, so book it as soon as you sign the lease.
  • Utilities: open an SP Group account for electricity and water if the bills are in your name.
  • Family passes: if your family is joining you, your employer applies for their Dependant's Passes; see our Dependant's Pass guide.
  • Healthcare: check your employer's medical insurance, find a nearby GP clinic, and save the numbers for 995 (ambulance) and 999 (police). More in our healthcare guide.
  • Groceries: NTUC FairPrice, Sheng Siong and Cold Storage for everyday shopping; Mustafa Centre and Little India for Indian groceries. See our favourite Indian restaurants for when you miss home food.

Rules to know from day one

  • No eating or drinking on the MRT and buses.
  • Littering, jaywalking and smoking outside designated areas can all get you fined.
  • Durians aren't allowed on public transport.
  • Chewing gum can't be sold, and you can't bring it in to sell.
  • Keep your address and details up to date with MOM and your employer.

It sounds strict, but that's exactly why the city is so clean, safe and easy to live in. The full list is in Singapore rules and fines.

FAQs

What should I do first after moving to Singapore?

Get a local SIM card, do your medical check-up if required, complete your pass card registration, then set up Singpass and a bank account. After that, focus on finding a long-term home.

Which SIM card is best in Singapore?

We use Singtel. Singtel, StarHub and M1 are the main networks, and budget SIM-only brands like GOMO, Giga, Circles.Life and SIMBA offer cheaper data plans.

Do I need an EZ-Link card in Singapore?

Not really. You can tap a contactless Visa or Mastercard, or your phone, on MRT gates and buses.

How do I send money from Singapore to India?

We use the Wise app because it shows the exchange rate and fee upfront. Banks and remittance services also work; compare the final amount received in rupees.

Can foreigners get Singpass?

Yes. Work pass holders aged 15 and above with a FIN can register for Singpass, often during their MOM registration visit.
