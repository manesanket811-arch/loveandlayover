Renting a Flat in Singapore: HDB vs Condo

Rent prices, HDB vs condo, deposits, agent fees, rules for foreigners and the mistakes to avoid, from a couple renting here.

11 min readUpdated November 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: This Photo was taken by Supanut Arunopra, CC BY 4.0

Rent is the biggest monthly cost for anyone moving to Singapore, and the rental process works quite differently from India. We rent a 1-bedroom + study condo for S$3,000 a month, so here's everything we learned about finding a place, what it costs and the rules you need to know.

As of November 2026Rules and prices in Singapore change. We check official sources (HDB, URA, IRAS, MOM), but always confirm the latest before you sign or pay.

Our take: HDB gives you more space and a local neighbourhood feel for less money. A condo makes sense if facilities and security matter to you, or if you find a good deal like we did.

Rules for renting as a foreigner

  • You need a valid pass. Employment Pass, S Pass, Dependant's Pass and other long-term pass holders can rent. Tourists can't rent a whole HDB flat.
  • HDB minimum stay is 6 months, and the landlord needs HDB's approval. You can check on HDB's website whether a flat can be rented to foreigners, because of the non-citizen quota.
  • Condos must be rented for at least 3 months (a URA rule).
  • Short-term rentals like Airbnb are illegal in HDB flats and condos. For your first weeks, book a hotel or a licensed serviced apartment.
  • Stamp duty: the tenancy agreement must be stamped with IRAS, usually within 14 days of signing. The tenant normally pays. It's a small amount: about 0.4% of the total rent for a one-year lease.

How to find a flat

  1. Start with a short stay: a serviced apartment, a hotel, or a room for the first month while you learn the neighbourhoods.
  2. Search the portals: PropertyGuru and 99.co list most rentals. Facebook groups for Indians in Singapore are useful for rooms and direct-from-owner deals.
  3. Shortlist by commute: stay on an MRT line that goes straight to your office. A 30-minute direct commute beats a cheaper flat with two changes.
  4. View in person or by video call: check aircon, water pressure, mould in bathrooms, noise and the walk to the MRT.
  5. Negotiate: rent, lease length, furniture and repairs are all negotiable, especially for longer leases.
  6. Sign the tenancy agreement, pay the deposit and stamp duty, then do a move-in check with photos.

So for a S$3,000 flat on a one-year lease, keep roughly S$6,000–8,000 ready for the first month: about ₹4.2–5.6 lakh at ₹70 per S$1.

What to check in the tenancy agreement

  • Diplomatic clause: lets you end the lease early if your job moves you out of Singapore, usually after 12 months with notice. Ask for it.
  • Minor repairs clause: tenants usually pay for small repairs up to a set amount (e.g. S$150–200); anything above is the landlord's cost.
  • Aircon servicing: leases often ask the tenant to service the aircon every few months.
  • Inventory list: list every item and its condition, with photos, so you get your full deposit back.
  • Who pays for what: utilities, internet, conservancy fees (HDB) and pest control.

Avoid rental scams

  • Never pay a deposit before viewing the flat (in person or on a live video call) and seeing the owner's details.
  • Check that agents are registered with the Council for Estate Agencies (CEA) public register.
  • Be wary of rent far below market, owners who are "overseas" and can't show the flat, and pressure to pay quickly.
  • Pay to a bank account in the owner's name, not to a third party.

Where Indians like to live

Many Indian families pick areas with good schools, temples and Indian groceries nearby, such as Little India and Farrer Park, Serangoon, Sengkang and Punggol, Woodlands and Yishun, Jurong and Clementi, and the East Coast. The best area is really the one that's a direct MRT ride to your office.

FAQs

How much is rent in Singapore?

Roughly S$900–2,000 a month for a room in a shared flat, S$2,800–4,000 for a whole 3–4 room HDB flat and S$3,200–5,500 for a 1–2 bedroom condo, depending on location. We pay S$3,000 for a 1-bedroom + study condo.

Can foreigners rent an HDB flat in Singapore?

Yes, if you hold a valid long-term pass such as an Employment Pass, S Pass or Dependant's Pass. The minimum lease is 6 months, the landlord needs HDB approval, and quotas limit how many flats in a block can be rented to non-Malaysian foreigners.

Is it cheaper to rent an HDB flat or a condo?

HDB flats are usually cheaper for the same space. Condos cost more but include a pool, gym and security.

How much deposit do you pay to rent in Singapore?

Usually one month's rent for each year of the lease, plus the first month's rent in advance. Stamp duty and an agent fee (if you use your own agent) are extra.

Can I use Airbnb when I first move to Singapore?

No. Short-term rentals in HDB flats and condos are illegal. Use a hotel or a licensed serviced apartment for your first weeks.
