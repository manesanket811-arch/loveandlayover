Visa-Free and Visa-on-Arrival Countries for Indians

Where an Indian passport can take you with little or no paperwork, with entry rules, the best months and costs in rupees.

9 min readUpdated November 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: Priyaflorenceshah, CC BY-SA 4.0

An Indian passport opens more doors than many people think. Across the countries we cover, a good number let Indians in without arranging a visa in advance, a few give a visa on arrival, and many others offer an online e-visa. Here's the full list, with what each one actually needs.

Check before you bookVisa rules change, sometimes with little notice. Everything here was reviewed in November 2026, but always confirm on the official government or embassy website before you pay for flights.

Visa-free countries for Indians

No visa to arrange before you fly, though some ask for a free online form or a permit on arrival.

Bhutan Best Mar–May, Sep–Nov

Indian citizens don't need a visa. You get an entry permit on arrival at Paro or Phuentsholing with a valid passport or voter ID card, and a further route permit to travel beyond Thimphu and Paro. Indian visitors also pay a Sustainable Development Fee (SDF) per person per night (₹1,200 at the time of writing, with reductions for children), and usually need a licensed guide outside Thimphu and Paro. Book permits and hotels through a licensed operator or your hotel.

About ₹₹6,000–₹10,000 a dayBhutan guide

Hong Kong Best Oct–Dec

Indian nationals can visit Hong Kong visa-free for up to 14 days, but must complete the free online Pre-arrival Registration (PAR) on the Hong Kong Immigration Department website before travel. Approval is usually quick and the registration is valid for multiple visits within its validity period.

About ₹₹8,600–₹15,000 a dayHong Kong guide

Kazakhstan Best May–Sep (snow Dec–Mar)

Indian passport holders can visit Kazakhstan visa-free for up to 14 days per visit (up to 42 days in any 180 days) for tourism. Longer stays need an e-visa. Carry a return ticket and hotel booking; hotels register you automatically.

About ₹₹5,100–₹8,500 a dayKazakhstan guide

Macau Best Oct–Dec

Indian passport holders can usually visit Macau visa-free for up to 30 days with a passport valid for at least 6 months. If you travel through Hong Kong, you also need Hong Kong's free pre-arrival registration for Indians before you fly.

About ₹₹10,500–₹17,000 a dayMacau guide

Nepal Best Oct–Nov, Mar–Apr

Indian citizens don't need a visa for Nepal. Carry a valid passport or voter ID card (Aadhaar is generally not accepted as the main travel document for air travel). Children and minors have separate document rules, so check with your airline.

About ₹₹2,500–₹4,400 a dayNepal guide

Philippines Best Dec–May

At the time of writing, Indian tourists can enter the Philippines visa-free for a short stay, with a longer visa-free stay for those holding a valid visa or residence permit from countries such as the USA, Japan, Australia, Canada, Schengen states, Singapore or the UK. Others can apply for a Philippine e-visa online. Everyone must complete the free eTravel registration online within 72 hours before arrival. These rules have changed recently, so check the Philippine embassy website for the current length of stay.

About ₹₹4,500–₹8,300 a dayPhilippines guide

Qatar Best Nov–Mar

Indian passport holders can usually enter Qatar visa-free for up to 30 days. You need a passport valid for at least 6 months, a confirmed return ticket and, in the past, a hotel booking made through the official Discover Qatar website. Requirements such as hotel bookings, insurance and extensions have changed over time, so check the Ministry of Interior and Hayya websites before you travel.

About ₹₹9,200–₹16,000 a dayQatar guide

Thailand Best Nov–Feb

Indians can currently visit Thailand visa-free for tourism, but the permitted length of stay has changed several times recently, so check the current limit with the Royal Thai Embassy before you fly. All visitors must also submit the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) online before arrival.

About ₹₹3,800–₹6,300 a dayThailand guide

Visa on arrival for Indians

You get the visa at the airport when you land, usually for a fee. Carry your return ticket, hotel booking and some cash in US dollars or the local currency.

Bali Best Apr–Oct

A Visa on Arrival (about IDR 500,000, 30 days, extendable once) is available for tourism, or buy it in advance online as an e-VoA on the official Indonesian immigration website. Bali also charges a foreign tourist levy (IDR 150,000), paid online or on arrival. Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months.

About ₹₹3,200–₹5,300 a dayBali guide

Cambodia Best Nov–Feb

Indians need a visa. Get the tourist e-visa on the official portal (evisa.gov.kh) or a visa on arrival at the main airports and border crossings, usually about US$30 for 30 days. All air arrivals must also fill in the free Cambodia e-Arrival card online before the flight.

About ₹₹4,300–₹7,700 a dayCambodia guide

Maldives Best Nov–Apr

The Maldives gives a free 30-day visa on arrival to visitors of all nationalities, including Indians, if you have a passport valid for at least 6 months, a confirmed hotel or resort booking and a return or onward ticket. You must also submit the online IMUGA traveller declaration within 96 hours before arrival.

About ₹₹13,000–₹25,500 a dayMaldives guide

Countries with an online e-visa

Apply online before you travel. Many are quick and simple, like Vietnam, Sri Lanka and Azerbaijan. Others are stricter: Japan asks for the same documents as a regular visa, and Turkey's e-visa is only for Indians who hold certain other visas. Always use the official government website, as many look-alike sites charge extra.

Azerbaijan Best Apr–Jun, Sep–Oct

Indians need a visa. Apply for the ASAN e-visa only on the official portal (evisa.gov.az): it's single-entry for up to 30 days and is usually issued within about 3 working days (a faster urgent option costs more). Fees are around US$25–30; avoid look-alike sites.

About ₹₹6,000–₹10,000 a dayAzerbaijan guide

Dubai Best Nov–Mar

Indian passport holders need a visa for the UAE, usually a tourist e-visa applied for online through airlines such as Emirates or flydubai, licensed travel agents or the official immigration portal. Indians holding a valid US visa or green card, or a UK or EU residence permit or visa, may be eligible for a visa on arrival.

About ₹₹9,200–₹16,000 a dayDubai guide

Egypt Best Oct–Apr

Apply for an Egypt e-Visa on the official visa2egypt.gov.eg portal before you travel (a single-entry tourist e-Visa is about US$25). A visa on arrival is generally only available to Indians who hold a valid US, UK or Schengen visa. Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months.

About ₹₹5,300–₹7,900 a dayEgypt guide

Georgia Best May–Jun, Sep–Oct

Indians holding a valid visa or residence permit from the US, UK, a Schengen country and some other countries can usually enter Georgia visa-free; check the current list. Everyone else needs the e-visa from the official portal (evisa.gov.ge), which takes about a week; there's no visa on arrival. New 2026 rules have been reported on mandatory travel insurance.

About ₹₹4,700–₹7,800 a dayGeorgia guide

Japan Best Mar–May, Sep–Nov

Indian passport holders need a visa for Japan; there is no visa on arrival. Most travellers apply for a single-entry short-stay tourist visa, which is now often issued as an eVISA, through the Japan Visa Application Centres (VFS Global) or an accredited travel agency, not directly at the embassy. Processing usually takes about 5 working days, so apply 3–4 weeks before you fly. Typical documents: passport valid for your trip, application form, a recent photo, flight and hotel bookings or an itinerary, recent bank statements, and ITR or employment proof. The permitted stay (up to 90 days) is shown on your visa. Rules as of October 2026; check the Embassy of Japan in India website for current requirements.

About ₹₹8,000–₹12,500 a dayJapan guide

Malaysia Best Dec–Apr

Visa-free entry for up to 30 days continues until 31 December 2026. From 1 January 2027, Indian passport holders need a visa approved before arrival, applied for on the official MyVISA portal (malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my), with applications open from 1 December 2026. Every visitor must also fill in the free Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) online within 3 days before arrival. See our Malaysia visa guide for 2027.

About ₹₹3,900–₹7,800 a dayMalaysia guide

Oman Best Oct–Mar

Indians need a visa: apply for the e-visa only on the official Royal Oman Police portal (evisa.rop.gov.om) before travelling. Indians holding certain valid foreign visas or residence permits (such as from the US, UK, Schengen countries, Canada, Australia or Japan) may be able to enter visa-free for short stays. Oman launched a new free 14-day tourist visa in 2026, but India wasn't on the published list at the time of writing.

About ₹₹8,800–₹15,500 a dayOman guide

Sri Lanka Best Dec–Mar (west & south)

Indian passport holders need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) for tourism, applied for online on the official Sri Lanka ETA website before you fly. Sri Lanka has at times waived the fee for Indian tourists, so check the current fee and use only the official site.

About ₹₹4,200–₹7,000 a daySri Lanka guide

Turkey Best Apr–May, Sep–Oct

If you hold a valid US, UK, Schengen or Irish visa or residence permit, you can apply for a Turkish e-Visa online at evisa.gov.tr (use only the official site). Otherwise, apply for a regular visa through the Turkish visa centre in India.

About ₹₹5,900–₹9,700 a dayTurkey guide

Uzbekistan Best Apr–May, Sep–Oct

Indians currently need a visa: apply for the e-visa only on the official portal (e-visa.gov.uz), usually about US$20 for up to 30 days. Visa-free entry for Indians has been announced but wasn't yet in force at the time of writing, so check before you apply. Hotels register your stay; keep the registration slips.

About ₹₹4,100–₹6,800 a dayUzbekistan guide

Vietnam Best Oct–Apr

Apply for a Vietnam e-visa on the official portal (evisa.gov.vn). It allows stays of up to 90 days, single or multiple entry; the single-entry fee is about US$25 and processing usually takes 3–5 working days. Use only the official site: look-alike agents charge much more.

About ₹₹2,700–₹4,400 a dayVietnam guide

Tips for smooth entry

  • Your passport should be valid for at least 6 months from your arrival date, with blank pages.
  • Carry printed copies of your return ticket, hotel bookings and any e-visa or arrival card.
  • "Visa-free" still means immigration can ask how long you're staying and where. Have your plans ready.
  • Some countries need a free online arrival card before you land (Thailand's TDAC, for example). Do it a day or two before.
  • Travel insurance is wise everywhere, and required for some visas.

Which one should you pick?

For a first trip abroad with no visa fuss, Thailand, Nepal and Hong Kong are easy wins. For a short luxury trip, Qatar and the Maldives are close to home. Not sure? Try our trip finder quiz or browse every destination guide, where you can filter by easy visa and the best time to go.

FAQs

Which countries can Indians visit without a visa?

From the countries we cover, Indians can currently visit Bhutan, Hong Kong, Kazakhstan, Macau, Nepal, Philippines, Qatar and Thailand without arranging a visa in advance. Some ask for a free online registration or a permit, so check each country's details.

Which countries give Indians a visa on arrival?

Bali, Cambodia and Maldives offer a visa on arrival for Indian tourists, usually for a fee paid when you land.

Which countries offer Indians an online e-visa?

Azerbaijan, Dubai, Egypt, Georgia, Japan, Malaysia, Oman, Sri Lanka, Turkey, Uzbekistan and Vietnam offer an online e-visa or travel authorisation. Requirements vary: some are quick, while Japan needs full documents and Turkey's e-visa depends on holding another visa. Always apply on the official government website.

Is Dubai visa-free for Indians?

No. Most Indians need a UAE tourist e-visa, applied for online. Indians with a valid US visa or green card, or a UK or EU residence permit or visa, may be eligible for a visa on arrival.
