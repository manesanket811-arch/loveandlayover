Singapore Work Culture and Interview Tips for Indians

Hours, leave, contracts and communication style, plus interview and salary tips that work in Singapore.

8 min readUpdated November 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: Basile Morin, CC BY-SA 4.0

Landing the job is step one. Fitting into Singapore's work culture, and acing the interviews to get there, is the rest. Here's how working in Singapore compares with India, from Sanket's experience and the people around us, plus interview tips that work here.

How work culture differs from India

India (typical)Singapore (typical)
HoursLate evenings common, fuzzy end of dayVaries by company, but leaving on time is normal in many teams
CommunicationMore hierarchy, more meetingsDirect, concise, written follow-ups
PunctualitySome flexibilityMeetings start on time
Notice periodOften 60–90 daysOften 1–2 months
Sick leaveVariesTake a medical certificate (MC) from a doctor
  • Leave: professionals typically get around 14–21 days of annual leave, plus Singapore's public holidays, including Deepavali.
  • Bonuses: many employers pay an annual bonus, often including a "13th month" (AWS) payment. Ask how it works.
  • Diversity: teams are very international, and English is the working language.
  • Work-life balance: it depends a lot on the company and team; banks and some tech roles can be intense. Sanket talks about work-life balance in our job video.

Contract vs permanent roles

Many tech roles, especially through IT consultancies and recruitment agencies, are contract roles, often 6–12 months and renewable. They can be a good way in, but compare carefully:

  • Who employs you and applies for your Employment Pass (the agency or the client)?
  • Leave, medical insurance and bonus: contracts often have fewer benefits.
  • What happens to your pass if the contract isn't renewed?

Permanent roles offer more stability, which also helps if you plan to apply for Singapore PR later.

Interview tips that work in Singapore

  • Expect 2–4 rounds: recruiter screen, technical or case interview, hiring manager, sometimes a panel. Most can be done online from India.
  • Use the STAR method (situation, task, action, result) for behavioural questions, with numbers wherever possible.
  • Be concise: short, structured answers land better than long stories.
  • Know your visa position: be ready to say you'll need an Employment Pass, and that you meet the salary criteria for your age.
  • Explain your notice period and whether you can join earlier.
  • Ask good questions about the team, how success is measured and the first 90 days.
  • Time zone: Singapore is 2.5 hours ahead of India; double-check interview times.

Talking salary

  • Quote expected salary as a monthly figure in Singapore dollars, the way it's done here, not as an annual package in rupees.
  • Research the market on Glassdoor, NodeFlair (for tech) and recruiter salary guides.
  • Remember the EP minimum for your age, and that a higher salary also helps your COMPASS score.
  • Look at the whole package: bonus, leave, insurance for family, relocation and housing support.

FAQs

Is work-life balance good in Singapore?

It depends on the company and team. Many teams leave on time and meetings are efficient, but banks and some tech roles can be intense.

How many days of annual leave do you get in Singapore?

Professionals typically get around 14–21 days of annual leave, plus public holidays. The legal minimum under the Employment Act is lower and rises with years of service.

How should I quote my expected salary in a Singapore interview?

As a monthly figure in Singapore dollars, based on market research, and above the Employment Pass minimum for your age. Consider the whole package, including bonus and benefits.

Are contract jobs in Singapore a good idea?

They can be a good way in, especially in tech, but check who sponsors your Employment Pass, what benefits you get and what happens if the contract isn't renewed.
