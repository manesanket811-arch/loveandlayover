Thailand Food Guide

Thai cuisine is legendary—a perfect balance of sweet, sour, salty, and spicy. From aromatic curries to zesty salads and fragrant rice dishes, Thai food is accessible, delicious, and incredibly affordable. Street food culture is central to Thailand's identity.

Must-Try Thai Dishes

  • Pad Thai - Stir-fried rice noodles with shrimp, egg, bean sprouts. Cost: THB 40-80
  • Green Curry (Gaeng Keow Wan) - Spicy green curry with chicken/meat, coconut milk. Cost: THB 60-120
  • Massaman Curry - Rich, mild curry with peanuts, potatoes. Cost: THB 70-130
  • Tom Yum Goong - Hot and sour shrimp soup with lemongrass, lime. Cost: THB 80-150
  • Larb (Laab) - Spicy minced meat salad with fresh herbs. Cost: THB 60-100
  • Pad Krapow Moo - Stir-fried pork with holy basil. Cost: THB 50-90
  • Satay (Sate) - Grilled meat skewers with peanut sauce. Cost: THB 10-20 per stick

Street Food & Snacks

  • Mango Sticky Rice (Khao Niao Mamuang) - Sweet dessert with coconut rice and fresh mango (THB 30-60)
  • Som Tam - Spicy green papaya salad with lime and fish sauce (THB 40-70)
  • Roti - Fried flatbread with sweetened condensed milk or savory curry dip (THB 20-40)
  • Satay Sticks - Grilled chicken/pork skewers (THB 10-20 each)
  • Fresh Fruit Shakes - Mango, papaya, banana smoothies (THB 30-50)
  • Thai Crepes (Roti with Egg) - Crispy roti with egg, sugar, condensed milk (THB 30-50)

Best Places to Eat

  • Chatuchak Weekend Market (Bangkok) - Massive market with 1000s of food stalls, street food paradise
  • Yaowarat Road (Bangkok) - Famous red light district turned night food market, seafood and curries
  • Sukhothai Food Centre (Bangkok) - Historic hawker-style stalls, authentic local food
  • Sunday Walking Street (Chiang Mai) - Night market with food vendors, local dishes, relaxed atmosphere
  • Phuket Town Markets (Phuket) - Fresh seafood, local specialties, cheaper than beach restaurants
  • Street Carts & Vendors - Look for queues of locals—best food is where Thai people eat

Dining Tips

Ask for "Mai Pet" (not spicy) if you can't handle heat—Thai food is often very spicy. Tell vendors your preference and they'll adjust. Fresh ingredients are paramount in Thai cooking. Eat where you see crowds of locals eating. Food is served fresh throughout the day, not kept warm. Try every food stall you see—variety is the spice of Thai travel. Tap water is not safe to drink; stick to bottled or boiled water. Most food stalls accept cash only.

Best Time to Visit Thailand

November - February (Cool Season - Peak Season)

Weather: 20-30°C, dry and pleasant. Crowds: VERY HIGH (peak tourism season).

Highlights: Ideal climate—cool mornings, perfect for exploring. Clear skies and excellent visibility. Loy Krathong festival in November (magical floating lantern festival). Perfect for beach and island hopping.

Costs: Peak pricing. Hotels 25-35% more expensive. Flights booked 2-3 months in advance. Tours crowded.

Best for: First-time visitors, those prioritizing perfect weather, festival experiences.

March - May (Hot Season - Summer)

Weather: 30-40°C, hot and dry. Crowds: Moderate (school holidays in late March-April spike crowds).

Highlights: Songkran Water Festival (Thai New Year) in mid-April—massive water fights and celebrations. Long daylight hours. Mangoes at peak ripeness. Fewer international tourists outside holidays.

Costs: Mid-range pricing. Hotels and flights more expensive during Songkran (3rd-15th April). Best budget deals in May.

Best for: Those comfortable with extreme heat, festival experiences, budget hunters in May.

June - October (Monsoon Season - Low Season)

Weather: 25-32°C, frequent afternoon rain, high humidity. Crowds: LOW (fewest tourists, between seasons).

Highlights: Lush green landscapes from rain. Buddhists' Lent period (spiritual significance). Rice paddies at their most beautiful. Fewer crowds mean authentic experiences and better service.

Costs: LOWEST prices of the year. Hotels 30-40% cheaper. Flights discounted. Tours less crowded. Best value period.

Concerns: Afternoon/evening rain (not all-day rain). Some islands less accessible. Humidity can be intense.

Best for: Budget travelers, those avoiding crowds, photographers seeking green landscapes, experience authentic Thailand.

September - October (Monsoon Tail, Best Budget Value)

Weather: 25-30°C, occasional rain but drying out. Crowds: LOWEST (between monsoon and cool season).

Highlights: End of monsoon season, rain becoming sporadic. Thai culture returns to normal routines. Fewer tourists than any other period. Best chance for authentic interactions.

Costs: Absolute lowest prices. Hotels 40-50% cheaper than peak season. Flights very affordable. Best accommodation deals of the year.

Best for: Budget travelers, experience-focused explorers, those seeking authentic Thailand away from crowds.

Recommendation

Best overall: November-January (ideal weather, manageable crowds after January).

Best weather: November-February (cool and dry).

Budget travelers: June-October (lowest prices, fewer crowds, green scenery).

Festival lovers: April (Songkran) or November (Loy Krathong).

Thailand Neighborhoods & Regions

Bangkok: The Chaotic Capital

Bangkok is Thailand's electrifying capital—a sprawling, chaotic metropolis where ornate temples stand beside shopping malls, street food vendors feed millions, and nightlife pulses until dawn. It's sensory overload in the best way possible, requiring 2-3 days minimum to experience.

What makes it special: Glittering temples, street food capital, vibrant nightlife, bustling markets, motorcycle taxis, authentic Thai energy.

Best for: First-time Thailand visitors, foodies, nightlife enthusiasts, cultural explorers, those seeking authentic urban chaos.

Must-see attractions: Grand Palace, Wat Phra Kaew (Temple of Emerald Buddha), Chao Phraya River cruises, Jim Thompson House, Lumphini Park, Night Bazaar, floating markets.

Budget: ฿800-1,500/day (accommodations ฿300-800, meals ฿150-400).

Local vibe: Chaotic, energetic, loud, spiritual yet modern, street food culture everywhere, motorcycle chaos.

Getting there: Suvarnabhumi Airport (BKK) 30km east. Airport train to city center (฿15-45, 30 mins). Taxis available (metered, around ฿400).

Best time to visit: November-February (cool and dry, perfect weather). March-May unbearably hot.

Chiang Mai: The Cultural Soul

Chiang Mai is Thailand's spiritual heart—an ancient city surrounded by misty mountains, filled with 300+ temples, vibrant night markets, and a relaxed pace that invites you to slow down. It's the gateway to northern Thailand's natural beauty and cultural immersion.

What makes it special: Temples at every corner, laid-back atmosphere, jungle trekking, night markets, elephant sanctuaries, authentic northern culture.

Best for: Culture seekers, nature lovers, trekking enthusiasts, elephant sanctuaries, those seeking slower pace.

Must-see attractions: Wat Chedi Luang, Sunday Walking Street Night Market, jungle trekking (elephants, waterfalls), Doi Suthep Temple (mountain views), local cooking classes.

Budget: ฿500-900/day (accommodations ฿200-500, meals ฿100-250).

Local vibe: Relaxed, spiritual, authentic, quieter than Bangkok, genuinely friendly locals.

Getting there: From Bangkok by train (10-13 hours, ฿300-700) or flight (2 hours, ฿1,000-2,000). Chiang Mai is a 1.5-hour flight or 10-hour drive north.

Best time to visit: November-February (perfect weather, cool evenings, festivals). Stay 2-4 days minimum.

Phuket & Islands: Beach Relaxation

Thailand's southern islands offer the complete beach experience—powdery white sand, crystal-clear waters, water sports, island-hopping, and nightlife. Phuket is the main hub, with Phi Phi Islands, James Bond Island, and countless others within reach.

What makes it special: World-class beaches, water sports (snorkeling, diving, kayaking), island hopping, water clarity, sunset views.

Best for: Beach lovers, water sports enthusiasts, those seeking relaxation, honeymooners, island-hoppers.

Must-see attractions: Phuket beaches (Patong, Kata, Karon), Phi Phi Islands (Maya Bay, snorkeling), James Bond Island, speedboat island tours, diving, sunset beach parties.

Budget: ฿900-1,400/day (beach resorts ฿400-1,000, meals ฿150-350 in tourist areas).

Local vibe: Touristy, beachy, relaxed, water-focused, expat influence, party atmosphere at night.

Getting there: Flight to Phuket from Bangkok (2 hours, ฿1,500-3,000) or from Chiang Mai (3 hours, ฿2,000-4,000). Ferries to islands from Phuket pier (฿300-500 return).

Best time to visit: November-April (dry season, calm waters). Avoid May-October (monsoon, rough seas).

Sukhothai: The Ancient Capital

Sukhothai is a glimpse into Thailand's golden age. This UNESCO World Heritage Site contains the ruins of a 13th-century capital surrounded by peaceful countryside, temples in various stages of restoration, and a much slower, less touristy vibe than the main destinations.

What makes it special: Ancient temple ruins, UNESCO heritage site, peaceful countryside, historical significance, fewer crowds.

Best for: History enthusiasts, photographers, those wanting less-touristy experiences, culture explorers.

Must-see attractions: Sukhothai Historical Park (temples, ruins), Wat Mahathat, Wat Si Chum, cycling through ruins, local villages, night bazaar (smaller scale).

Budget: ฿400-700/day (accommodations ฿150-400, meals ฿80-200).

Local vibe: Peaceful, historical, quiet, authentic Thai countryside, very few tourists.

Getting there: From Bangkok by bus (6 hours, ฿200-400) or train then bus. From Chiang Mai by bus (5 hours, ฿300). Small provincial town—rent a scooter (฿100-200/day) to explore ruins.

Best time to visit: November-February (cool and dry). Stay 1-2 days as day-trip from Chiang Mai or Bangkok.

Getting Around Thailand

Domestic Flights

Between Major Cities: The fastest way to cover long distances. Bangkok to Chiang Mai (1.5 hours), Bangkok to Phuket (2 hours). Cost: THB 1,500-3,500 (budget airlines like Thai AirAsia, Nok Air). Often cheaper than buses for long journeys. Book online or through agents.

Cost Tip: Flights are usually cheaper than flights found 2-3 days in advance. Avoid booking at airports (expensive).

Buses & Long-Distance Travel

Network: Extensive national bus system connecting all cities and towns. Cheap and reliable. VIP buses (sleeper buses) available for overnight travel.

Cost: Bangkok to Chiang Mai (12 hours): THB 500-1,500 depending on bus class. Very affordable.

Classes: Ordinary (cheapest, basic), VIP (comfortable seats), Sleeper (overnight, bunk beds).

Tip: Book overnight buses to save accommodation costs and time. Long journeys on rough roads—budget extra rest time.

Trains

Bangkok to Chiang Mai: Scenic overnight train (13 hours). Cost: THB 700-1,500. Romantic but slower than buses. Book online or at Bangkok Central Station.

Bangkok to Kanchanaburi: 2-hour train ride to famous River Kwai area. Cost: THB 100-300. Scenic route.

Note: Trains are scenic but slower than buses. Best for experience rather than speed.

Taxis & Tuk-Tuks

Bangkok Taxis: Metered, reliable, air-conditioned. Cost: THB 35-50 flag drop + THB 6 per km. Green taxis available via apps (Grab, Bolt). Always ask for meter or agree on price upfront for long journeys.

Tuk-Tuks: Three-wheeled vehicles, quintessential Thai experience. Cost: THB 50-150 per journey (negotiate price before boarding). Fun but can be slow and less comfortable than taxis.

Grab (Ride-Share): Like Uber, available in major cities (Bangkok, Chiang Mai, Phuket). Cheap and convenient. Surge pricing applies during peak hours.

Local Transport in Cities

  • Bangkok BTS Skytrain: Elevated train system covering downtown Bangkok. Cost: THB 16-60. Fast and efficient. Get a Rabbit Card for convenient travel.
  • Bangkok MRT Subway: Underground metro system. Cost: THB 16-55. Covers some areas BTS misses.
  • Motorbike Taxis: Quick for short distances, risky for safety. Cost: THB 20-50. Wear helmet (legally required).
  • Songthaews: Shared red taxis (mini-buses) on fixed routes. Cost: THB 10-20. Slow but cheap. Hail on the street.
  • Chiang Mai Songthaews: Red songthaews are the main transport. Cost: THB 20-40 per journey. Know the route before boarding.

Ferries & Islands

Phuket/Krabi to Islands: Fast ferries to Koh Phi Phi, Koh Samui, Koh Lanta. Cost: THB 400-900 return. Book at ferry terminals or hotels. Speedboats faster but more expensive.

Bangkok to River Temples: Longtail boats available for temple tours. Cost: THB 3,000-5,000 for group tours, THB 1,000-1,500 per person.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Take overnight buses to save on accommodation costs
  • Overnight buses are cheaper than flying when total cost (accommodation + transport) is calculated
  • Use songthaews for local travel—much cheaper than taxis
  • Book buses and flights online 2-3 days in advance for best prices
  • Fly budget airlines for long journeys (often cheaper than sleeper buses)
  • Motorbike rental in Chiang Mai/Phuket is cheap (THB 150-300/day) but practice safe riding

Travel Essentials

Budget Breakdown

  • Accommodation: THB 300-800/night (hostels THB 200-400, mid-range THB 600-1,200, hotels THB 1,500+)
  • Food: THB 100-400/day (street food THB 30-80/meal, restaurants THB 150-400/meal)
  • Transport: THB 50-200/day (local buses THB 10-20, flights THB 1,500-3,500 between cities)
  • Activities: THB 200-800/day (temples THB 50-300, tours THB 500-1,500)
  • Total/day: THB 1,000-1,800 (~$28-50 USD) for budget travelers

Visa & Entry Requirements

For Indian Passport Holders: 60-day visa-exempt entry (automatic upon arrival). No pre-approval needed. Bring: Valid passport (6+ months validity), return flight ticket, proof of funds (THB 20,000 per person—rarely checked, or credit card). Complete the TDAC (Thailand Digital Arrival Card) online or at the airport. Official source: Thai Immigration Bureau Last verified: July 2026.

Tourist Visa (60 days): Available from Thai embassy in India. Takes 3-5 working days. Allows 90 days stay from date of issue (longer validity).

Visa on Arrival: Available at major airports but slower and more expensive than visa-free entry. Use only if needed urgently.

Vaccinations: None required. Malaria pills recommended for rural/jungle areas (consult doctor). Yellow fever vaccination recommended if traveling from endemic countries.

Travel Insurance: Highly recommended. Cost: THB 500-1,500 for 7-10 days. Covers medical emergencies, trip cancellations, lost luggage.

Currency & Money

Currency: Thai Baht (THB). Exchange rates: 1 USD ≈ THB 35-37. 1 INR ≈ THB 0.43. Better rates at banks than currency exchanges. ATMs widely available (Bangkok Bank, Krungsri). Accept Visa/Mastercard.

Practical: Carry cash (THB) for street food and small vendors. Credit cards accepted in hotels, malls, upscale restaurants. ATM withdrawals typically charge THB 100-250 fee. Get local SIM at airport (THB 400-800 for 8-10 days unlimited data).
