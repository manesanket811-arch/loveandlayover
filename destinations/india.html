India Food Guide

Indian cuisine is among the world's greatest—complex spices, regional varieties, and incredible depth. From rich curries to street snacks, vegetarian specialties to regional biryani, Indian food is a journey through flavors. Food is central to Indian culture and offers unforgettable gastronomic experiences at unbeatable prices.

Must-Try Indian Dishes

  • Butter Chicken (Murg Makhani) - Creamy tomato-based curry with chicken. Cost: INR 200-350
  • Samosas - Fried pastries with spiced potato filling. Cost: INR 10-20 per piece
  • Dosa (Masala Dosa) - Crispy fermented rice pancake with potato filling, South Indian. Cost: INR 40-100
  • Thali - Plated meal with curries, rice, bread, lentils, pickles. Cost: INR 100-300
  • Chaat Varieties - Street snacks: Pani Puri, Bhel Puri, Aloo Tikki. Cost: INR 20-50 per plate
  • Biryani - Fragrant rice cooked with meat, saffron, spices. Cost: INR 150-300
  • Tandoori Chicken - Marinated chicken cooked in clay oven. Cost: INR 200-350
  • Masala Dosa - Crispy rice crepe with potato and masala filling. Cost: INR 50-80

Street Food & Snacks

  • Pani Puri (Gol Gappa) - Crispy spheres filled with potato, spicy water (INR 10-30)
  • Chai (Indian Tea) - Spiced black tea with milk (INR 10-20 per cup)
  • Aloo Paratha - Potato-stuffed flatbread (INR 30-60)
  • Lassi - Yogurt-based drink, sweet or salty (INR 30-50)
  • Jalebi - Sweet syrup-soaked spiral dessert (INR 20-40)
  • Vada Pav - Fried potato dumpling in bread, Mumbai (INR 15-30)

Best Places to Eat

  • Street Food Markets - Jaipur bazaars, Delhi's Chandni Chowk, best for authenticity and budget
  • Local Dhabas - Family-run restaurants, road-side eateries with regional specialties, true Indian dining
  • Rajasthan's Restaurants - Udaipur's lakeside restaurants, Jaipur's rooftop eateries, mid-range pricing
  • Hotel Restaurants - Mid-range options in every city, reliable quality
  • South Indian Cafes - Idli, Dosa, Uttapam in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka regions

Dining Tips

Indian food varies greatly by region and religion. Many Indians are vegetarian—vegetarian options abundant everywhere. Spice levels vary; ask for "mild" or "no spice" if you can't handle heat. Most street food vendors have small menus; point at photos. Dhal (lentil curry) is staple protein for vegetarians. Tea culture is strong—chai is everywhere, cheap, and delicious. Most food stalls and dhabas accept cash only. Eat with your right hand (it's culturally respectful) or use utensils provided. Tap water is NOT safe; drink bottled water only.

Best Time to Visit India

October - March (Cool and Dry Season - Peak Season)

Weather: 10-30°C depending on region, dry and pleasant. Crowds: High (peak tourism season).

Highlights: Ideal climate perfect for exploring cities and forts. Cool weather in Rajasthan and Delhi. Clear skies ideal for sightseeing. Diwali celebrations (October/November). Festival season. Best time for trekking in Himalayas.

Costs: Peak pricing. Hotels 20-30% more expensive, especially December-January. Book 2-3 months ahead.

Best for: First-time visitors, those prioritizing ideal weather, festival experiences.

April - June (Hot Season - Summer)

Weather: 30-45°C, hot and dry. Crowds: Moderate (school holidays spike in May).

Highlights: Fewer international tourists. Mangoes and fresh fruits at peak. Holi festival (March, sometimes early April). Long daylight hours.

Costs: Mid-range pricing. Better deals than peak season. May offers budget discounts.

Concerns: Extreme heat, especially in Delhi and Rajasthan. Requires hydration and heat management. Not ideal for outdoor activities midday.

Best for: Budget travelers comfortable with heat, festival experiences (Holi).

July - September (Monsoon Season - Low Season)

Weather: 25-35°C, humid with heavy rainfall (especially July-August). Crowds: LOW (fewest tourists).

Highlights: Lush green landscapes from rain, rice paddies beautiful. Authentic India—locals go about normal life. Lower prices mean deeper exploration. Spiritual significance (Hindu rituals flourish). Kerala's monsoon uniqueness.

Costs: LOWEST prices of the year. Hotels 40-50% cheaper. Flights discounted. Best budget period.

Concerns: Heavy rain can disrupt travel. Some regions become inaccessible. High humidity. Not ideal for trekking (slippery trails).

Best for: Budget travelers, experience-focused explorers, photographers seeking green landscapes, those avoiding crowds.

September - October (Monsoon Tail, Transition - Good Value)

Weather: 20-32°C, rain decreasing, pleasant. Crowds: MODERATE (between seasons).

Highlights: End of monsoon season, rain becoming sporadic. Gandh Jayanti (Oct 2), Dussehra/Dasara festivals. Green landscapes still visible. Fewer crowds than peak season, more authentic experiences.

Costs: Low to mid-range pricing. Good value between peak and monsoon.

Best for: Budget travelers valuing both savings and experience, festival lovers, photographers.

December - January (Winter Peak)

Weather: 10-25°C in north (coolest), 25-30°C in south, very pleasant. Crowds: VERY HIGH (holiday season, peak tourism).

Highlights: Most popular months. Ideal weather nationwide. Clear skies. New Year celebrations. Christmas festivities. Perfect for sightseeing and outdoor activities.

Costs: Peak pricing. Hotels 30% more expensive. Dec 20-Jan 2 highest surge. Book 3+ months ahead.

Best for: Those prioritizing ideal weather and willing to book early.

Recommendation

Best overall: October-November or February-March (ideal weather, manageable crowds, good value).

Best weather: December-January (coolest and driest).

Budget travelers: July-September (lowest prices, green landscapes, authentic experiences).

First-timers: October-March (reliable weather, festivals, peak infrastructure).

India Neighborhoods & Regions

Delhi: The Capital's Contrasts

Delhi is India's capital and the gateway for most travelers—a sprawling city where ancient monuments (Mughal forts, mosques) stand beside modern metro lines, where Old Delhi's chaos contrasts with New Delhi's wide avenues, and where street food culture thrives at every corner. It requires 2-3 days to experience its layers.

What makes it special: Mughal monuments, UNESCO sites, street food culture, diverse markets, historical weight, modern infrastructure.

Best for: First-time India visitors, history enthusiasts, foodies, culture explorers, monument chasers.

Must-see attractions: Red Fort, Jama Masjid, Chandni Chowk (Old Delhi market), Qutub Minar, Humayun's Tomb, India Gate, Rashtrapati Bhavan.

Budget: ₹1,000-2,000/day (accommodations ₹400-1,000, meals ₹200-500).

Local vibe: Chaotic, fast-paced, diverse, street food everywhere, historical sites on every corner, bustling markets.

Getting there: Indira Gandhi International Airport (25km west). Airport express train to city center (₹100, 20 mins) or taxi/Uber (₹600-800).

Best time to visit: October-March (cool and pleasant). April-June unbearably hot, July-September monsoon.

Agra: The Taj Mahal Romance

Agra is defined by one monument—but what a monument. The Taj Mahal is one of the world's most beautiful buildings, a perfect white marble mausoleum set in gardens. Seeing it at sunrise or sunset is unforgettable. The city also offers Mughal palaces and local culture beyond the monument.

What makes it special: Taj Mahal (world's most famous love monument), Mughal architecture, romantic destination, perfectly preserved gardens.

Best for: Monument chasers, couples, photographers, those wanting quintessential India experience, history lovers.

Must-see attractions: Taj Mahal (sunrise/sunset), Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri (nearby ghost city, UNESCO), local markets, riverfront walks.

Budget: ₹800-1,500/day (accommodations ₹400-800, meals ₹200-400).

Local vibe: Touristic but authentic, monument-focused, relaxed pace outside tourist areas, spiritual atmosphere.

Getting there: From Delhi by train (3-4 hours, ₹300-500) or car (4-5 hours, ₹2,000-3,000 shared). Most visitors do Delhi-Agra day trip or 1-2 night stay.

Best time to visit: October-March (cool weather perfect for sightseeing). November-January best for sunrise at Taj Mahal.

Jaipur: The Pink City

Jaipur is Rajasthan's crown jewel—called the "Pink City" for its terracotta-colored buildings painted per a royal decree. It's a vibrant, colorful city of palaces, forts, markets, and desert landscapes. The city is gateway to Rajasthan's forts and villages.

What makes it special: Pink-painted buildings, City Palace, Jantar Mantar (astronomical instruments), desert location, Rajasthani culture, colorful bazaars.

Best for: Architecture enthusiasts, culture lovers, those exploring Rajasthan, photographers of colorful streets.

Must-see attractions: City Palace, Jantar Mantar, Hawa Mahal (Palace of Winds), Albert Hall Museum, Nahargarh Fort (views), local bazaars, camel safaris nearby.

Budget: ₹1,000-1,800/day (accommodations ₹400-900, meals ₹200-400).

Local vibe: Colorful, vibrant, authentic Rajasthani culture, warm hospitality, chaotic bazaars, desert spirituality.

Getting there: From Delhi by train (5-6 hours, ₹300-600) or car (5-6 hours). From Agra by train or car (4 hours). Most do Delhi-Agra-Jaipur triangle.

Best time to visit: November-February (perfect weather, cool nights). March-May hot, June-September monsoon.

Rajasthan: The Desert Soul

Rajasthan is India's most enchanting state—a sprawling desert region of magnificent forts and palaces, colorful textiles, camels, and vivid culture. Beyond Jaipur lies Udaipur (lake palaces), Jodhpur (blue-painted old city), Bikaner (desert forts), and countless villages where traditional life continues.

What makes it special: Desert landscapes, magnificent forts and palaces, camel safaris, colorful textiles and culture, romantic lakes, traditional villages.

Best for: Adventure seekers, history buffs, photographers, culture explorers, those seeking authentic Indian experiences.

Must-see attractions: Mehrangarh Fort (Jodhpur), Lake Palace (Udaipur), Jaisalmer Fort, camel safaris (Thar Desert), Pushkar Camel Fair, village homestays, colorful bazaars.

Budget: ₹1,000-2,000/day (accommodations vary widely ₹300-1,500, meals ₹200-500).

Local vibe: Spiritual, artistic, slow-paced outside major cities, genuinely welcoming, connected to desert and tradition.

Getting there: Various entry points: Jaipur (air/rail hub), Udaipur (southwest), Jodhpur (west). Internal transport by bus or car (scenic but long journeys). Flights between major cities if time-limited.

Best time to visit: October-March (ideal weather). December-January coolest. November-December and February-March perfect for camel safaris and festivals.

Getting Around India

Indian Railways - The Backbone

Overview: One of the world's largest railway networks. Connects every major city and town. The Indian way to travel. Affordable, extensive, and uniquely Indian experience.

Trains for Tourists: Rajdhani (fast, premium), Shatabdi (express), AC 2-tier sleeper (comfortable for long journeys). Cost: Delhi-Agra express: INR 200-500. Delhi-Rajasthan: INR 400-800.

Classes: General (cheapest, crowded), Sleeper (affordable overnight), AC 2/3-tier (comfortable), AC First (premium but not luxury).

Booking: Book on IRCTC.co.in (official website) or at railway stations. Book 1-4 weeks in advance. Cancelled ticket refunds are common—book flexibility.

Domestic Flights

Fast but Expensive: Delhi to Jaipur (1 hour), Delhi to Kerala (3 hours). Cost: INR 2,000-5,000. Budget airlines (IndiGo, SpiceJet, AirAsia) are cheaper than premium carriers.

Tip: Often cheaper to take trains than fly for distances under 500km. Flights more economical for long distances (Delhi-Kerala).

Long-Distance Buses

Network: Extensive government and private bus routes. Cheap but slow. Available everywhere, including rural areas.

Cost: Delhi to Jaipur (6 hours): INR 300-600. Comfortable but journey times are long.

Tip: Overnight buses save accommodation costs. Book at bus stations or online.

Local Transport in Cities

  • Delhi Metro: Fast, air-conditioned, efficient. Cost: INR 10-60 per journey. Get a rechargeable card for convenience.
  • Taxis/Autos: Metered (Uber/Ola apps), negotiate price before boarding for street autos. Cost: INR 50-200 per journey.
  • Auto-Rickshaws: Three-wheeled vehicles, quintessential Indian experience. Cost: INR 30-100 depending on distance. Negotiate price upfront.
  • Buses: Cheap but crowded and slow (INR 5-20). Use for short distances or tourist routes.
  • Taxis (City Cabs): Yellow-and-black taxis in major cities, metered. Use apps (Uber, Ola) for transparency and safety.

Rajasthan Travel Tips

Golden Triangle Route: Delhi (2 days) → Agra (1 day, Taj Mahal) → Jaipur (2 days, forts, bazaars). Most popular itinerary for good reason.

Transport Options: Trains between cities (comfortable sleeper), or tourist taxis/cars for groups (split cost, flexible timing).

Car Rental: Useful for Rajasthan's palace circuit. Cost: INR 2,000-4,000/day with driver. Splits well among groups.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Take overnight trains to save hotel cost and travel time
  • Use Indian Railways—far cheaper than flights for medium distances
  • Book train tickets 2-3 weeks in advance for best availability
  • Use Ola/Uber instead of street taxis for accurate, transparent pricing
  • Walk within cities—Delhi and Jaipur have walkable areas
  • Hire cars with driver for groups in Rajasthan (split costs make it cheaper than trains)
  • Buy multi-day metro passes in Delhi if staying 4+ days

Travel Essentials

Budget Breakdown

  • Food: INR 300-800/day (street to decent)
  • Transport: INR 200-500/day (trains, buses)
  • Activities: INR 500-1,500/day

Visa & Entry Requirements

For Indian Passport Holders: NO VISA REQUIRED! Indian nationals can enter India with a valid passport. If traveling internationally (visiting relatives abroad), check visa requirements for that specific country. Upon return to India, only a passport is needed.

Travel Insurance: Highly recommended. Cost: INR 2,000-5,000 for 7-10 days. Covers medical emergencies, trip cancellations, lost luggage. Especially valuable for trekking in Himalayas or adventurous activities.

Currency & Money

Currency: Indian Rupee (INR). Exchange rates vary. ATMs widely available (HDFC, ICICI, SBI). Debit/credit cards mostly work, but have cash backup as some places don't accept cards.

Practical: UPI (Unified Payments Interface) is hugely popular—Paytm, Google Pay, PhonePe accepted everywhere from street vendors to restaurants. Indian nationals benefit from instant access to banks and familiarity with payment systems. Carry cash for street food and small vendors. Get a temporary SIM card if your plan doesn't have roaming (INR 100-300 for 7-10 days unlimited data).
