Why Visit Iceland?

Iceland is Earth's most otherworldly destination. Thundering waterfalls, massive glaciers, black sand beaches, and geothermal hot springs create landscapes that seem alien. Reykjavik's vibrant culture contrasts with pristine wilderness. The Ring Road connects dramatic scenery around the island. Perfect for 3-10 day road trips with excellent infrastructure and English-speaking locals. Summer offers midnight sun; winter brings Northern Lights. Iceland is pricey but unforgettable.

Iceland Food Guide

Icelandic cuisine is built on fresh seafood, lamb, and local ingredients. Traditional food is hearty and warming—essential in a harsh climate. Modern Reykjavik has excellent restaurants, but rural areas offer simple, authentic dishes.

Must-Try Icelandic Dishes

  • Fresh Salmon (Lax) - Grilled or smoked, exceptional quality. Cost: $15-25
  • Arctic Char - Delicate fish from cold waters. Cost: $14-22
  • Lamb Stew (Kjötsúpa) - Traditional hearty soup with root vegetables. Cost: $12-18
  • Plokkfiskur - Fish and potato stew, comfort food. Cost: $10-16
  • Rye Bread (Rugnúbaugur) - Dark bread baked in geothermal springs. Cost: $4-8
  • Fresh Fish Soup (Yfirlit) - Seafood medley in broth. Cost: $12-18

Seafood Specialties

  • Grilled Whole Fish - Catfish, halibut, or cod. Cost: $16-26
  • Lobster Tail (Humar) - Langoustine specialty, expensive. Cost: $25-40
  • Scallops (Kambar) - Fresh and tender. Cost: $18-28
  • Icelandic Mussels - Often in white wine sauce. Cost: $12-20

Street Food & Snacks

  • Hot Dog (Pylsa) - Icelandic style with lamb sausage. Cost: $4-7
  • Fish & Chips - Casual restaurants, quality fish. Cost: $12-18
  • Skyr - Icelandic yogurt, creamy and thick. Cost: $2-4
  • Bread & Jam - Simple café breakfast. Cost: $5-8

Best Places to Eat

  • Fish Market (Reykjavik) - Contemporary seafood restaurant
  • Grillmarkadurinn (Reykjavik) - Fine dining with lamb and fish
  • Cafe Culture (Reykjavik) - Casual, affordable, local favorite
  • Grocery Store Delis - Budget meals €8-12, simple but satisfying
  • Small Fishing Villages - Fresh fish restaurants, authentic, reasonable

Dining Tips

Reykjavik restaurants expensive (main dishes €20-40). Cooking in self-catering apartments saves money. Buy groceries at Bonus or Netto supermarkets (budget-friendly chains). Coffee culture strong—cafés offer cheap coffee (€3-4). Fish abundant and fresh year-round. Vegetarian options limited outside Reykjavik.

Best Time to Visit Iceland

Summer (June - August)

Weather: 12-18°C, mostly sunny. Midnight sun (no darkness June-July). Crowds: PEAK season.

Highlights: Midnight sun phenomenon (magical). Best weather for Ring Road driving. All roads passable (mountain roads open). Puffins visible (June-August). Waterfalls full from snowmelt.

Costs: HIGHEST of year. Hotels €120-250. Car rental €80-150/day. Book 3+ months ahead.

Best for: Road trippers, those seeking midnight sun, photographers.

Spring (April - May)

Weather: 5-12°C, increasing daylight (17-20 hours). Crowds: Moderate.

Highlights: Days rapidly lengthening. Snowmelt starts creating full waterfalls. Some mountain roads open by May. Easter in April brings holiday visitors.

Costs: Mid-range. Hotels €80-140. Better value than summer.

Best for: Flexible travelers, those avoiding peak crowds, spring enthusiasts.

Autumn (September - October)

Weather: 5-12°C, crisp and clear. 12-14 hours daylight. Crowds: Declining.

Highlights: Early Northern Lights possible (late September). Still warm enough for Ring Road. Fewer tourists. Golden light for photography. Fall colors in moss and lichen.

Costs: Moderate-High. Hotels €70-130. Cheaper than summer.

Best for: Budget-conscious, early Northern Lights hunters, photographers.

Winter (November - March)

Weather: -5 to 2°C, dark with only 4-5 hours daylight. Snow unpredictable. Crowds: Very low.

Highlights: Northern Lights visible (clear nights). Blue Ice Caves. Magical snowy landscapes. Ski season in Bláfell. Hot springs warm contrast. Solitude.

Costs: LOWEST of year. Hotels €50-100. 40-50% cheaper than summer.

Best for: Northern Lights seekers, budget travelers, winter enthusiasts.

Recommendation

Best overall: June-August (midnight sun, all roads open, best weather).

Budget travelers: September-October or November-March (lower prices, still possible).

Northern Lights: September-March (clearer dark skies).

Getting Around Iceland

Car Rental - Essential for Ring Road

Ring Road (Route 1): 1,332 km circle around entire island. Paved, well-maintained. Many sights require driving unpaved mountain roads. Rental mandatory for most travelers. Cost: €60-150/day depending on season and car type. Book 2-3 months ahead for summer.

Road Safety: Drive on RIGHT side. Speed limits: 90 km/h highway, 50 km/h towns. Headlights required 24/7. Gravel roads common—drive slowly. 4WD recommended September-May for mountain roads.

Gas: Expensive. €1.50-1.70 per liter. Fill up in towns (gas stations sparse on Ring Road).

Buses

FlixBus and Strætó operate routes. Cost: €20-50 for intercity journeys. Slower than driving (7-8 hours Ring Road, broken into segments). Good for those uncomfortable driving.

Domestic Flights

Air Iceland Express connects Reykjavik to Akureyri, Ísafjörður. Cost: $50-100 one-way. Useful for skipping long drives, saving time. Summer only for some routes.

Reykjavik City Transport

  • Buses: Extensive city network. Cost: ISK 500-3,100. Day pass: ISK 3,000
  • Walking: City center very walkable
  • Taxis: Expensive. €10 flag drop + €2/km. Uber available

Tours & Guided Drives

Tour companies offer Golden Circle (€80-120) and South Coast (€100-150) day tours. Useful if not renting a car. Often include Blue Lagoon admission. Professional drivers, minimal navigation stress.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Rent car in shoulder seasons (May-June, September) for 20-30% discount
  • Smaller economy car cheaper than SUV; high-clearance needed only for F-roads
  • Self-cater in apartments; restaurants 2-3x supermarket prices
  • Free waterfalls, geysers, hot springs—best experiences are free

Travel Essentials

Budget Breakdown

  • Accommodation: ISK 12,000-40,000/night (~$85-290) (hostels ISK 8,000-12,000, mid-range ISK 15,000-25,000, hotels ISK 30,000+)
  • Food: ISK 8,000-18,000/day (~$58-130) (groceries €20-30, restaurants €20-35/meal)
  • Car rental: ISK 8,000-20,000/day (~$60-150) (fuel extra)
  • Activities: ISK 8,000-40,000/day (~$58-290) (Free waterfalls/geysers, Blue Lagoon ISK 8,000, tours ISK 12,000+)
  • Total daily budget: $65-125/day for budget-mid travelers

Visa & Entry Requirements

For Indian Passport Holders: 90-day Schengen visa required. Apply at Icelandic embassy. Processing: 5-15 days. Cost: €80. Proof: €2,500+ funds, return flight, accommodation, travel insurance.

EU/US/UK/Australia: Visa-free for 90 days within Schengen area.

Currency: Icelandic Króna (ISK). 1 USD ≈ 130-140 ISK. Credit cards widely accepted. ATMs everywhere. No forex needed.

Getting a SIM: Buy from Vodafone, Síminn, or Nova at airport (ISK 3,000-8,000 for 8-10 days data).

Vaccinations & Health

No vaccinations required. Healthcare excellent. Travel insurance recommended for activities (hiking, glacier tours). Cost: ISK 3,000-8,000 for 7-10 days.
