Why Visit South Korea?

South Korea merges ancient Buddhist temples with ultra-modern technology. Seoul's neon-lit streets contrast with serene mountain temples. Busan offers coastal beauty and fresh seafood. K-culture, world-class food, excellent public transport, and affordable prices make South Korea perfect for 3-10 day trips. From DMZ tours to temple stays, Korean BBQ feasts to shopping in Myeongdong, Korea delivers diverse unforgettable experiences.

South Korea Food Guide

Korean cuisine is bold, balanced, and deeply rooted in fermentation and banchan (side dishes). Spicy, savory, sweet, and sour flavors harmonize in every meal. Food culture is communal—eating together strengthens bonds.

Must-Try Korean Dishes

  • Bibimbap - Mixed rice with veggies, egg, spicy sauce. Cost: ₩7,000-12,000
  • Bulgogi - Marinated grilled beef, sweet and savory. Cost: ₩10,000-18,000
  • Korean BBQ (Samgyeopsal) - Grilled pork belly you cook at table. Cost: ₩15,000-30,000 per person
  • Tteokbokki - Spicy rice cakes in gochujang sauce. Cost: ₩5,000-8,000
  • Kimchi Jjigae - Kimchi stew with pork, tofu, vegetables. Cost: ₩8,000-12,000
  • Tonkatsu - Breaded pork cutlet, crispy and tender. Cost: ₩10,000-15,000

Seafood Specialties

  • Sashimi (Hoe) - Fresh raw fish, premium quality. Cost: ₩15,000-30,000
  • Grilled Fish (Saengseon Gui) - Whole fish grilled with salt. Cost: ₩12,000-20,000
  • Spicy Squid (Ojingeochim) - Tender squid in red sauce. Cost: ₩10,000-15,000
  • Clam Soup (Jogae Guk) - Broth with fresh clams. Cost: ₩8,000-12,000

Street Food & Snacks

  • Kimbap - Korean sushi rolls, portable and cheap. Cost: ₩3,500-6,000
  • Hotteok - Sweet pancake with brown sugar filling. Cost: ₩3,000-5,000
  • Korean Fried Chicken (ChickenKing) - Crispy, spicy or soy glazed. Cost: ₩15,000-25,000 per order (serves 2-3)
  • Odeng (Fishcake skewers) - Street vendor staple. Cost: ₩2,000-4,000

Best Places to Eat

  • Gwangjang Market (Seoul) - Street food, bibimbap, kimbap stalls
  • Myeongdong Nolita (Seoul) - Street food alley, touristy but vibrant
  • Jagalchi Market (Busan) - Fresh seafood market with restaurants
  • Korean BBQ Restaurants - Specialized venues for grilled meats, ₩20,000-40,000
  • Convenience Stores (CU, GS25) - Quality meals ₩4,000-8,000, widely available

Dining Tips

Remove shoes when eating on tatami mats. Slurp noodles (polite). Banchan (side dishes) are free and refillable—always eat them. Tap water safe and free. Tipping NOT expected or customary in Korea. Most restaurants have English menus or picture menus in Seoul.

Best Time to Visit South Korea

Spring (March - May)

Weather: 10-20°C, mild and increasingly warm. Crowds: HIGH (cherry blossoms April-May).

Highlights: Cherry blossoms peak late March to early April—stunning. Mild weather perfect for walking. Gardens burst with color. Spring festivals throughout the country.

Costs: Peak pricing. Hotels 20-30% more. Flights book 2+ months ahead.

Best for: First-timers seeking iconic spring experience, photography.

Summer (June - August)

Weather: 20-30°C, humid with rain in June-July. Crowds: Moderate to High.

Highlights: Festivals and fireworks, beach season starts July-August, night markets vibrant, warm enough for water sports.

Costs: Mid to High. Schools on vacation mid-July to August.

Best for: Festival enthusiasts, those comfortable with heat and humidity.

Autumn (September - November)

Weather: 15-25°C, crisp and clear—ideal. Crowds: High (fall foliage, perfect weather).

Highlights: Fall colors peak October-November. Perfect hiking weather. Clear skies. Typhoon season ends by October. Harvest season brings fresh food.

Costs: High. Peak season second to spring. Book 6-8 weeks ahead.

Best for: Nature lovers, hikers, photographers, best weather overall.

Winter (December - February)

Weather: -5 to 5°C, dry and cold. Rare snow except central regions. Crowds: Low (except Christmas/New Year).

Highlights: Ski season in Gangwon-do, winter festivals, Christmas illuminations (heavily celebrated despite Christian minority), temples and sites peaceful without crowds.

Costs: LOWEST of year. 30-40% cheaper than peak seasons. Great value.

Best for: Budget travelers, those avoiding crowds, ski enthusiasts.

Recommendation

Best overall: Late April-May or September-October (ideal weather, manageable crowds).

Budget travelers: January-February (lowest prices, clear winter skies).

First-timers: April-May (cherry blossoms, despite crowds).

Getting Around South Korea

KTX (Korea Train eXpress) - The Backbone

Seoul to Busan: 2.5-3 hours, ₩48,000-59,000. Fastest intercity option. Book online at Korail.com or at stations. Reserve seat in advance for popular times.

Local Trains (ITX, Mugunghwa): Slower but cheaper. Seoul to Busan: 4-5 hours, ₩25,000-35,000. Good views, comfortable seating.

Subway Systems

  • Seoul Metro: 9 lines, extremely efficient. Cost: ₩1,250-2,450 per ride. T-money card: ₩2,500 initial + load funds
  • Busan Metro: 4 lines. Cost: ₩1,450 per ride
  • Both systems: Clean, English signage, frequent trains (every 2-5 mins)

Buses

Extensive network connecting all cities and villages. Cost: ₩1,450-2,550 within cities (Seoul), ₩15,000-50,000 for intercity. Slower than trains but cheaper. Book online for intercity routes.

Taxis

Affordable in major cities. Flag drop: ₩3,000 + ₩100/km. Night surcharge 20% (10pm-4am). Uber (called Uber Black) available but more expensive. Kakao Taxi app popular for app-based taxis.

Domestic Flights

Asiana and Korean Air connect Seoul to Jeju, Busan, Gwangju. Cost: ₩60,000-120,000 one-way. Book online for discounts. Useful for Jeju island day trip (1 hour flight).

Car Rental

International Driving Permit required. Useful for Jeju island exploration or rural regions. Cost: ₩40,000-80,000/day. Roads excellent. Parking in cities challenging.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Get T-money card for all public transport (saves 10% vs single tickets)
  • KTX cheaper if booked online 1+ week ahead
  • Book night buses/trains for intercity to save on accommodation
  • Walk Seoul neighborhoods (Gangnam, Hongdae, Insadong are walkable)

Travel Essentials

Budget Breakdown

  • Accommodation: ₩40,000-120,000/night (~$30-90) (hostels ₩20,000-30,000, mid-range ₩60,000-100,000, hotels ₩120,000+)
  • Food: ₩20,000-40,000/day (~$15-30) (street food ₩5,000-10,000, local restaurant ₩8,000-15,000, fine dining ₩30,000+)
  • Transport: ₩2,000-10,000/day (~$1.50-7.50) (T-money card, intercity ₩20,000-50,000)
  • Activities: ₩5,000-30,000/day (~$4-22) (museums ₩5,000-10,000, temples ₩3,000-5,000, DMZ tour ₩50,000)
  • Total daily budget: $25-42/day for budget travelers

Visa & Entry Requirements

For Indian Passport Holders: 90-day tourist visa exemption. Automatic upon arrival—no visa needed. Bring: Passport (6+ months validity), return flight, proof of funds (₩1 million or credit card).

Currency: South Korean Won (₩). 1 USD ≈ ₩1,300-1,350. ATMs everywhere accept Visa/Mastercard. No forex needed.

Getting a SIM: Buy at airport from SK Telecom, KT, or LG U+ (₩20,000-40,000 for 8-10 days unlimited data). eSIM available online before arrival.

Travel Insurance: Recommended. Cost: ₩20,000-50,000 for 7-10 days. Covers medical, cancellations, lost luggage.

Vaccinations & Health

None required. Healthcare excellent. English-speaking doctors available in major cities. Tap water safe to drink.
