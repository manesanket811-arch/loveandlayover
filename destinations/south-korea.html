Why Visit South Korea?

South Korea is a vibrant blend of cutting-edge technology, ancient temples, and infectious K-pop culture. From Seoul's bustling streets and neon-lit neighborhoods to serene mountain temples, Korean palaces, and coastal beauty, South Korea delivers world-class experiences in an incredibly walkable and transit-friendly package. Perfect for 3–10 day trips with excellent public transport and unforgettable food.

The energy in Seoul is unmatched, the food scene is phenomenal, and locals are genuinely welcoming to travelers. Whether you're exploring Myeongdong's shopping chaos, meditating at a temple, or eating street food at 2 AM, South Korea feels like controlled chaos in the best way.

South Korea Itineraries: 3, 5 or 7 Days

Pick 3, 5 or 7 days. Each longer plan builds on the shorter one: the first days stay the same and the extra days add more places. Costs are approximate per person per day; prices and timings change, so check before you book.

1

Arrival & Myeongdong

Morning
Arrive at Incheon International Airport. Take the Airport Railroad to Seoul Station (₩9,000, 60 mins) or Airport Limousine Bus to your hotel (₩10,000–15,000, flexible timing). Check in and freshen up.
Afternoon
Head to Myeongdong district via Seoul Metro Line 4. Shop at Korean beauty stores (Etude House, Innisfree, Laneige — prices are lower than international duty-free). Walk through the pedestrian shopping zone. Grab street food: tteokbokki (spicy rice cakes, ₩3,000–5,000), hotteok (sweet pancakes, ₩2,000), or corn cheese at street stalls.
Evening
Dinner at a Korean BBQ restaurant (₩8,000–15,000 per person) or visit Myeongdong's food courts. Explore Lotte Young Plaza or duty-free shops. Walk along the nearby Cathedral area for photos. Return to hotel and rest.
₩35,000–50,000/day
2

Palaces & Gangnam

Morning
Visit Gyeongbokgung Palace (₩3,000 entry). Watch the Changing of the Guard ceremony (11 AM daily). Walk through the palace grounds and nearby Bukchon Hanok Village—traditional Korean houses and cafes. Grab coffee at one of the many trendy cafes (₩4,000–6,000).
Afternoon
Take Seoul Metro to Gangnam. Visit the iconic "Gangnam Style" statue area and explore upscale shopping streets. Try Korean cosmetics at Amorepacific or Missha flagship stores. Lunch: Korean gimbap or bibimbap (₩6,000–10,000).
Evening
Experience Seoul's nightlife. Visit a noraebang (karaoke, ₩15,000–25,000/hour). Grab dinner in Gangnam's restaurant zone or hit up a pojangmacha (street food cart alley) for casual bites and soju. Visit a convenience store like GS25 or CU for late-night snacks and drinks (affordable).
₩40,000–60,000/day
3

Namsan Tower, Bukchon & Insadong

Morning
Visit N Seoul Tower or Namsan Park for panoramic city views. Or, visit Deoksu Palace. Enjoy a final Korean breakfast: kimbap and miso soup at a local restaurant (₩5,000–7,000).
Afternoon
Shopping at Doota or Lotte World Mall. Pick up Korean snacks to take home: ginseng, dried seaweed, instant ramyeon, honey butter chips, etc. Last-minute souvenirs from duty-free at Incheon Airport.
Evening
Wander the hanok houses of Bukchon Hanok Village and the tea houses and craft shops of Insadong, then dinner at Gwangjang Market (bindaetteok and kimbap).
₩35,000–55,000/day
4

DMZ tour

5-day plan
Morning
Join a guided tour to the DMZ (Demilitarized Zone) from Seoul: observatories, the Third Tunnel and Dorasan Station. Carry your passport; tours must be booked ahead and can change at short notice.
Afternoon
Return to Seoul and rest, or visit the War Memorial of Korea (free).
Evening
Have a Korean fried chicken and drinks dinner, a local favourite known as chimaek.
₩90,000–130,000/day (incl. tour)
5

Hongdae, Yeouido & the Han River

5-day plan
Morning
Explore the cafés and street art of Hongdae and Yeonnam-dong.
Afternoon
Visit Yeouido Hangang Park; rent a bike or order delivery fried chicken to the riverside like locals do. The Hyundai Seoul mall is nearby.
Evening
Watch the Banpo Bridge Moonlight Rainbow Fountain (seasonal) or catch Hongdae's street performers.
₩40,000–60,000/day
6

KTX to Busan & Haeundae

7-day plan
Morning
Take the KTX high-speed train from Seoul to Busan (about 2.5 hours, roughly ₩60,000 one way).
Afternoon
Relax at Haeundae Beach and walk the coastal Dongbaekseom trail.
Evening
Ride the Haeundae Blueline Park sky capsule along the coast, or dinner at Gwangalli with views of the lit-up Gwangan Bridge.
₩80,000–120,000/day (incl. KTX)
7

Gamcheon Village, Jagalchi & departure

7-day plan
Morning
Visit Haedong Yonggungsa, a temple on the seaside rocks.
Afternoon
Wander the colourful hillside lanes of Gamcheon Culture Village and see the fish market at Jagalchi.
Evening
Fly home from Gimhae Airport in Busan, or take the KTX back to Seoul for an Incheon flight.
₩40,000–70,000/day

Korean Food You Must Try

South Korea's food scene is legendary. Here's what to eat:

  • Korean BBQ (Gogi-gui) — Grill meat at your table. Budget ₩12,000–20,000 per person. Go to Gangnam or Itaewon for best options.
  • Bibimbap — Mixed rice with vegetables and egg. ₩8,000–12,000. Traditional version at Insadong.
  • Tteokbokki — Spicy rice cakes in gochujang sauce. ₩3,000–5,000 at any street stall.
  • Korean Fried Chicken (Chimaek) — Chicken with beer. ₩10,000–15,000. Chains like Kyochon and Bonchon are excellent.
  • Kimbap — Korean seaweed rolls with vegetables. ₩3,000–6,000. Grab from convenience stores or restaurants.
  • Kimchi Jjigae — Kimchi stew. ₩6,000–10,000. Comfort food in Korean restaurants.
  • Hotteok — Sweet pancakes filled with brown sugar and cinnamon. ₩2,000–3,000 at street carts.
  • Samgyetang — Ginseng chicken soup. ₩12,000–18,000. Famous at Samchon Gomtang in Seoul.

Best Time to Visit

Spring (April–May): Cherry blossoms bloom, mild weather, perfect for walking tours. Peak season — expect crowds.

Fall (September–October): Crisp weather, beautiful foliage, comfortable for sightseeing. Highly recommended.

Summer (June–August): Hot and humid, monsoon season (June–July). Fewer tourists, deals on hotels.

Winter (November–March): Cold and dry. Great for K-pop concerts and winter activities, but bundle up. Fewer visitors = cheaper rates.

Getting Around Seoul

Seoul Metro: World-class subway system. Safe, cheap, and efficient. A single ride costs ₩1,250–2,450 depending on distance. Get a T-money card (rechargeable transit card, ₩2,500–4,000) from convenience stores for convenience.

Buses: Extensive network covering all of Seoul. T-money card works on buses too. Slower than metro but useful for specific routes.

Taxis: Affordable. Base fare ₩3,000, then ₩100 per 142 meters. Avoid during rush hours (7–9 AM, 5–7 PM). Use Kakao Taxi or Naver Taxi apps (with card payment).

Bikes: Seoul Bike sharing stations throughout the city. Cheap and fun way to explore. Download the app to unlock bikes.

Travel Essentials

Visa for Indians: a visa is required. India is not eligible for the K-ETA, so apply for a short-term tourist visa through the Korea Visa Application Centre (KVAC) in India or an accredited travel agency. Processing usually takes about 1–2 weeks; apply early. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you apply.

Money: Korean Won (₩). 1 USD = ~1,100–1,200 KRW (varies). ATMs at airports and convenience stores accept international cards. Credit cards widely accepted in Seoul, but carry some cash for street food and small shops.

SIM Card: Buy at airport (KT, SK Telecom, LG Uplus). 7-day tourist plans ₩15,000–25,000 with unlimited data. Extremely reliable 4G/5G coverage everywhere.

Transport Card: T-money card available at convenience stores. Load cash and use on metro, buses, and many shops. Essential for getting around.

Apps You'll Need: Naver Map or Kakao Map (navigation), Kakao Taxi (taxis), Coupang Eats (food delivery), Papago (translation).

Language: English is not widely spoken outside tourist areas. Carry a translation app (Papago or Google Translate). Younger Koreans speak some English; always be polite—respect goes a long way.

Accommodation: Budget hotels in Myeongdong, Gangnam, or Itaewon: ₩40,000–80,000/night. Hostels: ₩20,000–35,000. Airbnb and Booking.com have good options.

South Korea FAQs

Do Indians need a visa for South Korea?

A visa is required. India is not eligible for the K-ETA, so apply for a short-term tourist visa through the Korea Visa Application Centre (KVAC) in India or an accredited travel agency. Processing usually takes about 1–2 weeks; apply early. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you apply.

How much does a South Korea trip cost per day?

Plan for roughly ₩40,000–60,000 per person per day (about ₹2,500–3,700) for a mid-range trip, covering accommodation, food, local transport and sightseeing. Prices are approximate as of October 2026.

How many days do you need in South Korea?

3 days covers the highlights: Arrival & Myeongdong, Palaces & Gangnam and Namsan Tower, Bukchon & Insadong. With 5 days you can add DMZ tour and Hongdae, Yeouido & the Han River, and a full week adds KTX to Busan & Haeundae and Gamcheon Village, Jagalchi. Our 3, 5 and 7-day itineraries build on each other, so you can stop at any point.

When is the best time to visit South Korea?

Apr–May, Sep–Oct. Spring (April–May): Cherry blossoms bloom, mild weather, perfect for walking tours. Peak season — expect crowds.
