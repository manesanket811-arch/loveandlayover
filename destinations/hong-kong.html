Why Visit Hong Kong?

Hong Kong squeezes a dramatic skyline, harbour ferries, temples, night markets, hiking trails and some of the world's best dim sum into a small, safe and efficient city. It's about 5 hours from Delhi or Mumbai, and Indians can visit visa-free after a quick online registration.

Base yourself in Tsim Sha Tsui or Central, then add Lantau Island, a theme park day and a side trip to Macau for a longer stay.

Hong Kong Itineraries: 3, 5 or 7 Days

Pick 3, 5 or 7 days. Each longer plan builds on the shorter one: the first days stay the same and the extra days add more places. Costs are approximate per person per day; prices and timings change, so check before you book.

1

Tsim Sha Tsui & the harbour

Morning
Land at Hong Kong International Airport and take the Airport Express train to Kowloon or Hong Kong station (about 25 minutes). Get an Octopus card for all transport.
Afternoon
Walk the Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront and the Avenue of Stars, with views across Victoria Harbour.
Evening
Watch the Symphony of Lights show (usually at 8 PM), then ride the historic Star Ferry across the harbour at night.
HK$700–1,200/day
2

Victoria Peak & Central

Morning
Take the Peak Tram up Victoria Peak for the classic skyline view; walk the shaded Lugard Road loop.
Afternoon
Ride the Central–Mid-Levels escalators through SoHo, visit Man Mo Temple and browse PMQ and Tai Kwun.
Evening
Dinner in Central, then drinks around Lan Kwai Fong, or a ride on a double-decker tram ("ding ding").
HK$700–1,200/day
3

Lantau: Big Buddha & Tai O

Morning
Take the MTR to Tung Chung and the Ngong Ping 360 cable car (a crystal cabin has a glass floor) to the Tian Tan Buddha.
Afternoon
Visit Po Lin Monastery (vegetarian lunch available), then a bus to Tai O fishing village with its stilt houses.
Evening
Head back to the city; have dinner at a dai pai dong (open-air food stall) in Temple Street.
HK$800–1,300/day
4

Disneyland or Ocean Park

5-day plan
Morning
Spend the day at Hong Kong Disneyland (great for families) or Ocean Park, with rides, giant pandas and the cable car over the sea. Book tickets online.
Afternoon
Continue at the park; weekdays are much quieter.
Evening
Stay for the evening show, then rest.
HK$1,000–1,800/day (incl. tickets)
5

Kowloon markets & temples

5-day plan
Morning
Visit Wong Tai Sin Temple and the peaceful Chi Lin Nunnery and Nan Lian Garden.
Afternoon
Explore Mong Kok's Ladies' Market, Goldfish Market and Flower Market. Chungking Mansions in Tsim Sha Tsui has many budget Indian eateries.
Evening
Browse the Temple Street Night Market for snacks, fortune tellers and street food.
HK$600–1,000/day
6

Macau day trip

7-day plan
Morning
Take a ferry or the cross-border bus over the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge to Macau (about an hour). Macau has its own entry rules, so check them for Indian passports before you go.
Afternoon
See the Ruins of St Paul's, Senado Square and the Portuguese-style old town, and eat Portuguese egg tarts.
Evening
Visit the Cotai Strip resorts, then return to Hong Kong.
HK$800–1,500/day
7

Dragon's Back hike & departure

7-day plan
Morning
Hike the Dragon's Back trail on Hong Kong Island (about 2–3 hours) for sea views, ending at Big Wave Bay or Shek O beach.
Afternoon
Last dim sum lunch and shopping in Causeway Bay.
Evening
Take the Airport Express (you can check in bags at Hong Kong station for many airlines) for your flight home.
HK$500–900/day

Hong Kong Food to Try

  • Dim sum — Har gow (prawn dumplings), siu mai, char siu bao and cheung fun, best at a busy yum cha restaurant.
  • Roast goose and char siu — Cantonese roast meats over rice.
  • Wonton noodles — Springy noodles in broth with prawn wontons.
  • Egg tarts and pineapple buns — From a cha chaan teng (local café), with Hong Kong-style milk tea.
  • Claypot rice — A winter favourite with a crispy bottom.
  • Egg waffles — Bubble-shaped street snack.

For Indian travellers: Hong Kong has a long-established Indian community. Tsim Sha Tsui (especially Chungking Mansions) has many Indian restaurants, and Buddhist vegetarian restaurants are common.

Best Time to Visit Hong Kong

Autumn and early winter (October–December): the best time, with clear, dry and comfortable weather.

Spring (March–April): mild but often humid and foggy.

Summer (May–September): hot, humid and rainy, and typhoons are possible from about June to October. Check the Hong Kong Observatory for warnings.

Winter (January–February): cool and dry; Chinese New Year brings festivities but also crowds and some closures.

Getting Around Hong Kong

MTR: fast and easy, covering most sights. Tap in with an Octopus card or a contactless card.

Ferries and trams: the Star Ferry and the Hong Kong Island trams are cheap and great for sightseeing.

Airport Express: the quickest way between the airport and the city.

Taxis: red (urban), green (New Territories) and blue (Lantau) taxis; have your destination written in Chinese if possible.

Travel Essentials

Visa for Indians: Indian nationals can visit Hong Kong visa-free for up to 14 days, but must complete the free online Pre-arrival Registration (PAR) on the Hong Kong Immigration Department website before travel. Approval is usually quick and the registration is valid for multiple visits within its validity period. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you travel.

Money: Hong Kong dollar (HK$). HK$1 is roughly ₹11. Cards and Octopus work almost everywhere; carry some cash for small eateries and markets.

Daily budget (per person): Hong Kong hotels are expensive and small; with a mid-range room share, food and transport, expect about HK$800–1,400 a day.

SIM card: tourist SIMs and eSIMs from CSL, 3HK and China Mobile Hong Kong are sold at the airport and convenience stores.

Plugs: type G (three square pins), 220V.

Tips: many restaurants add a 10% service charge; tipping beyond that is optional. Stand on the right on escalators and keep MTR queues orderly.

Hong Kong FAQs

Do Indians need a visa for Hong Kong?

Indian nationals can visit Hong Kong visa-free for up to 14 days, but must complete the free online Pre-arrival Registration (PAR) on the Hong Kong Immigration Department website before travel. Approval is usually quick and the registration is valid for multiple visits within its validity period. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you travel.

How much does a Hong Kong trip cost per day?

Plan for roughly HK$800–1,400 per person per day (about ₹8,600–15,000) for a mid-range trip, covering accommodation, food, local transport and sightseeing. Prices are approximate as of October 2026.

How many days do you need in Hong Kong?

3 days covers the highlights: Tsim Sha Tsui & the harbour, Victoria Peak & Central and Lantau: Big Buddha & Tai O. With 5 days you can add Disneyland or Ocean Park and Kowloon markets & temples, and a full week adds Macau day trip and Dragon's Back hike.
