Why Visit Uzbekistan?

Uzbekistan is the heart of the old Silk Road: turquoise domes, tiled madrasas and bazaars that have traded for 2,000 years. It's about 3 hours from Delhi, very good value, and easy to travel thanks to fast trains between the great cities.

Indians will find a lot of shared history here. Babur, founder of the Mughal empire, came from the Fergana Valley, and his ancestor Timur is buried in Samarkand's Gur-e-Amir, the tomb that inspired the Taj Mahal's design. The classic route links Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara and walled Khiva.

Uzbekistan Itineraries: 3, 5 or 7 Days

Pick 3, 5 or 7 days. Each longer plan builds on the shorter one: the first days stay the same and the extra days add more places. Costs are approximate per person per day; prices and timings change, so check before you book.

1

Tashkent

Morning
Arrive in Tashkent and see the Hazrati Imam complex, home of one of the world's oldest Qurans.
Afternoon
Shop and snack at the domed Chorsu Bazaar, then ride the metro to see its ornate stations.
Evening
Evening at Amir Timur Square and an Uzbek dinner with plov.
UZS 500k–800k/day
2

Fast train to Samarkand

Morning
Take the Afrosiyob high-speed train to Samarkand (about 2 hours; book ahead).
Afternoon
Visit Gur-e-Amir, Timur's tomb, whose dome and design influenced Mughal architecture.
Evening
See the Registan's three madrasas lit up at night.
UZS 600k–1M/day (incl. train)
3

Samarkand's Silk Road wonders

Morning
Walk the avenue of blue-tiled mausoleums at Shah-i-Zinda.
Afternoon
Visit the vast Bibi-Khanym Mosque and the Siab Bazaar next door, then Ulugbek's 15th-century observatory.
Evening
Return to the Registan for sunset photos.
UZS 600k–1M/day
4

Train to Bukhara

5-day plan
Morning
Train from Samarkand to Bukhara (about 1½–2 hours).
Afternoon
Explore the Lyabi-Hauz pool and the old trading domes.
Evening
See the Kalyan Minaret and Poi Kalyan complex glowing at dusk.
UZS 600k–1M/day (incl. train)
5

Bukhara's old city

5-day plan
Morning
Visit the Ark of Bukhara fortress and the Bolo Hauz Mosque with its wooden pillars.
Afternoon
Find the four-towered Chor Minor and browse carpet and suzani workshops.
Evening
Folk dance and fashion show in a madrasa courtyard, then dinner by Lyabi-Hauz.
UZS 500k–900k/day

To Khiva

7-day plan
Morning
Travel to Khiva by train (about 6–7 hours) or fly to nearby Urgench.

Walk the walls of Itchan Kala, a complete walled city (UNESCO).
Evening
Sunset from a watchtower over the mud-brick domes.
UZS 600k–1M/day (incl. travel)

Khiva & departure

Food to Try in Uzbekistan

  • Plov — Rice cooked with carrots, chickpeas, raisins and meat in a giant kazan; the national dish.
  • Samsa — Tandoor-baked pastries; the pumpkin version is vegetarian.
  • Lagman — Hand-pulled noodles in a rich vegetable and meat sauce.
  • Non — Round, stamped bread baked in a tandoor, sold at every bazaar.
  • Shashlik — Skewers grilled over coals and served with raw onions.
  • Dried fruit and halva — Apricots, raisins, nuts and sweets piled high in the bazaars.

For Indian travellers: Uzbek food is meat-heavy, but vegetarians can manage with salads, non, pumpkin samsa, vegetable soups and fruit. Tashkent and Samarkand have Indian restaurants, and you can ask for plov without meat at some places.

Best Time to Visit Uzbekistan

Spring (April–May): the best time, with warm days, blossoms and comfortable sightseeing.

Autumn (September–October): pleasant weather and harvest-season bazaars.

Summer (June–August): very hot, often 40°C in Bukhara and Khiva; sightsee early and late.

Winter (November–March): cold but quiet, with lower hotel prices; some smaller sights close early.

Getting Around Uzbekistan

Flights from India: direct flights from Delhi to Tashkent take about 3 hours; check current routes and other cities when you book.

Between cities: the Afrosiyob high-speed train links Tashkent, Samarkand and Bukhara. Book tickets on the official railway website or app as soon as sales open; they sell out.

To Khiva: a slower train from Bukhara, or a domestic flight to Urgench (30 minutes from Khiva).

In cities: Yandex Go taxis are cheap; Tashkent also has a metro.

Travel Essentials

Entry for Indians: Indians currently need a visa: apply for the e-visa only on the official portal (e-visa.gov.uz), usually about US$20 for up to 30 days. Visa-free entry for Indians has been announced but wasn't yet in force at the time of writing, so check before you apply. Hotels register your stay; keep the registration slips. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you travel.

Money: Uzbek som (UZS). 100,000 UZS is about ₹680. Cards work in many hotels and city shops; carry cash for bazaars and smaller towns. Exchange at banks or official counters.

Daily budget (per person): a mid-range hotel share, food, local transport and sightseeing come to about UZS 600,000–1,000,000 a day, plus trains and flights.

SIM card: Beeline, Ucell and Uzmobile sell SIMs with your passport; airport counters are easiest.

Plugs: types C and F, 220V. Indian two-pin plugs usually fit; bring an adapter for three-pin chargers.

Etiquette and safety: Uzbekistan is very safe and hospitable. Dress modestly at mosques and shrines, remove shoes where asked, and bargain politely in bazaars.

Uzbekistan FAQs

How much does a Uzbekistan trip cost per day?

Plan for roughly UZS 600k–1M per person per day (about ₹4,100–6,800) for a mid-range trip, covering accommodation, food, local transport and sightseeing. Prices are approximate as of October 2026.

How many days do you need in Uzbekistan?

3 days covers the highlights: Tashkent, Fast train to Samarkand and Samarkand's Silk Road wonders. With 5 days you can add Train to Bukhara and Bukhara's old city, and a full week adds To Khiva and Khiva. Our 3, 5 and 7-day itineraries build on each other, so you can stop at any point.

When is the best time to visit Uzbekistan?

Apr–May, Sep–Oct. Spring (April–May): the best time, with warm days, blossoms and comfortable sightseeing.
