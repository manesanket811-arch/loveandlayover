Why Visit Bali?

Bali is a tropical paradise offering ancient temples, stunning rice terraces, pristine beaches, and vibrant culture. From the spiritual heart of Ubud to coastal charm of Kuta and Seminyak, Bali delivers affordable luxury and authentic experiences. Perfect for beach lovers, culture seekers, and digital nomads.

Explore ornate temples like Tanah Lot perched on cliffs, trek through emerald rice fields, dive into crystal-clear waters, and immerse yourself in Balinese traditions. With exceptional hospitality and incredible value, Bali is one of Asia's most beloved destinations.

Bali Itineraries: 3, 5 or 7 Days

Pick 3, 5 or 7 days. Each longer plan builds on the shorter one: the first days stay the same and the extra days add more places. Costs are approximate per person per day; prices and timings change, so check before you book.

1

Ubud: Culture & Rice Terraces

Morning
Arrive at Denpasar Airport. Take shuttle to Ubud (IDR 400k, 1.5 hours). Check in. Visit Ubud Palace - free entry, beautiful courtyard. Walk Ubud Market for local crafts and souvenirs (IDR 50-150k for items).
Afternoon
Explore Tegallalang Rice Terraces (free, 10 mins from town). Walk through iconic emerald-green rice paddies. Grab lunch at local warung (IDR 40-60k). Relax at a cafe overlooking fields. Sunset visit to get best lighting.
Evening
Attend Balinese dance performance (IDR 100-150k) at Ubud Palace. Dinner at restaurant - try Nasi Campur, Gado-gado, or Indonesian curry (IDR 50-100k). Evening walk through Ubud streets.
IDR 750k-1M/day
2

Tanah Lot Temple & Beach Day

Early Morning
Wake before sunrise. Take organized tour or taxi to Tanah Lot (IDR 300k round trip). Watch sunrise from clifftop temples. The iconic Tanah Lot temple sits on volcanic rock in the sea—stunning photography opportunity.
Afternoon
Explore nearby attractions: Batukaru Temple, Jatiluwih Rice Terraces (UNESCO site, free). Lunch at seaside warung (IDR 50-80k). Head to Kuta Beach for swimming and sunset. Relax on black sand beach, watch surfers.
Evening
Dinner at Kuta's beach clubs or traditional restaurants (IDR 80-150k). Watch sunset from beach. Explore Kuta nightlife or beach shacks. Return to Ubud or stay coastal.
IDR 800k-1.2M/day
3

Monkey Forest & Spiritual Sites

Morning
Visit Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary (IDR 100k). Walk through ancient temples with playful monkeys. Beautiful forested paths and peaceful atmosphere. Breakfast at Ubud cafe (IDR 50-80k). Keep bags secure from monkeys!
Afternoon
Visit Goa Gajah (Elephant Cave Temple) - ancient carvings (IDR 50k). Explore Tegenungan Waterfall (free). Visit local artisan studios and galleries. Lunch at family-run warung (IDR 40-60k).
Evening
Cooking class option (IDR 150-250k, 2-3 hours with meal) or sunset yoga (IDR 100-150k). Final dinner at rooftop restaurant (IDR 80-150k). Pack for departure or extend trip.
IDR 700k-1M/day
4

Nusa Penida day trip

5-day plan
Morning
Take an early fast boat from Sanur to Nusa Penida (about 45 minutes). Most visitors join a day tour with a driver, as roads are rough.
Afternoon
See Kelingking Beach (the T-Rex cliff), Broken Beach and Angel's Billabong. The steep path down to Kelingking is only for the fit and well-shod.
Evening
Boat back to Sanur and dinner by the beach. Seas can be rough; check conditions and choose a reputable operator.
IDR 900k–1.5M/day
5

Uluwatu temple & Kecak dance

5-day plan
Morning
Head south to the Bukit Peninsula. Swim at Padang Padang or Melasti beach.
Afternoon
Lunch at a clifftop café, then visit Uluwatu Temple perched above the ocean (sarong provided; watch your sunglasses around the monkeys).
Evening
Watch the Kecak fire dance at sunset at the temple's amphitheatre (about IDR 150k), then seafood on Jimbaran Bay.
IDR 700k–1.1M/day
6

Mount Batur sunrise trek

7-day plan
Early Morning
Pickup around 2 AM for a guided Mount Batur sunrise trek (about 2 hours up). Watch the sun rise over Lake Batur and Mount Agung.
Afternoon
Soak in the natural hot springs at Toya Bungkah, then visit a coffee plantation on the way back to Ubud.
Evening
An easy evening after the early start: a Balinese massage and dinner in Ubud.
IDR 800k–1.2M/day
7

East Bali temples

7-day plan
Morning
Drive east to Lempuyang Temple for the famous "Gates of Heaven" view of Mount Agung. Go early: queues for photos can be long.
Afternoon
Visit the water palace of Tirta Gangga with its stepping stones and koi ponds, and optionally the Tirta Empul water temple on the way back.
Evening
Return to your hotel or near the airport for your flight home.
IDR 700k–1.2M/day

Bali Food Guide

Balinese cuisine is flavorful, spiced, and uses fresh local ingredients. Food is incredibly cheap and delicious. Street food and warungs offer authentic experiences at budget prices.

Must-Try Dishes

  • Nasi Campur – Mixed rice with curries, vegetables, fried egg, sambal. Indonesia's national dish, filling and authentic. Cost: IDR 40-80k
  • Satay (Sate Ayam) – Grilled chicken skewers with peanut sauce. Found everywhere at street vendors with smoky flavor and fresh lime. Cost: IDR 50-100k
  • Gado-gado – Vegetable salad with peanut dressing, tempeh, tofu. Heavy peanut flavor, best at local markets. Cost: IDR 30-60k
  • Mie Goreng – Spicy stir-fried noodles with egg, peppers, onion. Indonesia's most famous noodle dish. Cost: IDR 35-70k
  • Babi Guling – Spit-roasted suckling pig (Hindu Balinese specialty). Succulent with crispy skin, very popular in Ubud. Cost: IDR 80-150k
  • Lumpia – Spring rolls with meat/vegetables, sweet and spicy dipping sauce. Crispy exterior with hot filling. Cost: IDR 20-40k
  • Soto Ayam – Yellow chicken soup with turmeric, ginger, lemongrass. Warming and aromatic, excellent breakfast option. Cost: IDR 40-80k
  • Es Cendol – Sweet coconut milk dessert with green rice flour noodles and palm sugar. Cooling in tropical heat. Cost: IDR 15-25k
  • Perkedel – Fried potato croquettes with savory filling. Cheap breakfast option at warungs throughout Bali. Cost: IDR 10-20k
  • Rendang – Slow-cooked meat in rich coconut spice paste. Complex flavors, can be very spicy, worth trying. Cost: IDR 60-100k

Best Food Spots

  • Ubud Markets – Street food, affordable eats, fresh fruits
  • Warung chains – Look for busy ones with queues (sign of quality)
  • Seminyak Beach Clubs – Upscale dining with beachfront views (IDR 200-400k)
  • Kuta Night Market – Evening street food, local specialties, very cheap

Best Time to Visit Bali

Best overall: April-October (dry season, 25-28°C, sunny, perfect for beaches and temples). This is peak season with most tourists, but the weather is ideal for all activities.

Budget travelers: June-August (high season but stable weather, excellent for hiking and water sports). Prices slightly higher but infrastructure is optimal.

Low season bargains: January-March (rainy but fewer tourists, hotels offer 30-50% discounts). Still possible to visit with rain jackets and flexibility.

Avoid: November-March (monsoon/rainy season, flooding in some areas, lower diving visibility, cyclone risk in northern islands)

Shoulder season: March-April, September-October (fewer crowds than peak, good weather, cheaper flights and accommodation than June-August)

Rainfall patterns: October-April is wet; May-September is dry. Morning rain in wet season usually clears by afternoon.

Getting Around Bali

Taxis & Ride-share

Gojek & Grab: App-based services. Cheap: IDR 20-60k per ride within cities.

Traditional Taxi (Blue Bird): Metered, reliable. IDR 7k start + IDR 4k per km.

Organized Tours

For temples, rice terraces: IDR 300-500k for full-day private tours. Usually includes hotels, sites, meals, driver.

Rental Motorbikes

Popular in Bali. IDR 60-100k/day. Requires international driving permit. Wear helmet—mandatory and accident risk is real.

Buses & Public Transport

Cheap buses connect main towns (IDR 20-50k). Local buses are very cheap (IDR 10-30k) but slow.

Travel Essentials

Visa & Entry

Visa for Indians: a Visa on Arrival (about IDR 500,000, 30 days, extendable once) is available for tourism, or buy it in advance online as an e-VoA on the official Indonesian immigration website. Bali also charges a foreign tourist levy (IDR 150,000), paid online or on arrival. Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you apply.

Currency & Money

Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). Exchange rate: 1 USD ≈ 15,500 IDR.

ATMs: Widespread in cities. Fee: IDR 20-30k per transaction.

Credit Cards: Accepted in hotels and restaurants but not at small warungs. Use cash for meals.

Tipping: NOT expected. Service charge (10-15%) often included. Round up if you wish (optional).

Budget Breakdown (per day, budget traveler)

  • Accommodation: IDR 200-400k (budget hotels, hostels, guesthouses)
  • Food: IDR 150-300k (warungs, street food, occasional nicer meals)
  • Transport: IDR 50-100k (Gojek, taxis, buses)
  • Activities: IDR 100-250k (temples, tours, experiences)
  • Total: IDR 600k-1.2M/day (~$40-80 USD)

Getting a SIM Card

Buy at airport or convenience stores (Indomaret). Providers: Telkomsel, Indosat, XL Axiata. 7-day plans: IDR 100-200k.

Important Notes

  • Sunscreen is ESSENTIAL. SPF 50+ for extreme UV index.
  • Respect temple etiquette: wear sarongs, no shoes inside, be quiet during prayers.
  • Motorbike safety: helmets mandatory, drive on left, roads chaotic.
  • Water: Tap water not potable—drink bottled or filtered water.
  • Monkeys can steal—secure loose items and bags.

How much does a Bali trip cost per day?

Plan for roughly IDR 600k-1M per person per day (about ₹3,200–5,300) for a mid-range trip, covering accommodation, food, local transport and sightseeing. Prices are approximate as of October 2026.
