Why Visit Peru?

Peru is South America's crown jewel. Machu Picchu, one of the world's most iconic sites, anchors an epic country of contrasts—coastal deserts, Amazon rainforest, and snow-capped Andes mountains. Lima rivals any world-class food city. The Sacred Valley offers history, culture, and trekking. Train journeys through mountains are unforgettable. Perfect for 3-10 day trips blending adventure, culture, and cuisine. Peru remains affordable and magical—bucket-list essential.

Peru Food Guide

Peruvian cuisine is among the world's greatest. Lima is South America's culinary capital. Traditional Andean ingredients (potatoes, corn, quinoa) meet coastal seafood. Every region has signature dishes. Food is integral to Peruvian culture and pride.

Must-Try Peruvian Dishes

  • Ceviche - Fresh fish cured in lime, iconic dish. Cost: $8-20
  • Causa Limeña - Layered potato with lime and filling. Cost: $5-12
  • Anticuchos - Grilled meat skewers with spicy sauce. Cost: $4-8
  • Lomo Saltado - Stir-fried beef with potatoes and peppers. Cost: $10-16
  • Aji de Gallina - Chicken in creamy walnut sauce. Cost: $8-14
  • Cevicheria Platter - Multiple seafood preparations. Cost: $15-25

Seafood & Coastal Specialties

  • Tiradito - Peruvian sashimi style fish. Cost: $10-18
  • Parihuela - Seafood stew, aromatic and rich. Cost: $12-20
  • Ceviche Mixto - Mixed seafood ceviche. Cost: $12-18
  • Mussels & Clams - Fresh and prepared multiple ways. Cost: $8-15

Andean & Traditional Dishes

  • Pachamanca - Lamb cooked in earth oven. Cost: $12-18
  • Choclo Corn - Large Peruvian corn, unique flavor. Cost: $3-6
  • Potatoes (Papa) - 4,000 varieties in Peru; numerous preparations. Cost: $2-5
  • Quinoa Dishes - Ancient grain in modern preparations. Cost: $6-12

Street Food & Snacks

  • Empanadas - Savory filled pastry. Cost: $1-2
  • Chifa - Peruvian-Chinese fusion, very popular. Cost: $4-7
  • Picarones - Fried dough with syrup. Cost: $1-2
  • Fresh Tropical Fruits - Lucuma, chirimoya, dragon fruit. Cost: $2-4

Best Places to Eat

  • Cevicheria (Lima) - Casual, authentic, seafood focused €8-20
  • Market Food Stalls (Central Lima) - Budget and authentic €3-8
  • Cusco Sacred Valley Markets - Local food, affordable €3-8
  • Fine Dining (Lima) - World-class restaurants €30-60 per person
  • Picanteria - Traditional Andean tavern, cheap and cheerful €4-8

Dining Tips

Lunch (12-2pm) is main meal with cheap set menus (€6-10). Dinner lighter. Always try ceviche in Lima at coastal area. Cusco market food cheaper than restaurants. Tap water generally not safe—buy bottled. Peruvian coffee excellent. Chicha (corn drink) local and refreshing.

Best Time to Visit Peru

Dry Season (May - September)

Weather: 10-25°C, sunny and clear. Crowds: PEAK season.

Highlights: Machu Picchu crystal clear visibility. Inca Trail hiking best. Cusco bright and sunny. Amazon slightly cooler but still humid. Dry weather means roads accessible.

Costs: HIGHEST. Hotels €50-120. Inca Trail permits hard to get. Book 4-6 months ahead.

Best for: Inca Trail hikers, Machu Picchu first-timers, photographers.

Wet Season (October - April)

Weather: 15-28°C, afternoon rain. November-March wettest. Crowds: Lower.

Highlights: Machu Picchu lush and green (some cloud cover). Amazon thriving with wildlife. Fewer tourists. Lower prices. Easier Inca Trail permits. Landscape vibrant.

Costs: LOWEST. Hotels €25-60. 50% cheaper than dry season. Inca Trail available without months advance booking.

Best for: Budget travelers, Amazon seekers, those avoiding crowds, flexible on Machu Picchu views.

Shoulder Seasons (March-April, September-October)

Weather: 15-23°C, mix of sun and occasional rain. Crowds: Moderate.

Highlights: Good compromise. April clearest of shoulder season. September-October dry starts. Prices reasonable.

Costs: Moderate. Hotels €35-80. Fair value balance.

Best for: Flexible travelers, balanced weather and prices, reasonable Inca Trail access.

Recommendation

Best overall: May-June or August-September (dry weather, clear Machu Picchu views, pre-peak crowds).

Budget travelers: October-November (cheaper, still good weather) or January-March (cheapest, rainy but manageable).

Inca Trail: May-September (best weather), but book 6+ months ahead.

Getting Around Peru

Trains to Machu Picchu

Cusco to Aguas Calientes (Machu Picchu town): 3.5-4 hours. PeruRail and Inca Rail operate scenic routes. Cost: $50-150 one-way depending on service. Book online. Views spectacular. Reserve seats in advance during high season.

Alternative: Inca Trail Trek - 4-day hike to Machu Picchu, $600-1,200 with guide (mandatory). Stunning but demanding. Need altitude acclimatization.

Domestic Flights

LATAM and Avianca connect Lima to Cusco, Iquitos (Amazon gateway). Cost: $80-200 one-way. Recommend for time efficiency (2 hours flight vs 24-30 hours by bus from Lima to Cusco).

Buses

Extensive network. Lima to Cusco: 24+ hours, $30-60. Overnight buses save accommodation. Companies: Cruz del Sur, Ormeno (quality). Comfortable long-distance buses have bathrooms and seats that recline.

Lima City Transport

  • Buses: Extensive network. Cost: PEN 1-2.50 (€0.25-0.65). Chaotic but goes everywhere.
  • Taxis: Negotiate before entering. Cost: $2-8 depending on distance
  • Uber: Available. Cost: €3-7 per ride
  • Walking: Miraflores and San Isidro neighborhoods walkable and safe

Sacred Valley Transport

Local buses connect villages (Ollantaytambo, Urubamba, Pisac). Cost: $1-3. Minibuses (colectivos) cheaper but crowded. Organized tours from Cusco convenient ($40-80).

Money-Saving Tips

  • Take overnight bus to Cusco (saves hotel night)
  • Book trains online 2 weeks ahead for discounts
  • Walk Cusco—city is compact and pedestrian-friendly
  • Use colectivos (minibuses) instead of tours in Sacred Valley

Travel Essentials

Budget Breakdown

  • Accommodation: $20-80/night (hostels $10-20, mid-range $30-60, hotels $80+)
  • Food: $10-30/day (street food $1-3, local restaurant $5-10, fine dining $20-40)
  • Trains: $50-150 Cusco-Machu Picchu, $80-200 flights Lima-Cusco
  • Activities: $20-100/day (Machu Picchu entry $50, Inca Trail $600-1,200, city tours $20-40)
  • Total daily budget: $28-50/day for budget travelers

Visa & Entry Requirements

For Indian Passport Holders: Tourist card upon arrival, valid 90 days. Automatic—no visa required. Bring: Passport (6+ months validity), return flight, proof of funds (PEN 400-500 or ~$150), hotel booking.

US/UK/Australia: Visa-free for up to 180 days (tourist card at arrival).

Currency: Peruvian Sol (PEN). 1 USD ≈ 3.8-4 PEN. ATMs widespread. Credit cards accepted in restaurants. Cash needed for markets and small vendors.

Getting a SIM: Buy from Movistar, Claro, or Entel at airport (PEN 20-50 for 8-10 days data).

Altitude Acclimatization

CRITICAL: Cusco is 3,400m, Machu Picchu at 2,400m. Altitude sickness common. Spend 2+ days in Lima (sea level) before Cusco. Drink coca tea, avoid alcohol first day. Breathe slowly. Acetazolamide (Diamox) medication available if needed. Serious symptoms: descend immediately.

Travel Insurance

Strongly recommended. Cost: $1-2/day. Covers medical, altitude-related issues, trip cancellations, lost luggage. Many travel claims from Peru (altitude, theft, accidents).
