Why Visit Maldives?

The Maldives is about 1,200 coral islands scattered across the Indian Ocean: turquoise lagoons, white sand and some of the best snorkelling and diving in the world. It's a short flight from India (Kochi, Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi and others) and one of the easiest international trips for Indians, with a free visa on arrival.

You can stay at a private-island resort (from overwater villas to more affordable beach rooms) or on a local island such as Maafushi or Thulusdhoo, where guesthouses cost a fraction of resort prices. Many travellers combine both.

Maldives Itineraries: 3, 5 or 7 Days

Pick 3, 5 or 7 days. Each longer plan builds on the shorter one: the first days stay the same and the extra days add more places. Costs are approximate per person per day; prices and timings change, so check before you book.

1

Arrive & settle into island life

Morning
Land at Velana International Airport in Malé. Your resort or guesthouse arranges the transfer: speedboat for nearby atolls, or seaplane or domestic flight for farther ones (seaplanes only fly in daylight).
Afternoon
Check in, swim in the lagoon and snorkel the house reef if your island has one. Rent fins and a mask on day one so you're ready.
Evening
Watch the sunset from the jetty, then dinner on the beach.
US$150–300/day
2

Snorkelling excursion & sandbank

Morning
Join a snorkelling trip to nearby reefs to see turtles, reef sharks and colourful fish. Use reef-safe sunscreen and never stand on coral.
Afternoon
Visit a sandbank, a strip of sand in the middle of the ocean, for a picnic and photos.
Evening
Take a sunset dolphin cruise; spinner dolphins are commonly seen in many atolls.
US$150–300/day
3

Water sports & spa

Morning
Try kayaking, paddleboarding or a glass-bottom boat. Beginners can do a supervised introductory dive.
Afternoon
Relax at the spa or by the pool during the hottest hours.
Evening
Go night fishing with the locals, a classic Maldivian experience, and have your catch cooked for dinner.
US$150–350/day
4

Manta rays or whale sharks

5-day plan
Morning
Join a marine excursion to look for manta rays (seasonal, at cleaning stations in several atolls) or whale sharks (South Ari Atoll is famous for year-round sightings). Go with an operator that follows responsible-viewing guidelines.
Afternoon
Rest and snorkel the house reef again; afternoons often bring calmer light for photos.
Evening
Stargaze from the beach. With little light pollution, the night sky is spectacular.
US$180–350/day
5

Local island visit

5-day plan
Morning
Take a boat or public ferry to a local island such as Maafushi, Thulusdhoo or Dhigurah to see everyday Maldivian life: mosques, schools, cafés and a designated "bikini beach" for tourists.
Afternoon
Try local short eats (hedhikaa) at a café. Dress modestly away from the bikini beach, and remember that alcohol is not served on local islands.
Evening
Return to your resort, or stay a night on the local island to save money.
US$100–200/day
6

Free day or island hopping

7-day plan
Morning
Have a slow morning: a floating breakfast, or another snorkel when the water is calm.
Afternoon
Book an island-hopping trip to a nearby resort or uninhabited island, or simply enjoy your villa.
Evening
A special final dinner on the beach or a sunset cruise.
US$150–300/day
7

Malé city & departure

7-day plan
Morning
Transfer back to Malé. If your flight is later, take a short taxi or walking tour of the capital.
Afternoon
See the coral-stone Old Friday Mosque (Hukuru Miskiy), the Malé fish market and the local market, and walk along the waterfront.
Evening
Head to Velana Airport (a short ferry or bridge ride from Malé) for your flight home.
US$80–150/day

Maldivian Food to Try

  • Mas huni — Shredded tuna with coconut, onion and chilli, eaten with roshi (flatbread) for breakfast.
  • Garudhiya — Clear, fragrant tuna broth served with rice, lime and chilli.
  • Hedhikaa — "Short eats" such as bajiya (samosa-like pastries), gulha (fish balls) and kavaabu (fritters), sold at local cafés.
  • Fihunu mas — Fish grilled with chilli and spices.
  • Bis keemiya — Pastry filled with egg, cabbage and tuna.
  • Fresh coconut and reef fish — Simple and excellent.

For Indian travellers: most resorts serve international buffets with Indian dishes and vegetarian options; tell your resort about dietary needs before you arrive. Food at resorts is expensive, so meal plans (half or full board) often save money.

Best Time to Visit Maldives

Dry season (November–April): sunny, calm seas and the best visibility for snorkelling. December to March is peak season, with the highest prices.

Wet season (May–October): more rain and wind, often in short bursts, with lower prices and good conditions for seeing manta rays and whale sharks in some atolls.

Temperature: warm all year (about 27–32°C), with sea temperatures to match.

Getting Around Maldives

Speedboats: the usual transfer to resorts in North and South Malé atolls, typically 20–90 minutes.

Seaplanes and domestic flights: needed for distant atolls. Seaplanes are scenic but expensive and only fly in daylight, so plan arrival times carefully.

Public ferries: very cheap ways to reach local islands from Malé, but with limited schedules and no Friday service on many routes. Shared speedboats are faster.

In Malé: the city is small and walkable; taxis are cheap. Velana Airport is on Hulhulé island, connected by bridge.

Travel Essentials

Visa for Indians: the Maldives gives a free 30-day visa on arrival to visitors of all nationalities, including Indians, if you have a passport valid for at least 6 months, a confirmed hotel or resort booking and a return or onward ticket. You must also submit the online IMUGA traveller declaration within 96 hours before arrival. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you travel.

Money: Maldivian rufiyaa (MVR), but resorts price everything in US dollars and cards are widely accepted. US$1 is roughly ₹85. Bills usually add a service charge, tax and a per-night green tax; check what your quote includes.

Daily budget (per person): local-island guesthouses and excursions can cost around US$100–200 a day, while mid-range resorts with meals are more like US$150–300 and luxury resorts far more.

SIM card: Dhiraagu and Ooredoo sell tourist SIMs at the airport; most resorts have Wi-Fi.

Plugs: type G (and D), 230V.

Local customs: the Maldives is a Muslim country. On local islands dress modestly away from bikini beaches and don't bring alcohol, which is only served at resorts and liveaboard boats. Importing alcohol or pork is not allowed.
