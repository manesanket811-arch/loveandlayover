Why Visit Japan?

Japan is a mesmerizing blend of ultra-modern technology and ancient tradition. From Tokyo's electric energy and neon-lit streets to Kyoto's serene temples and bamboo forests, Mount Fuji's iconic peak, and pristine beaches, Japan delivers unforgettable experiences. It's one of the most traveler-friendly countries globally with excellent public transit, incredible food, and a unique culture that feels both futuristic and timeless.

Whether you're exploring bustling city centers, quiet mountain villages, or relaxing in onsen (hot springs), Japan offers something for every traveler. The food alone—ramen, sushi, tempura, okonomiyaki—is worth the trip.

Japan Itineraries: 3, 5 or 7 Days

Pick 3, 5 or 7 days. Each longer plan builds on the shorter one: the first days stay the same and the extra days add more places. Costs are approximate per person per day; prices and timings change, so check before you book.

1

Arrival & Shibuya Crossing

Morning
Arrive at Narita or Haneda Airport. Take the Narita Express (N'EX) train to Tokyo Station (¥3,000, 60 mins). Check into your hotel in Shinjuku or Shibuya area. Rest and freshen up.
Afternoon
Explore Shibuya Crossing (world's busiest pedestrian crossing). Visit Hachiko statue. Walk through Takeshita Street (trendy shopping, packed but iconic). Grab street food: takoyaki or okonomiyaki (¥500–1,000).
Evening
Dinner at a ramen or izakaya restaurant (¥1,000–2,000). Explore neon-lit streets of Shinjuku at night. Experience Tokyo's electric nightlife or visit a karaoke bar.
¥5,000–8,000/day
2

Temples & Traditional Tokyo

Morning
Visit Senso-ji Temple in Asakusa (Tokyo's oldest temple, free). Walk through the traditional shopping street (Nakamise) filled with souvenirs. Breakfast at a local cafe or convenience store (¥500–1,000).
Afternoon
Head to Meiji Shrine (forested shrine, peaceful, free). Walk through Omotesando (Tokyo's upscale shopping street). Visit teamLab Borderless digital art museum (¥3,200) if interested in modern art.
Evening
Dinner at a conveyor belt sushi restaurant (¥1,500–2,500). Explore Harajuku's quirky fashion district or relax at a local onsen (¥1,500–3,000).
¥6,000–9,000/day
3

Modern Tokyo & Day Trip

Morning
Day trip to Mount Fuji by bullet train (Shinkansen) to Hakone or Kawaguchiko (¥8,000–12,000 round trip). Visit Lake Kawaguchi for Mount Fuji views. Hike around the lake or visit a local shrine.
Afternoon
Explore Hakone Open-Air Museum (¥1,500) or relax at an onsen with mountain views (¥2,000–5,000). Have lunch at a local restaurant with scenic views.
Evening
Return to Tokyo by Shinkansen. Dinner at your favorite spot or try a Japanese BBQ restaurant (¥3,000–5,000).
¥8,000–12,000/day (including transport)
4

Kyoto: Fushimi Inari & Gion

5-day plan
Morning
Take the Shinkansen from Tokyo to Kyoto (about 2 hours 15 minutes, roughly ¥14,000 one way). Store luggage in coin lockers or send it ahead with a luggage service.
Afternoon
Walk through the thousands of red torii gates at Fushimi Inari Taisha (free, open all day).
Evening
Stroll the lantern-lit lanes of Gion and Pontocho. Please don't photograph geiko or maiko without permission.
¥15,000–22,000/day (incl. Shinkansen)
5

Kyoto: temples & Arashiyama

5-day plan
Morning
Go early to the Arashiyama Bamboo Grove and Tenryu-ji Temple, before the crowds.
Afternoon
See Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion (about ¥500), and walk around Kiyomizu-dera and the old streets of Higashiyama.
Evening
Dinner at Nishiki Market or a vegetarian shojin ryori (Buddhist temple food) restaurant.
¥6,000–10,000/day
6

Nara day trip & Osaka

7-day plan
Morning
Train to Nara (about 45 minutes). Meet the bowing deer in Nara Park and see the giant Buddha at Todai-ji.
Afternoon
Continue to Osaka (about 45 minutes) and check in.
Evening
Eat your way through Dotonbori: takoyaki, okonomiyaki and the Glico running man sign.
¥6,000–10,000/day
7

Osaka Castle & departure

7-day plan
Morning
Visit Osaka Castle and its park, then snack at Kuromon Market.
Afternoon
Shop in Shinsaibashi or Umeda. Optional: Universal Studios Japan is in Osaka if you have an extra day.
Evening
Fly home from Kansai International Airport (about 1 hour by train). Booking an open-jaw ticket, into Tokyo and out of Osaka, saves backtracking.
¥5,000–9,000/day

Extend to Kyoto? Take the Shinkansen from Tokyo to Kyoto (2 hours, ¥13,320). Kyoto offers temples, geisha districts (Gion), bamboo forests, and traditional culture.

Japanese Food You Must Try

Japan's food culture is world-class. Here's what to eat:

  • Ramen — Noodles in broth. ¥800–1,500. Try tonkotsu (pork bone) or miso ramen.
  • Sushi & Sashimi — Fresh raw fish. ¥2,000–5,000. Go to Tsukiji outer market or a conveyor belt sushi bar.
  • Tempura — Fried seafood & vegetables. ¥1,500–3,000. Light, crispy, delicious.
  • Okonomiyaki — Savory pancake with cabbage, meat, and sauce. ¥800–1,200. Street food favorite.
  • Takoyaki — Octopus balls. ¥500–800. Perfect street snack.
  • Tonkatsu — Breaded pork cutlet. ¥1,500–2,500. Served with cabbage and rice.
  • Gyoza — Dumplings (pan-fried or steamed). ¥500–1,000. Usually 6 pieces.
  • Edamame — Boiled soybeans. ¥300–500. Perfect with beer.

Best Time to Visit

Spring (March–May): Cherry blossoms bloom (peak in early April). Perfect weather, mild temperatures. Peak tourist season—expect crowds and higher prices.

Fall (September–November): Cool, crisp weather. Fall foliage stunning. Comfortable for walking. Highly recommended.

Summer (June–August): Hot and humid. Typhoon season (June–July). Fewer tourists but physically exhausting.

Winter (December–February): Cold and dry (but beautiful). Some ski resorts operate. Lower prices, fewer tourists. Perfect if you enjoy cold weather.

Getting Around Japan

Japan Railways (JR): The backbone of Japan's transport. Shinkansen (bullet train) connects major cities. Fast, reliable, and punctual (average delay: 1 minute!). Single journey tickets or JR Pass for multiple trips.

Tokyo Metro: Excellent subway system in Tokyo. A single ride costs ¥150–220 depending on distance. Get a Suica card (rechargeable, ¥2,000 with ¥1,500 balance) from convenience stores or ticket machines.

Buses: Useful for exploring rural areas. Most accept Suica cards. Check local schedules before boarding.

Taxis: Expensive but abundant. Base fare ¥500, then ¥80 per 1 km. Use only if necessary or for short distances.

IC Cards: Suica (Tokyo area) and Icoca (Kyoto area) work on trains, buses, and at convenience stores. Highly convenient.

Travel Essentials

Visa for Indians: Indian passport holders need a visa for Japan; there is no visa on arrival. Most travellers apply for a single-entry short-stay tourist visa, which is now often issued as an eVISA, through the Japan Visa Application Centres (VFS Global) or an accredited travel agency, not directly at the embassy. Processing usually takes about 5 working days, so apply 3–4 weeks before you fly. Typical documents: passport valid for your trip, application form, a recent photo, flight and hotel bookings or an itinerary, recent bank statements, and ITR or employment proof. The permitted stay (up to 90 days) is shown on your visa. Rules as of October 2026; check the Embassy of Japan in India website for current requirements.

Money: Japanese Yen (¥). 1 USD = ~145–150 JPY (varies). ATMs at 7-Eleven and post offices accept international cards. Credit cards widely accepted in Tokyo, but carry cash for rural areas and small restaurants.

SIM Card: Buy at airport (Docomo, AU, SoftBank). 7-day tourist plans ¥3,000–4,500 with unlimited data. Extremely reliable coverage nationwide.

IC Card: Suica card essential for Tokyo and regional rail travel. ¥2,000 gets you ¥1,500 usable balance.

Apps: Google Maps (works perfectly), Tabelog (restaurant reviews), Hyperdia (train schedules), PayPay (mobile payment).

Language: English is spoken in major tourist areas but not widespread. Download Google Translate for real-time camera translation.

Accommodation: Hotels in Tokyo/Kyoto: ¥4,000–8,000/night for budget options. Capsule hotels: ¥2,000–4,000. Airbnb available but limited.

How much does a Japan trip cost per day?

Plan for roughly ¥6,000–10,000 per person per day (about ₹3,400–5,700) for a mid-range trip, covering accommodation, food, local transport and sightseeing. Prices are approximate as of October 2026.

How many days do you need in Japan?

3 days covers the highlights: Arrival & Shibuya Crossing, Temples & Traditional Tokyo and Modern Tokyo & Day Trip.
