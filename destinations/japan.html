Japan Food Guide

Japanese cuisine is an art form—from humble street food to Michelin-starred restaurants. Food is central to Japanese culture, and trying local dishes is essential to understanding the country.

Must-Try Japanese Dishes

  • Ramen (ラーメン) - Noodle soup with broth, egg, meat. Cost: ¥800-1,500
  • Sushi & Sashimi - Fresh fish over rice. Cost: ¥1,000-3,000 (conveyor belt) or ¥5,000+ (fine dining)
  • Tempura (天ぷら) - Battered and deep-fried seafood/vegetables. Cost: ¥1,500-3,000
  • Okonomiyaki (お好み焼き) - Savory Japanese pancake. Cost: ¥800-1,200
  • Tonkatsu (とんかつ) - Breaded pork cutlet. Cost: ¥1,500-2,500
  • Udon (うどん) - Thick wheat noodles in broth. Cost: ¥600-1,200

Street Food & Snacks

  • Takoyaki - Octopus balls from street vendors (¥300-500)
  • Yakitori - Grilled chicken skewers at izakayas (¥100-300 each)
  • Mochi - Sweet rice cakes (¥100-300)
  • Edamame - Steamed soybeans (¥300-600 as appetizer)

Best Places to Eat

  • Tsukiji Outer Market (Tokyo) - Fresh seafood, sushi, street food
  • Dotonbori (Osaka) - Famous street food district, takoyaki, okonomiyaki
  • Gion District (Kyoto) - Traditional kaiseki restaurants
  • Ramen Alley (Tokyo) - 35+ ramen shops in Shinjuku
  • Convenience Stores (7-11, Lawson) - Affordable, quality meals ¥500-1,500

Dining Tips

Remove shoes when entering traditional restaurants. Slurp noodles (it's polite!). Chopsticks rest called "hashioki" are provided. Tap water is safe and often free. Tipping is NOT expected in Japan.

Best Time to Visit Japan

Spring (March - May)

Weather: 10-20°C, mild and pleasant. Crowds: VERY HIGH (cherry blossom season).

Highlights: Cherry blossoms (sakura) bloom late March to early April—iconic and breathtaking. Mild weather perfect for walking. Gardens burst with color.

Costs: Peak pricing. Hotels 20-30% more expensive. Flights booked 2-3 months in advance.

Best for: First-time visitors who want the iconic Japan experience.

Summer (June - August)

Weather: 22-32°C, humid with monsoon rains in June. Crowds: Moderate to High.

Highlights: Summer festivals (matsuri), fireworks, Obon holiday celebrations. Green gardens at peak.

Costs: Mid-range. Schools on vacation, some hotels pricier mid-July to mid-August.

Best for: Festival enthusiasts, those comfortable with heat and humidity.

Autumn (September - November)

Weather: 15-25°C, crisp and clear—ideal. Crowds: High (fall foliage, good weather).

Highlights: Autumn foliage (kouyou) paints forests red and gold. Perfect weather for hiking, temples, gardens. Typhoon season ends by October.

Costs: High. Peak season after cherry blossoms. Book 2 months ahead.

Best for: Nature lovers, photographers, best weather overall.

Winter (December - February)

Weather: 0-10°C, dry and clear. Rare snow except Hokkaido. Crowds: Low (except New Year's).

Highlights: Christmas illuminations (not a holiday but heavily celebrated), ski season in Hokkaido, temples and gardens peaceful without crowds, winter festivals.

Costs: LOWEST of the year. 30-40% cheaper than peak seasons. Great value.

Best for: Budget travelers, those avoiding crowds, ski enthusiasts.

Recommendation

Best overall: Late September to November (autumn foliage, mild weather, fewer crowds than spring).

Budget travelers: January-February (lowest prices, clear weather).

First-timers: March-April (cherry blossoms, despite crowds).

Japan Neighborhoods & Regions

Tokyo: The Modern Capital

Tokyo is Japan's electric heartbeat—a sprawling metropolis where ancient temples coexist with neon-lit skyscrapers. As the gateway for most visitors, Tokyo demands at least 2-3 days to experience its chaos, culture, and culinary excellence.

What makes it special: Endless neighborhoods, world-class food, cutting-edge technology, temples hidden between office buildings.

Best for: First-time Japan visitors, foodies, urban explorers, shopaholics.

Must-see attractions: Shibuya Crossing, Senso-ji Temple, teamLab Borderless, Tsukiji Market, Meiji Shrine.

Budget: ¥6,000-10,000/day (accommodation ¥4,000-8,000, meals ¥1,500-3,000).

Local vibe: Chaotic energy, fast-paced, incredibly clean, highly efficient, respectful despite crowds.

Getting there: Narita or Haneda Airport to central Tokyo via train (¥3,000-4,000, 60-90 mins). Internal transport via subway is quick (¥170-320 per ride).

Best time to visit: Late September-November (autumn foliage, mild weather) or January-February (low crowds, cheap prices).

Kyoto: The Ancient Temple City

Kyoto is where Japan's soul lives. With over 2,000 temples and shrines, traditional geisha districts, bamboo groves, and formal gardens, Kyoto feels like stepping into feudal Japan. It's Japan's cultural treasure chest and worth 2-3 days minimum.

What makes it special: 2,000+ temples, geisha culture, authentic Japanese aesthetics, peaceful atmosphere away from urban chaos.

Best for: Culture enthusiasts, photographers, spiritual seekers, those wanting traditional Japan.

Must-see attractions: Fushimi Inari (10,000 vermillion gates), Arashiyama Bamboo Grove, Gion District, Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion), Kiyomizu-dera Temple.

Budget: ¥5,000-8,000/day (accommodation ¥3,500-6,000, meals ¥1,000-2,500).

Local vibe: Serene, traditional, slower-paced, respectful, deeply spiritual, less tourist infrastructure than Tokyo.

Getting there: From Tokyo via Shinkansen (2h 15min, ¥13,320). Buses and trains cover the city—buy a day pass (¥1,100) for unlimited rides.

Best time to visit: March-April (cherry blossoms), September-November (autumn foliage, cool weather). Avoid summer (humid, hot).

Osaka: The Food Capital & Gateway

Osaka is Japan's fun-loving younger sibling—less formal than Kyoto, more relaxed than Tokyo. Known for incredible street food, vibrant nightlife, and as a gateway to nearby Kobe and Himeji Castle, Osaka deserves 1-2 days in any Japan itinerary.

What makes it special: Best street food in Japan, energetic nightlife, Okonomiyaki and takoyaki capital, friendly locals, excellent nightlife.

Best for: Foodies, nightlife enthusiasts, those wanting authentic local vibes, travelers heading to Kobe/Himeji.

Must-see attractions: Dotonbori Street Food District, Osaka Castle, Shinsekai Market, Shinsaibashi Shopping Street, day trip to Himeji Castle.

Budget: ¥4,500-7,000/day (accommodation ¥3,000-5,500, meals ¥1,000-2,000, meals cheapest of major cities).

Local vibe: Friendly, casual, energetic, less touristy than Tokyo, genuinely fun atmosphere.

Getting there: From Kyoto via train (75 mins, ¥3,000). From Tokyo via Shinkansen (2h 30min, ¥13,870). Subway connects all neighborhoods.

Best time to visit: September-November (perfect weather, fewer crowds). Spring also good but more crowded and expensive.

Mount Fuji & Hakone: Adventure & Hot Springs

Mount Fuji is Japan's most iconic image and a sacred peak. Whether hiking to the summit, enjoying hot springs in nearby Hakone, or viewing from Tokyo, Mount Fuji's presence is unforgettable. This region offers outdoor adventure, natural beauty, and traditional onsen (hot spring) experiences.

What makes it special: Japan's highest and most sacred mountain, hot spring resorts, alpine lakes, hiking trails, views across the Kanto Plain.

Best for: Hikers, nature lovers, adventure seekers, those wanting outdoor experiences, couples seeking romantic getaways.

Must-see attractions: Mount Fuji Summit (hike), Hakone Spa resorts, Lake Ashi (pirate ship cruises), Owakudani Valley, Hakone Open-Air Museum.

Budget: ¥5,000-8,000/day (accommodation with onsen ¥6,000-10,000, meals ¥1,500-2,500).

Local vibe: Peaceful, nature-focused, traditional inn experience, quiet mountain towns, meditative atmosphere.

Getting there: From Tokyo via train to Hakone (1.5 hours). Mount Fuji base accessible from Tokyo (2-3 hours). Day trips possible from Tokyo, but 1-2 nights recommended for onsen experience.

Best time to visit: July-September for hiking (mountain accessible only July-mid-September due to snow). September-November for autumn foliage and onsen without summer crowds.

Getting Around Japan

Japan Railways (JR) - The Backbone

Shinkansen (Bullet Train) - The fastest way between cities. Tokyo to Kyoto in 2h 15min. Cost: ¥13,320 one-way. JR Pass (7-day ¥29,650) covers unlimited train travel nationwide—worthwhile if doing 3+ long journeys.

Local Trains - Extensive network in every city. Cheap (¥150-300 per ride). Get a rechargeable IC card (Suica/Pasmo) at any station—tap to ride trains, buses, convenience stores.

Public Transport in Cities

  • Tokyo: Subway is fastest (2-3min between stations). Cost: ¥170-320 per ride. Buy a Suica card.
  • Kyoto: Buses cover most temples. Cost: ¥230 per ride. Tourist bus pass (¥1,100/day) covers unlimited rides.
  • Osaka: Subway + JR loop line. Integrated IC card system.

Taxis

Expensive but convenient. Tokyo flag drop: ¥500 + ¥80-100 per km. Not recommended for long distances. Uber/Grab available in Tokyo/Kyoto but often cost more than trains.

Buses

Cheap and extensive. Cost: ¥100-300. Slower than trains but reach areas trains don't. Many have English signage in tourist areas.

Car Rental

International Driving Permit required. Road signs in English on major routes. Gas cheaper than taxis (¥130/liter). Useful only for rural areas (Hakone, Japanese Alps). Parking expensive in cities (¥2,000-5,000/day).

Money-Saving Tips

  • JR Pass breaks even at 2-3 long journeys
  • IC cards (Suica) save 5-10% vs single tickets
  • Get a rail pass if doing Tokyo → Kyoto → Osaka
  • Walk—Tokyo and Kyoto are pedestrian-friendly

Travel Essentials

Budget Breakdown

  • Accommodation: ¥3,000-8,000/night (hostels ¥2,500, mid-range ¥5,000, hotels ¥10,000+)
  • Food: ¥2,000-5,000/day (budget eats ¥800/meal, fine dining ¥5,000+)
  • JR Pass: ¥7,650-29,650 (multi-day trains, buy only if taking 2+ long journeys)
  • Activities: ¥1,000-3,000/day (temples free-¥1,000, museums ¥1,000-2,000)
  • Total daily budget: ¥5,000-8,000 (~$35-55 USD) for budget travelers

Visa & Entry Requirements

For Indian Passport Holders: 90-day tourist visa exemption. Automatic upon arrival—no visa needed. Bring: Passport (valid 6+ months), return flight ticket, proof of funds (¥200,000 or credit card).

Vaccinations: None required. Check latest COVID guidelines before travel.

Currency: Japanese Yen (¥). Exchange rates: 1 USD ≈ ¥130-140. No forex needed—ATMs at 7-11 accept Visa/Mastercard.

Getting a SIM: Buy pocket Wi-Fi (¥500/day) or SIM card at airport (¥2,000-4,000 for 8-10 days unlimited data).

Travel Insurance

Highly recommended. Cost: ¥1,500-3,000 for 7-10 days. Covers medical emergencies, trip cancellations, lost luggage.
