Why Visit Qatar?

Qatar makes a perfect short break or stopover from India: a dramatic skyline on the Corniche, world-class museums, a traditional souq, and a desert where dunes roll straight into the sea. Flights take about 3½–4½ hours from most Indian cities, and Indians can visit visa-free for short stays.

It's calmer and more compact than Dubai. Spend most of your time in Doha, with a day in the desert at Khor Al Udaid (the Inland Sea) and longer trips adding the north coast and Al Zubarah, a UNESCO site.

Qatar Itineraries: 3, 5 or 7 Days

Pick 3, 5 or 7 days. Each longer plan builds on the shorter one: the first days stay the same and the extra days add more places. Costs are approximate per person per day; prices and timings change, so check before you book.

1

Corniche & Museum of Islamic Art

Morning
Arrive at Hamad International Airport and take the metro into Doha.
Afternoon
Visit the Museum of Islamic Art (designed by I. M. Pei) and its park with skyline views.
Evening
Wander Souq Waqif: spices, perfumes, the falcon souq and dinner in the lanes.
QAR 350–600/day
2

National Museum & The Pearl

Morning
See the National Museum of Qatar, shaped like a desert rose.
Afternoon
Walk the restored Msheireb downtown, then the waterfront Corniche; take a traditional dhow ride at sunset.
Evening
Evening at The Pearl and the colourful Qanat Quartier.
QAR 400–650/day
3

Desert safari & the Inland Sea

Morning
Join a 4×4 desert safari south of Doha with dune bashing at Sealine.
Afternoon
Reach Khor Al Udaid, where the dunes meet the sea, a UNESCO-listed nature reserve. Try sandboarding or a camel ride.
Evening
Return to Doha, or stay at a desert camp.
QAR 450–750/day (incl. safari)
4

Katara & Lusail

5-day plan
Morning
Explore Katara Cultural Village: the amphitheatre, mosques and beach.
Afternoon
Visit Lusail: the World Cup stadium area and the Place Vendôme mall.
Evening
Evening on Lusail Boulevard or Lusail Marina.
QAR 400–650/day
5

Al Zubarah & the north

5-day plan
Morning
Drive north to the Al Zubarah Fort and archaeological site (UNESCO).
Afternoon

Evening
Seafood or Kerala dinner back in Doha.
QAR 400–650/day

Beach or museum day

Food to Try in Qatar

  • Machboos — Spiced rice with chicken, lamb or fish, the national dish.
  • Harees — Slow-cooked wheat and meat porridge, popular during Ramadan.
  • Luqaimat — Crispy dumplings with date syrup.
  • Balaleet — Sweet saffron vermicelli topped with an omelette, eaten at breakfast.
  • Karak chai — Strong, sweet spiced tea, a favourite brought from the subcontinent.
  • Fresh seafood and mezze — Grilled hammour, hummus, moutabal and fresh bread.

For Indian travellers: Qatar is very easy for Indian travellers: there are hundreds of Indian restaurants (Kerala, North Indian, South Indian and pure-veg) in Doha.

Best Time to Visit Qatar

Winter (November–March): the best time, with warm, sunny days around 20–28°C, perfect for the desert and outdoor festivals.

April and October: getting hot but still manageable in the mornings and evenings.

Summer (May–September): extremely hot, often 40–45°C with high humidity; plan indoor sights and visit the desert only at dawn or dusk.

Ramadan: dates change each year; eating in public in the daytime is restricted, but evenings are festive.

Getting Around Qatar

Flights from India: many direct flights connect Doha with Indian cities, taking about 3½–4½ hours. It's also a popular stopover on the way to Europe.

In Doha: the clean, modern Doha Metro (Red, Gold and Green lines) is cheap and connects the airport, Souq Waqif, Katara and Lusail. Get a Travel Card.

Taxis: Karwa taxis and Uber are easy and fairly priced.

Desert and north: book a licensed tour, or rent a car (with an International Driving Permit) for Zubarah and the mangroves.

Travel Essentials

Entry for Indians: Indian passport holders can usually enter Qatar visa-free for up to 30 days. You need a passport valid for at least 6 months, a confirmed return ticket and, in the past, a hotel booking made through the official Discover Qatar website. Requirements such as hotel bookings, insurance and extensions have changed over time, so check the Ministry of Interior and Hayya websites before you travel. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you travel.

Money: Qatari riyal (QAR). 1 QAR is about ₹23. Cards are accepted almost everywhere.

Daily budget (per person): a mid-range hotel share, food, transport and sightseeing come to about QAR 400–700 a day, plus a desert safari.

SIM card: Ooredoo and Vodafone Qatar sell tourist SIMs and eSIMs at the airport.

Plugs: type G (UK three-pin), 240V. Bring an adapter for Indian plugs.

Etiquette and safety: Qatar is very safe. Cover shoulders and knees in souqs, museums and malls, and don't drink alcohol in public (only in licensed hotels). Public displays of affection are frowned upon.

Qatar FAQs

How much does a Qatar trip cost per day?

Plan for roughly QAR 400–700 per person per day (about ₹9,200–16,000) for a mid-range trip, covering accommodation, food, local transport and sightseeing. Prices are approximate as of October 2026.

How many days do you need in Qatar?

3 days covers the highlights: Corniche & Museum of Islamic Art, National Museum & The Pearl and Desert safari & the Inland Sea. With 5 days you can add Katara & Lusail and Al Zubarah & the north, and a full week adds Beach or museum day and Shopping.
