Why Visit Malaysia?

Malaysia packs a lot into one easy, affordable trip: the glittering skyline of Kuala Lumpur, Hindu cave temples, cool misty highlands, colonial Malacca, the street art and hawker food of Penang, and the duty-free beaches of Langkawi. Malay, Chinese and Indian cultures live side by side, so the food is some of the best in Asia, and Indian travellers will find dosa, banana leaf rice and Tamil spoken all over the country.

It’s also one of the simplest international trips from India: short flights, visa-free entry for Indians, English widely spoken and prices that stretch a rupee budget. From Singapore, where we live, it’s a quick flight, a bus ride or even a train across the causeway.

Free 5-Day Malaysia Itinerary

Five days is the sweet spot for a first trip: Kuala Lumpur, Batu Caves, Genting Highlands and Malacca, all from one hotel base in KL. Costs are per person and approximate.

▶ Plan with our videos: where we’ve filmed a day, you’ll find a link to the vlog under it. Seeing the place first makes timing and tickets much easier to plan. See all our Malaysia videos.

1 Arrive in Kuala Lumpur & the Petronas Towers

Morning
Land at KLIA or KLIA2 and take the KLIA Ekspres train to KL Sentral (about 30 minutes, around RM55). Buy a Touch ’n Go card at the station for the LRT, MRT and monorail. Check in around Bukit Bintang or KLCC.
Afternoon
Visit the Petronas Twin Towers. Skybridge and observation deck tickets sell out, so book a time slot online in advance. Walk through KLCC Park and cool off in Suria KLCC mall.
Evening
Watch the towers light up from the KLCC Park lake (the fountain show runs in the evening), then head to Jalan Alor for satay, grilled stingray, char kway teow and fresh fruit (RM10–30 per dish).
RM250–400/day

2 Batu Caves, Merdeka Square & Brickfields

Early Morning
Take the KTM Komuter train to Batu Caves station and arrive by 8 AM, before the heat and crowds. Climb the 272 rainbow steps past the giant golden Lord Murugan statue to the Temple Cave. Entry to the main cave is free; cover shoulders and knees (sarongs can be rented at the bottom).
Afternoon
Back in the city, walk around Merdeka Square, the Sultan Abdul Samad Building and the colonial-era Kuala Lumpur City Gallery, then browse Central Market for batik and souvenirs.
Evening
Dinner in Brickfields, KL’s Little India: banana leaf rice, dosa and filter coffee feel like home. Finish with a teh tarik at a 24-hour mamak stall.
RM150–250/day

3 Genting Highlands & the Awana SkyWay

Morning
Take a bus from KL Sentral or TBS to Awana station (about 1 hour), or a Grab. Ride the Awana SkyWay cable car over the rainforest. Carry a light jacket: the hilltop is 15–20°C cooler than KL.
Afternoon
Hop off at Chin Swee station for the Chin Swee Caves Temple and its 9-storey pagoda, then continue up to SkyAvenue at the peak for food, shopping and Genting SkyWorlds theme park if you want rides.
Evening
Shop the Genting Highlands Premium Outlets at Awana before heading back down to KL. Check SkyWay hours and ticket rates on the Resorts World Genting website before you go.

RM200–400/day

4 Day trip to Malacca (Melaka)

Morning
Bus from TBS to Melaka Sentral (about 2 hours, roughly RM15–25), then a short Grab into the old town. Start at the red Christ Church and Dutch Square and climb St Paul’s Hill.
Afternoon
Lunch on chicken rice balls and Nyonya laksa, then walk the Melaka River and the Baba & Nyonya Heritage Museum. Cendol is the perfect fix for the afternoon heat.
Evening
If it’s Friday to Sunday, stay for the Jonker Street Night Market before the bus back to KL (or stay overnight in a heritage hotel).
RM150–250/day

5 Putrajaya & departure

Morning
Visit Putrajaya, Malaysia’s planned administrative capital, about 30 minutes from KL and on the way to the airport. The pink-domed Putra Mosque lends robes to visitors outside prayer times.
Afternoon
Last-minute shopping at Pavilion KL or Petaling Street, then the KLIA Ekspres to the airport. Allow at least 3 hours before an international flight.
RM150–250/day

Got 8–10 Days? Add Penang & Langkawi

Penang (2–3 days)

Take the ETS train from KL Sentral to Butterworth (about 4 hours) and the short ferry across to George Town, a UNESCO World Heritage city. Hunt for the famous street-art murals on Armenian Street, ride the funicular up Penang Hill, visit the Kek Lok Si Temple and eat your way through Gurney Drive and the clan jetties. Penang’s Little India is great for nasi kandar and Indian sweets.

Langkawi (2–3 days)

Fly from Penang or KL (about an hour) or take the ferry. Ride the SkyCab cable car to the Langkawi Sky Bridge, take a mangrove and island-hopping tour, and relax on Cenang or Tanjung Rhu beach. Langkawi is a duty-free island, so chocolates and drinks are noticeably cheaper than on the mainland.

East coast islands: the Perhentian and Redang islands have some of Malaysia’s clearest water, but resorts mostly close during the November–March monsoon. Go between April and September.

Malaysian Food You Must Try

  • Nasi Lemak — Coconut rice with sambal, peanuts, anchovies and egg; the national dish. RM5–15.
  • Roti Canai — Flaky flatbread with dal or curry from any mamak stall. RM2–5.
  • Char Kway Teow — Wok-fried flat noodles with prawns and cockles; best in Penang. RM8–15.
  • Penang Asam Laksa — Tangy, spicy fish-based noodle soup. RM7–12.
  • Satay — Chicken or beef skewers with peanut sauce, sold by the stick. RM1–2 each.
  • Banana Leaf Rice — South Indian rice meal with vegetables, curries and papadum; great for vegetarians. RM10–20.
  • Nasi Kandar — Penang’s Indian-Muslim rice with mixed curries poured over. RM10–20.
  • Teh Tarik & Cendol — “Pulled” milk tea, and shaved ice with coconut milk and palm sugar. RM2–8.

Vegetarian tip: look for Indian banana leaf restaurants and Chinese vegetarian (“su shi”) stalls. Many Malay dishes contain belacan (shrimp paste) even when they look vegetarian, so ask.

Best Time to Visit

West coast (KL, Penang, Langkawi, Malacca): December to April is the driest and most comfortable stretch, with January to March the best for Langkawi beaches. Expect short afternoon showers year-round.

East coast islands (Perhentian, Redang, Tioman): April to September. The northeast monsoon from November to March brings rough seas and many resort closures.

Weather: hot and humid all year (around 27–33°C), except Genting and the Cameron Highlands, which are much cooler. Festivals like Deepavali, Thaipusam (huge at Batu Caves) and Chinese New Year are spectacular but book up early.

Getting Around Malaysia

Airport to city: the KLIA Ekspres train runs from both KLIA terminals to KL Sentral in about 30 minutes. Airport buses are cheaper but slower.

Kuala Lumpur: LRT, MRT, monorail and KTM Komuter trains cover most sights. Use a Touch ’n Go card (or contactless bank card on many lines). Grab is cheap and reliable for everything else.

Between cities: ETS electric trains link KL with Penang (Butterworth) and Ipoh. Long-distance buses from TBS terminal are frequent and cheap. AirAsia, Malaysia Airlines and Batik Air fly to Penang, Langkawi and Borneo.

From Singapore: (full comparison in our Singapore to Malaysia by bus or train guide) flights to KL take about an hour; coaches take 5–6 hours depending on the causeway; the KTM Shuttle Tebrau train crosses to Johor Bahru in about 5 minutes, with onward trains and buses north.

From India: direct flights to Kuala Lumpur from Chennai, Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi, Kochi, Hyderabad and other cities, usually 4–6 hours.

Travel Essentials

Visa for Indians: visa-free entry for up to 30 days for tourism (extended to 31 December 2026). Fill in the free Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) online within 3 days before arrival. Carry a return ticket and hotel booking. Always check the latest rules before you fly.

Money: Malaysian Ringgit (RM / MYR). RM1 is roughly ₹19–20. Cards work in malls, hotels and many restaurants; carry cash for hawker stalls, markets and Batu Caves. Money changers in malls often beat airport rates.

SIM card: CelcomDigi, Maxis (Hotlink) and U Mobile sell tourist prepaid SIMs at the airport, typically RM20–50 for a week or more of data. eSIMs work well too.

Plugs: Type G (three square pins, UK-style), 240V. Bring an adapter for Indian plugs.

Apps: Grab (rides and food), Touch ’n Go eWallet, Google Maps, MyRapid (KL transit) and Klook for attraction tickets.

Accommodation: hostels RM50–100, mid-range hotels RM150–300, and good-value 4–5 star hotels from around RM350 a night in KL.

Cultural tips: Malaysia is a Muslim-majority country. Dress modestly at mosques and temples (robes and sarongs are usually provided), remove shoes before entering, and use your right hand to give and receive. Public displays of affection are frowned upon.

Malaysia FAQs

Do Indians need a visa for Malaysia?

Indian passport holders can currently visit Malaysia visa-free for up to 30 days for tourism; the scheme has been extended to 31 December 2026. You still need to fill in the free Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) within 3 days before arrival. Rules change, so check the Malaysian immigration website before you fly.

How many days are enough for Malaysia?

Five days covers Kuala Lumpur, Batu Caves, Genting Highlands and a Malacca day trip. With 8 to 10 days you can add Penang and Langkawi.

How much does a Malaysia trip cost per day?

Plan for roughly RM200 to RM400 per person per day for a mid-range trip covering a hotel share, food, local transport and one paid attraction. Hawker food keeps costs low; alcohol is the most expensive item outside Langkawi.

Is Genting Highlands worth it?

Yes, for a day trip from Kuala Lumpur. The Awana SkyWay cable car ride over the rainforest, the Chin Swee Caves Temple and the cooler mountain air are the highlights. We filmed the whole cable car journey on our channel.

Is vegetarian and Indian food easy to find in Malaysia?

Very. Brickfields (Little India) in Kuala Lumpur and Penang’s Little India have banana leaf rice, dosa and thali, and mamak stalls across the country serve roti canai, thosai and teh tarik.

Prices and rules are approximate as of October 2026. Please check official websites before you book.
