Why Visit France?

France is the world's most visited country for good reason. Paris offers iconic landmarks and world-class museums. Provence captivates with lavender fields and medieval villages. The French Riviera dazzles with coastal beauty and glamour. Perfect for 3-10 day trips with excellent trains, exquisite food and wine, and layers of art, history, and culture.

France Food Guide

French cuisine is UNESCO-recognized for its artistry and technique. Every region celebrates local specialties, from Breton seafood to Burgundy wines to Alpine cheeses. Dining in France is not merely eating—it's a cultural ritual steeped in tradition, family, and the seasons. Even modest neighborhood bistros serve meticulously prepared food.

Must-Try French Dishes

  • Croissants (Croissant au Beurre) - Buttery, flaky pastry for breakfast. Cost: €1.50-3 at bakeries
  • Baguettes - Traditional French bread, best within hours of baking. Cost: €0.80-1.50
  • Coq au Vin - Chicken braised in red wine with mushrooms and onions. Cost: €15-22 in restaurants
  • French Cheese - Camembert, Roquefort, Brie, Comté. Cost: €3-8 per wedge at markets
  • Bouillabaisse - Provençal fish stew from Marseille. Cost: €20-30
  • Escargots de Bourgogne - Snails with garlic butter. Cost: €12-18 (specialty)

Regional Foods & Wine Regions

  • Burgundy Wines - Pinot Noir and Chardonnay. Wine tastings: €5-15/glass in wine bars, €30-50 at estates
  • Bordeaux Wines - Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot. Similar tasting prices
  • Champagne - Sparkling wine from Reims/Épernay. Tastings: €10-20/glass, €40-80 per bottle in shops
  • Foie Gras - Luxury delicacy, expensive (€20-40+ per serving at fine dining)
  • Ratatouille - Provençal vegetable stew (€8-14 in Provence)
  • Cassoulet - Bean stew with duck, Occitane specialty (€12-18)

Street Food & Café Culture

  • Pain au Chocolat - Chocolate-filled pastry (€2-3)
  • Crêpes - Sweet or savory thin pancakes (€4-8)
  • Café au Lait - Coffee with hot milk (€2-4 at cafes, €1 at bars)
  • Macaron - Colorful almond meringue cookie from Ladurée or local patisseries (€2-4)
  • Choux à la Crème - Cream-filled pastry (€2-3)

Best Places to Eat

  • Parisian Bistros - Cozy neighborhood restaurants with set menus. Cost: €15-25 for lunch, €25-40 dinner
  • Marché d'Aligre (Paris) - Open-air market with street food, produce, vintage goods. Budget-friendly meals available
  • Provence Markets - Avignon, Aix-en-Provence, Arles sell local produce, cheese, olive oil
  • Champagne Houses - Tour Moët & Chandon, Veuve Clicquot with tastings (€15-40)
  • Street Crêpe Stands - Paris, Lyon, Strasbourg (€4-7 for meal crêpes)

Dining Tips

Lunch is served 12-2pm, dinner 7:30-10pm. Many restaurants close on Sundays/Mondays. Bread and water are always free. Tipping is not expected (service included), but 5-10% appreciated for exceptional service. Espresso (café) is short and strong. "Café au lait" is larger with milk. Never order cappuccino after 11am. French people linger over meals—no rushing. Wine by the glass (verre): €4-8.

Best Time to Visit France

Spring (March - May)

Weather: 10-18°C, mild with occasional rain. Crowds: Moderate (Easter peak, but less than summer).

Highlights: Lavender begins blooming in Provence (peak in July), gardens burst with color. Easter celebrations. Longer daylight for sightseeing.

Costs: Mid-range pricing. Hotels 10-20% more than winter. Book 6-8 weeks ahead.

Best for: Flower lovers, those avoiding summer crowds.

Summer (June - August)

Weather: 20-28°C, warm and sunny. Crowds: EXTREMELY HIGH (peak tourism).

Highlights: Perfect beach weather on Côte d'Azur. Lavender in full bloom. Outdoor festivals, concerts, markets. Long days (sunset at 10pm in Paris).

Costs: HIGHEST prices of the year. Hotels 40-50% more expensive. Book 2-3 months ahead.

Best for: Beach enthusiasts, festival-goers. Avoid if you prefer fewer crowds.

Autumn (September - October)

Weather: 12-22°C, crisp and clear—excellent. Crowds: Lower than summer (school resumes).

Highlights: Fall foliage in Loire Valley. Harvest season with wine tastings. Still warm enough for Riviera. Clear skies perfect for sightseeing.

Costs: High but less than summer. Book 1 month ahead.

Best for: Best overall choice—excellent weather, fewer crowds, lower prices.

Winter (November - February)

Weather: 2-10°C, cool and rainy. Crowds: Low (except Christmas/New Year).

Highlights: Christmas markets and lights in Paris, Strasbourg. Fewer tourists, authentic experiences. Ski season in French Alps. Winter sales in January.

Costs: LOWEST prices, 30-40% cheaper than summer. Great value for budget travelers.

Best for: Budget travelers, museum enthusiasts (no lines), Christmas lovers.

Monthly Cost Comparison

Most Expensive: July-August. Moderately Expensive: April-May, June, September-October. Cheapest: November-February. Budget note: December (except Christmas week) and February offer excellent value.

Festival Calendar

  • Cannes Film Festival (May) - Glamorous cinema event on Côte d'Azur
  • Avignon Festival (July) - Theatre and arts in Provence
  • Strasbourg Christmas Market (November-December) - Europe's oldest and largest
  • Les Plages Électroniques (August) - Beach electronic music festival

Recommendation

Best overall: September-October (perfect weather, lower crowds, good prices). Budget travelers: November-February (lowest prices, Christmas markets). First-timers: May-June (spring beauty, manageable crowds).

Getting Around France

SNCF (French Railways) - Backbone of Transport

TGV (High-Speed Trains) - France's pride. Paris to Lyon: 2 hours, €25-70 one-way. Paris to Marseille: 3.5 hours, €40-100. Book online via SNCF.com or Trainline.eu for 30-50% discounts. Travel off-peak (midweek, morning) for cheaper fares.

Regional Trains - Slower but 40-60% cheaper. Useful for exploring Provence, Loire Valley, Burgundy. Cost: €15-40 depending on distance.

Rail Pass Options: France Rail Pass (3-6 days unlimited): €280-450. Only worth it if taking 4+ long journeys.

Paris Metro & City Transport

  • Paris Metro: 16 lines, clean and efficient. Single ride: €2.15. 10-ride carnet: €16.90. Day pass: €8. Best option: Navigo Easy card (rechargeable, €2, then load credit)
  • Buses: Same ticketing as metro. Slower but scenic routes available
  • Walking: Paris is very walkable. Many neighborhoods (Marais, Latin Quarter) best explored on foot

Long-Distance Buses

FlixBus, Blablacar, and regional carriers offer cheap alternatives to trains. Cost: €10-30 for longer routes. Slower (6-8 hours Paris-Provence), but budget-friendly. Useful for small towns trains don't serve.

Regional Transport

  • Lyon: Metro + tram system. Day pass: €7
  • Marseille: Metro + buses. Day pass: €6
  • Provence Towns: Avignon, Arles, Aix-en-Provence have local buses (€1-2 per ride)
  • Côte d'Azur: Trains and buses connect Nice, Cannes, Monaco. Single ride: €3-5

Car Rental

International Driving Permit required. Useful for rural Provence, Loire Valley, Burgundy. Cost: €30-60/day. Fuel expensive (€1.50+/liter). Tolls on highways: €10-30 depending on distance. Road signs in English on main routes. Parking expensive in cities (€15-30/day).

Money-Saving Tips

  • Book TGV trains 4-6 weeks ahead for 50% discount
  • Travel Tuesday-Thursday for 30% cheaper fares
  • Night trains available (Paris to Provence overnight)
  • Paris: Get a 3-day Museum Pass (€72, includes 60+ museums like Louvre and Versailles, but does NOT include metro transit)
  • Regional trains cost 60% less than TGV for scenic routes

Travel Essentials

Budget Breakdown

  • Accommodation: €30-70/night (hostels €20-35, mid-range €50-70, hotels €100-150+)
  • Food: €25-50/day (street food €5-8/meal, bistros €12-20, fine dining €30+)
  • Trains: €15-50/journey (regional), €25-80 (TGV high-speed)
  • Activities: €10-30/day (museum entry €8-15, metro day pass €8)
  • Total daily budget: €60-100 (~$65-109 USD) for budget travelers

Visa & Entry Requirements

For Indian Passport Holders: France is part of the Schengen Area. Indian nationals need a Schengen visa. Tourist visas valid for 90 days within a 180-day period. Apply at French embassy/consulate in India 4-6 weeks before travel. Documents: Passport (valid 6+ months), proof of funds (€40-50/day), accommodation bookings, return flight, and travel insurance (mandatory for Schengen). Official source: French Embassy India - Visa Information Last verified: July 2026.

For EU/EEA Citizens: No visa needed. Just bring passport or national ID card.

Travel Insurance: Required for Schengen visa. Cost: €50-120 for 7-10 days. Must be purchased before visa application.

Currency & Money

Currency: Euro (€). Exchange rates: 1 USD ≈ €0.92-0.95. ATMs widely available. Card payments accepted almost everywhere.

Withdrawals: ATMs at banks, train stations, airports. Fees minimal (€1-3). Visa/Mastercard work. Avoid exchanging money at airport or street changers (bad rates).

Payments: Card accepted in restaurants, shops, museums. Small markets and cafes may prefer cash. Carry €50-100 for small vendors.

SIM Cards & Mobile

Local SIM Options: Buy at airport or Orange/Bouygues stores. Cost: €15-25 with 10GB data valid 1 month.

Pocket Wi-Fi: €5-7/day from airport. Unlimited data, connects 5+ devices.

eSIM: Apps like Airalo or Holafly. Cost: €10-20 for 10GB. Download before arrival.
