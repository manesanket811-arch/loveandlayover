Why Visit Egypt?

Egypt is home to one of the world's greatest civilizations. Explore the iconic Giza pyramids, cruise the legendary Nile River, discover the temples of Luxor and Aswan, and experience Cairo's bustling energy. Egypt's ancient wonders, combined with modern amenities, make it perfect for 3-10 day trips and journeys through history.

Egypt Food Guide

Egyptian cuisine is ancient, flavorful, and deeply rooted in tradition. Drawing from centuries of Mediterranean and Middle Eastern influences, Egyptian food is hearty, affordable, and deeply satisfying. Street food culture thrives in Cairo, and traditional dishes reflect the country's agricultural heritage.

Must-Try Egyptian Dishes

  • Koshari - National dish: lentils, rice, pasta, tomato sauce, chickpeas. Cost: EGP 15-30 per serving
  • Ful Medames - Stewed fava beans with olive oil, lemon, garlic. Cost: EGP 10-20 per serving
  • Falafel - Deep-fried chickpea patties, served in pita. Cost: EGP 3-8 per piece
  • Freshly Grilled Fish - Tilapia or sea bass grilled with lemon, herbs. Cost: EGP 80-200 depending on size
  • Egyptian Breakfast (Ftar) - Bread, cheese, eggs, olives, honey. Cost: EGP 20-50 per meal
  • Molokhia - Green leafy vegetable stew served with rice and bread. Cost: EGP 30-60
  • Kofta - Grilled minced meat with spices and parsley. Cost: EGP 40-80
  • Ta'ameya - Egyptian version of falafel made with fava beans. Cost: EGP 3-8 per piece

Street Food & Snacks

  • Kushari from Street Vendors - Egypt's ultimate street food (EGP 15-25 per serving)
  • Grilled Corn - Corn on the cob grilled fresh (EGP 10-20)
  • Sugar Cane Juice - Fresh pressed, incredibly refreshing (EGP 3-5)
  • Basboosa - Coconut semolina cake soaked in syrup (EGP 5-10 per piece)
  • Egyptian Baladi Bread - Thick flatbread, staple with every meal (EGP 1-2 per loaf)
  • Hibiscus Tea (Karkade) - Traditional red drink, sweet or unsweetened (EGP 3-8)

Best Places to Eat

  • Khan el-Khalili Bazaar (Cairo) - Historic bazaar with street food stalls, authentic Egyptian dining
  • Street Vendors & Koshari Shops - Found everywhere in Cairo, cheapest and tastiest meals
  • Local Restaurants (Lokanda) - Family-run eateries serving home-cooked Egyptian food
  • Nile-side Restaurants - Fish restaurants in Aswan and Luxor with river views, fresh catch daily
  • Cairo's Downton Neighborhoods - Islamic Cairo, Islamic Art museums area packed with authentic eateries

Dining Tips

Koshari is Egypt's soul food and best budget meal—found on nearly every street corner. Bread (aish, meaning "life") is sacred and always present. Grilled fish in Aswan is exceptional quality and cheap. Egyptian tea and coffee are strong and sweet—ask for less sugar if needed. Street food is safe and widely consumed by locals. Eat where you see crowds of Egyptians—best indicator of quality. Most street vendors accept cash only. Bargain politely for street food prices; quoted prices are often negotiable. Traditional dishes are served during Ramadan (avoid travel during fasting hours).

Best Time to Visit Egypt

October - April (Cool Season - Peak Season)

Weather: 15-28°C, dry and pleasant. Crowds: High (peak tourism season).

Highlights: Ideal climate perfect for sightseeing ruins and deserts. Clear skies for photography. Nile cruises most comfortable. Less sun exposure risk. Comfortable exploration of outdoor monuments.

Costs: Peak pricing. Hotels 20-30% more expensive. Nile cruises peak pricing. Book 2-3 months ahead.

Best for: First-time visitors, those prioritizing ideal weather, outdoor exploration.

May - September (Hot Season - Low Season)

Weather: 35-45°C, extremely hot and dry. Crowds: Low (fewest international tourists).

Highlights: Very few tourists means authentic Egypt, better service at attractions, quieter temples. No crowds at popular sites. Longer daylight hours.

Costs: LOWEST prices of the year. Hotels 40-50% cheaper. Flights discounted. Tours less crowded.

Concerns: Extreme heat makes daytime sightseeing unbearable (40°C+). Requires serious heat management, hydration. UV exposure intense. Not recommended for general tourism.

Best for: Heat-tolerant budget travelers, those avoiding crowds, sunset/night exploration focused travelers.

December - January (Winter Peak)

Weather: 15-25°C, ideal across the country. Crowds: VERY HIGH (holiday season, peak tourism).

Highlights: Perfect weather in Cairo, deserts, and along the Nile. Clear skies for temple photography. Year-end celebrations, festive atmosphere. Nile cruises fully booked but comfortable.

Costs: HIGHEST of the year. Hotels 30-40% more expensive. Nile cruises peak pricing. Dec 20-Jan 2 absolutely peak (book 3+ months ahead).

Best for: Those prioritizing perfect weather and willing to book early and pay premium prices.

February - March (Post-Holiday Shoulder)

Weather: 18-32°C, warm and dry. Crowds: Moderate (after holidays, before spring break).

Highlights: Still good weather. Lower crowds than peak season. Nile cruises still available. Spring break approaches (late March spike). Flowers bloom in deserts.

Costs: Mid-range pricing. Good value between peak and low season. Late March higher due to spring break.

Best for: Budget travelers valuing decent weather, those avoiding peak crowds, photographers.

April - May (Pre-Summer Transition)

Weather: 28-38°C, getting hot. Crowds: Low to moderate (spring break over, summer approaching).

Highlights: Still reasonable weather. Very few international tourists. Last chance for Nile cruises before extreme heat. Low hotel availability due to fewer visitors.

Costs: Low to mid-range pricing. Good value before peak heat season.

Best for: Budget travelers starting to experience heat, those seeking fewer crowds than peak season.

Recommendation

Best overall: November or February-March (ideal weather, manageable crowds, reasonable pricing).

Best weather: December-January (coolest and driest).

Budget travelers: May-September (lowest prices, heat management required).

First-timers: October-April (reliable weather, full tourism infrastructure, organized tours).

Egypt Neighborhoods & Regions

Cairo: The Bustling Capital

Cairo is Egypt's chaotic, magnificent heart—a massive city of 20+ million people, ancient mosques, the Egyptian Museum, bustling bazaars, and incredible street food. It's overwhelming but authentic, a gateway to understanding Egypt's culture, history, and modern life.

What makes it special: Islamic heritage, bazaars, museums, street food culture, Nile views, contemporary Egyptian energy.

Best for: Culture enthusiasts, museum lovers, foodies, photographers of street life, those seeking authentic Egypt.

Must-see attractions: Egyptian Museum, Khan el-Khalili Bazaar, Islamic Cairo mosques, Muhammad Ali Mosque, Citadel, Nile views at sunset.

Budget: EGP 400-800/day (accommodations EGP 150-400, meals EGP 100-300).

Local vibe: Chaotic, loud, energetic, bustling with commerce, spiritual mosques, street food everywhere.

Getting there: Cairo International Airport (23km east). Airport bus or taxi to city center (EGP 80-300, 60 mins). Most Egypt trips start here.

Best time to visit: October-March (comfortable). November-January coolest. Avoid June-August (extreme heat).

Giza Plateau: The Ancient Wonder

Giza Plateau is home to the Great Pyramids, one of humanity's most extraordinary achievements. These 4,500-year-old monuments stand on the outskirts of Cairo, commanding respect and wonder. Seeing the pyramids at sunrise or sunset is bucket-list magic.

What makes it special: Great Pyramids of Khufu, Menkaure, Khafre, Sphinx, ancient engineering marvel, UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Best for: Ancient history enthusiasts, monument chasers, photographers, bucket-list travelers.

Must-see attractions: Great Pyramids (Khufu, Khafre, Menkaure), The Sphinx, Pyramid of Khafre interior, Solar Boat Museum, sunrise/sunset views.

Budget: EGP 300-600/day (day-trip from Cairo, accommodations optional ₹250-400).

Local vibe: Touristy but awe-inspiring, ancient spiritual energy, desert landscapes, camel rides available.

Getting there: From Cairo by car (45 mins, EGP 150-200) or tour bus. Most visit as day-trip from Cairo. Separate ticket required for pyramid entry.

Best time to visit: November-February (cool weather for climbing). June-August scorching heat. Sunrise visits best (fewer crowds, perfect light).

Nile Valley: Luxor & Aswan

The Nile Valley is Egypt's ancient soul. Luxor (ancient Thebes) and Aswan are connected by the Nile, with temples on both banks, Valley of the Kings across the river, and Aswan offering a more relaxed pace. Most visitors experience this by Nile cruise, the quintessential Egyptian travel.

What makes it special: Nile cruises, Valley of the Kings, Luxor temples, Aswan cultural immersion, ancient Theban heritage.

Best for: Ancient Egypt enthusiasts, cruise lovers, those seeking authentic Nile experience.

Must-see attractions: Luxor Temple, Karnak Temple, Valley of the Kings, Temple of Hatshepsut, Aswan botanical gardens, Felucca sailing.

Budget: EGP 2,500-4,500/day (Nile cruises ₹1,500-3,000 including meals, independent travel cheaper).

Local vibe: Spiritual, historical, slower-paced than Cairo, genuinely local in Aswan, temple-focused spirituality.

Getting there: Flights from Cairo to Luxor (1 hour) or Aswan (1.5 hours). Nile cruises depart from Luxor/Aswan (4-5 days typical). Most travelers fly to Luxor, cruise to Aswan, fly back.

Best time to visit: November-February (perfect weather for cruising). December-January peak season and most expensive.

Red Sea Resorts: Diving & Beach Paradise

Egypt's Red Sea coast offers some of the world's best diving and snorkeling. Hurghada and Sharm el-Sheikh are beach resort towns where turquoise waters, coral reefs, and marine life create a completely different Egypt—more beach vacation than ancient history.

What makes it special: World-class diving and snorkeling, coral reefs, tropical fish, clear warm waters, beach resort relaxation.

Best for: Divers, snorkelers, beach lovers, those wanting tropical vacation experience.

Must-see attractions: Diving/snorkeling (Ras Muhammad, Blue Hole), white-sand beaches, coral reefs, water sports, sunset beach bars.

Budget: EGP 800-1,500/day (beach resorts ₹400-900, diving ₹300-600 per dive).

Local vibe: Resort-focused, beach relaxed, touristy, water-sports centered, expat influence.

Getting there: Hurghada International Airport (flights from Cairo, 1 hour). Hotels provide beach access. Ferries to nearby reefs. Many combine Cairo ancient sites with Red Sea beach relaxation.

Best time to visit: October-April (warm water, calm seas). November-February best diving (cool air, clear water). Avoid May-September (hot, occasional storms).

Getting Around Egypt

Nile Cruises - The Iconic Way

Overview: Cruising the Nile is the classic Egyptian experience. Multi-day cruises between Luxor and Aswan (4 days typical). Accommodation, meals, and guided temple visits included.

Cost: EGP 3,000-8,000 per person (budget to mid-range cruises). 5-star luxury cruises EGP 15,000+. Book through hotels, travel agencies, or online.

What's Included: Onboard meals, guided excursions to temples, cabin accommodations. Quality varies by ship—read reviews.

Domestic Flights

Cairo to Luxor/Aswan: Fastest way to reach Upper Egypt. Flight time: Cairo to Luxor (1 hour), Cairo to Aswan (2.5 hours). Cost: EGP 1,500-3,500 depending on airline.

Tip: Flying Cairo-Luxor then taking Nile cruise to Aswan is popular combination. Saves time vs. land travel.

Trains

Cairo to Luxor/Aswan: Overnight sleeper trains available (10-12 hours). Cost: EGP 500-1,500 depending on class. Book at Cairo Central Station or online.

Note: Trains slower than flights but cheaper. Sleeper cabins include meals. Experience Egyptian rail travel.

Long-Distance Buses

Network: Extensive bus routes connecting Cairo, Luxor, Aswan. Cheap but slow. Day and night buses available.

Cost: Cairo to Luxor (10 hours): EGP 150-300. Cairo to Aswan (12-14 hours): EGP 200-400.

Tip: Overnight buses save accommodation cost but are less comfortable than trains.

Local Transport in Cairo

  • Cairo Metro: Fast, inexpensive, air-conditioned. Cost: EGP 2-5 per ride (one of world's cheapest). Women-only cars available (cars 1-2).
  • Taxis: Metered white taxis, negotiable price. Cost: EGP 30-100 per short journey. Uber available in Cairo (more transparent pricing).
  • Microbuses: Cheap shared minibuses on fixed routes. Cost: EGP 3-10. Crowded but authentic Cairo experience.
  • Buses: City buses very cheap (EGP 2-5) but slow and crowded. Useful for budget travelers.

Inter-City Transport in Upper Egypt

Luxor to Aswan: Nile cruise (4 days), flights (1 hour, EGP 2,000+), buses (6-8 hours, EGP 100-200). Cruise is most scenic and includes sightseeing.

West/East Bank Crossings: Ferries across Nile River (EGP 5-10). For Valley of the Kings (West Bank), take ferry then shared taxi to sites.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Take overnight trains instead of flights to save accommodation costs
  • Cairo Metro is incredibly cheap (EGP 2-5)—best transport value
  • Book Nile cruises through hotels for discounts (not directly with cruise companies)
  • Use Uber in Cairo for transparent, lower taxi pricing
  • Fly Cairo-Luxor then cruise to Aswan (saves time vs. all-land travel)
  • Walk within Luxor and Aswan (both small and walkable)
  • Negotiate microbuses and taxis prices before boarding (quoted prices not fixed)

Travel Essentials

Budget Breakdown

  • Accommodation: EGP 500-1,500/night
  • Food: EGP 400-1,000/day
  • Transport: EGP 200-400/day
  • Activities: EGP 500-1,500/day

Visa & Entry Requirements

For Indian Passport Holders: Tourist visa required. 30-day visa available on arrival at Cairo airport (USD 25, paid in USD or EGP). Bring: Valid passport (6+ months validity), return flight ticket, proof of funds.

Alternative: Apply in advance at Egyptian embassy in India (takes 1-2 weeks, similar cost, provides peace of mind).

Travel Insurance: Highly recommended. Cost: EGP 500-1,500 for 7-10 days. Covers medical emergencies, trip cancellations, lost luggage. Important for activities like hot air balloon rides or desert adventures.

Currency & Money

Currency: Egyptian Pound (EGP). Exchange rates: 1 USD ≈ EGP 32-35 (rates fluctuate). ATMs widely available (NBE, Banque Misr, ADIB). Accept Visa/Mastercard with minimal fees (EGP 25-50 per withdrawal).

Practical: Withdraw EGP at airport ATM upon arrival for best rates (better than currency exchange). Credit cards accepted in hotels and restaurants. Street vendors and smaller restaurants prefer cash. Get a SIM card at airport (EGP 300-500 for 8-10 days unlimited data). USD still popular for large tourist payments but EGP preferred.

Highlights

Giza: Great pyramids, Sphinx, Valley of the Kings. Luxor: Karnak and Luxor temples, tombs. Aswan: Felucca boat rides, Philae temple. Cairo: Bazaars, mosques, Egyptian Museum.
