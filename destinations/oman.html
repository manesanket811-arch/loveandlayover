Why Visit Oman?

Oman is Arabia at its most natural: turquoise wadis you can swim in, golden dunes, mountain villages, forts and sea turtles, with a calm, friendly feel. Muscat is only 2½–3½ hours from most Indian cities, and Oman has a large, long-standing Indian community, with Hindu temples and Indian food everywhere.

A first trip combines Muscat with a coastal road trip to Wadi Shab and the turtles at Ras al Jinz, a night in the Wahiba Sands desert and the old fort town of Nizwa.

Oman Itineraries: 3, 5 or 7 Days

Pick 3, 5 or 7 days. Each longer plan builds on the shorter one: the first days stay the same and the extra days add more places. Costs are approximate per person per day; prices and timings change, so check before you book.

1

Muttrah Corniche & Souq

Morning
Arrive in Muscat and check in near Qurum or Muttrah.
Afternoon
Explore the maze-like Muttrah Souq (frankincense, silver, halwa) and see Al Alam Palace from outside.
Evening
Walk the Muttrah Corniche at sunset, with dhows in the harbour and forts on the hills.
OMR 35–60/day
2

Grand Mosque & Royal Opera House

Morning
Visit the Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque (non-Muslim visiting hours are usually Saturday to Thursday mornings; dress modestly, women cover their hair).
Afternoon
Tour the Royal Opera House, then the Bait Al Zubair museum.
Evening
Relax at Qurum beach or dine at the Opera Galleria.
OMR 35–60/day
3

Bimmah Sinkhole & Wadi Shab

Morning
Drive the coast road to the turquoise Bimmah Sinkhole for a swim.
Afternoon
Hike and swim through Wadi Shab, ending at a hidden waterfall cave (wear water shoes).
Evening
On a 3-day trip return to Muscat; on longer trips continue to Sur and see turtles nesting at Ras al Jinz (book the guided night visit).
OMR 40–70/day
4

Wadi Bani Khalid & Wahiba Sands

5-day plan
Morning
Swim in the green pools of Wadi Bani Khalid.
Afternoon
Drive into the Wahiba Sands with a 4×4 for dune bashing and a camel ride.
Evening
Sunset on the dunes and a night under the stars at a desert camp.
OMR 50–90/day (incl. desert camp)
5

Nizwa Fort & souq

5-day plan
Morning
Visit Nizwa Fort and its round tower (Friday mornings bring the famous goat market).
Afternoon
Browse the souq for pottery and dates, then see the abandoned mud-brick village of Birkat Al Mouz.
Evening
Stay in Nizwa or a heritage guesthouse.
OMR 40–70/day
6

Jebel Akhdar or Jebel Shams

7-day plan
Morning
Drive up the Jebel Akhdar ("green mountain"; a 4×4 is required at the checkpoint) to terraced villages and rose gardens.
Afternoon
Or go to Jebel Shams to see "Oman's Grand Canyon" and walk the Balcony Walk.
Evening
Cool mountain evening, then head back towards Muscat.
OMR 45–80/day
7

Muscat & departure

7-day plan
Morning
Visit a Muscat beach or the Shri Shiva (Motishwar) temple, one of the oldest Hindu temples in the region.
Afternoon
Final shopping for frankincense, halwa and dates.
Evening
Fly home from Muscat.
OMR 30–55/day

Food to Try in Oman

  • Shuwa — Lamb or goat marinated in spices and slow-cooked underground for a day or more, a festival dish.
  • Majboos — Spiced rice with meat or fish, similar to biryani.
  • Omani halwa — Rich, sticky sweet made with sugar, ghee, rose water and saffron.
  • Kahwa and dates — Cardamom-spiced coffee served with dates: the symbol of Omani hospitality.
  • Mishkak — Grilled meat skewers from street stalls.
  • Fresh seafood — Kingfish, lobster and tuna along the coast.

For Indian travellers: Indian food is everywhere in Oman, with Kerala, Gujarati and North Indian restaurants in every town, so vegetarian meals are easy.

Best Time to Visit Oman

October–March: the best time, with warm days of 25–30°C, ideal for wadis, desert camps and road trips.

April–May and September: hot; swim in wadis early in the day.

Summer (June–August): very hot in Muscat and the interior. The exception is Salalah in the south, which turns green during the khareef monsoon (July–September) and is popular with Gulf visitors.

Getting Around Oman

Flights from India: direct flights link Muscat with many Indian cities, taking about 2½–3½ hours.

Road trips: renting a car is the best way to see Oman. Roads are excellent and signs are in English. You need an International Driving Permit with your Indian licence; rent a 4×4 for the desert and Jebel Akhdar.

In Muscat: use taxis or ride apps (Otaxi, OmanTaxi). Buses are limited.

Tours: day tours cover Wadi Shab, the desert and Nizwa if you don't want to drive.

Travel Essentials

Entry for Indians: Indians need a visa: apply for the e-visa only on the official Royal Oman Police portal (evisa.rop.gov.om) before travelling. Indians holding certain valid foreign visas or residence permits (such as from the US, UK, Schengen countries, Canada, Australia or Japan) may be able to enter visa-free for short stays. Oman launched a new free 14-day tourist visa in 2026, but India wasn't on the published list at the time of writing. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you travel.

Money: Omani rial (OMR), one of the strongest currencies in the world. 1 OMR is about ₹220. Cards work widely; carry cash for villages and souqs.

Daily budget (per person): a mid-range hotel share, food, fuel and sightseeing come to about OMR 40–70 a day, plus car hire and the desert camp.

SIM card: Omantel, Ooredoo and Vodafone Oman sell tourist SIMs at the airport with your passport.

Plugs: type G (UK three-pin), 240V. Bring an adapter for Indian plugs.

Etiquette and safety: Oman is very safe and polite. Dress modestly away from the beach, ask before photographing people, and never enter wadis when rain or flash floods are forecast.

Oman FAQs

How much does a Oman trip cost per day?

Plan for roughly OMR 40–70 per person per day (about ₹8,800–15,500) for a mid-range trip, covering accommodation, food, local transport and sightseeing. Prices are approximate as of October 2026.

How many days do you need in Oman?

3 days covers the highlights: Muttrah Corniche & Souq, Grand Mosque & Royal Opera House and Bimmah Sinkhole & Wadi Shab. With 5 days you can add Wadi Bani Khalid & Wahiba Sands and Nizwa Fort & souq, and a full week adds Jebel Akhdar or Jebel Shams and Muscat.
