Why Visit Bhutan?

Bhutan, the last great Himalayan kingdom, measures success in Gross National Happiness. Expect fortress-monasteries (dzongs), prayer flags on mountain passes, spotless forests and a culture that has changed little over centuries. Indians don't need a visa, the ngultrum is pegged to the rupee and you can fly in or arrive by road from West Bengal.

A first trip usually covers Thimphu, the capital; Punakha, across the Dochula Pass; and Paro, home to the cliffside Tiger's Nest monastery.

Bhutan Itineraries: 3, 5 or 7 Days

Pick 3, 5 or 7 days. Each longer plan builds on the shorter one: the first days stay the same and the extra days add more places. Costs are approximate per person per day; prices and timings change, so check before you book.

1

Paro arrival & Thimphu

Morning
Fly into Paro (one of the world's most dramatic airport approaches) or arrive by road via Jaigaon–Phuentsholing. Collect your entry permit with your passport or voter ID.
Afternoon
Drive to Thimphu (about 1 hour). Visit the giant Buddha Dordenma statue overlooking the valley.
Evening
See Tashichho Dzong lit up in the evening and walk Thimphu's main street, which has no traffic lights.
₹5,000–9,000/day + SDF
2

Thimphu culture

Morning
Visit the National Memorial Chorten, where locals circle and spin prayer wheels, then the Folk Heritage Museum.
Afternoon
See the national animal at the Motithang Takin Preserve and the traditional arts at the Institute for Zorig Chusum (the painting school).
Evening
On weekends, browse the Centenary Farmers' Market; have dinner with ema datshi and red rice.
₹5,000–8,000/day + SDF
3

Dochula Pass & Punakha Dzong

Morning
Drive over Dochula Pass (about 3,100 m) with its 108 chortens and, on clear days, views of the Himalaya.
Afternoon
Visit Punakha Dzong, the most beautiful fortress in Bhutan, at the meeting of two rivers, and cross the long suspension bridge nearby.
Evening
Overnight in Punakha, which is warmer than Thimphu. Indian visitors usually need a route permit and a licensed guide for travel beyond Thimphu and Paro, so arrange this through your hotel or a tour operator.
₹6,000–10,000/day + SDF
4

Chimi Lhakhang & Punakha valley

5-day plan
Morning
Walk through rice fields to Chimi Lhakhang, the fertility temple of the "Divine Madman".
Afternoon
Hike up to Khamsum Yulley Namgyal Chorten for valley views, or try river rafting on the Mo Chhu (seasonal).
Evening
A quiet evening in Punakha.
₹5,000–9,000/day + SDF
5

Back to Paro

5-day plan
Morning
Drive back over Dochula to Paro (about 4–5 hours).
Afternoon
Visit Rinpung Dzong and the National Museum above it.
Evening
Stroll Paro town and rest well before tomorrow's hike. Try a hot-stone bath (dotsho) at a farmhouse.
₹5,000–9,000/day + SDF
6

Tiger's Nest hike

7-day plan
Morning
Hike to Paro Taktsang (Tiger's Nest), the monastery clinging to a cliff about 900 m above the valley. The round trip takes 4–6 hours; start early and go at your own pace.
Afternoon
Lunch at the cafeteria viewpoint on the way down, then visit the ancient Kyichu Lhakhang temple.
Evening
Celebrate the climb with a traditional dinner.
₹5,000–9,000/day + SDF
7

Chele La & departure

7-day plan
Morning
If your flight allows, drive up to Chele La pass (about 3,900 m) for prayer flags and views of Jomolhari; or browse Paro's handicraft shops.
Afternoon
Fly out of Paro, or drive back to Phuentsholing (about 4–5 hours) to cross into India.
Evening
Onward journey home from Bagdogra or Paro.
₹4,000–8,000/day + SDF

Bhutanese Food to Try

  • Ema datshi — The national dish: chillies cooked in cheese. Seriously spicy.
  • Kewa datshi and shamu datshi — Potatoes or mushrooms in cheese sauce, milder versions of ema datshi.
  • Red rice — Nutty Bhutanese red rice served with most meals.
  • Momos — Dumplings with cheese, vegetable or meat filling.
  • Jasha maru — Spicy minced chicken stew.
  • Suja — Salty butter tea, an acquired taste.

For Indian travellers: vegetarian food is easy to find and most hotels can make dal, sabzi and roti. Ask for "less chilli" if you don't want it very hot.

Best Time to Visit Bhutan

Spring (March–May): rhododendrons in bloom, pleasant days and festivals such as the Paro Tshechu.

Autumn (September–November): clear skies and the best mountain views, plus the Thimphu Tshechu festival. This is peak season.

Winter (December–February): cold, especially at night, but clear; passes can close after snow.

Monsoon (June–August): lush and quiet, but rain and landslides can affect roads.

Getting Around Bhutan

By air: Druk Air and Bhutan Airlines fly to Paro from Delhi, Kolkata, Bagdogra, Guwahati and other cities. Flights are weather-dependent.

By road: cross from Jaigaon (West Bengal) to Phuentsholing, then drive about 4–5 hours to Thimphu. Bagdogra is the nearest Indian airport.

Getting around: there are no trains, and roads are narrow and winding, so most visitors travel by hired car with driver. Allow plenty of time between towns.

Travel Essentials

Entry for Indians: Indian citizens don't need a visa. You get an entry permit on arrival at Paro or Phuentsholing with a valid passport or voter ID card, and a further route permit to travel beyond Thimphu and Paro. Indian visitors also pay a Sustainable Development Fee (SDF) per person per night (₹1,200 at the time of writing, with reductions for children), and usually need a licensed guide outside Thimphu and Paro. Book permits and hotels through a licensed operator or your hotel. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you travel.

Money: Bhutanese ngultrum (Nu.), pegged 1:1 to the Indian rupee. Indian rupees are widely accepted, though larger notes are sometimes refused. ATMs exist in towns, but card acceptance is limited.

Daily budget (per person): roughly ₹6,000–10,000 a day for a hotel share, car and driver, guide and food, plus the SDF.

SIM card: B-Mobile and TashiCell sell SIMs with your permit and ID.

Plugs: types D, G and M, 230V.

Etiquette: dress formally for dzongs and temples (long sleeves and long trousers or skirts; no caps), walk clockwise around chortens, and don't point at religious objects. Smoking in public places is prohibited and tobacco you bring in is taxed.

Bhutan FAQs

How much does a Bhutan trip cost per day?

Plan for roughly ₹6,000–10,000/day + SDF per person per day for a mid-range trip, covering accommodation, food, local transport and sightseeing. Prices are approximate as of October 2026.

How many days do you need in Bhutan?

3 days covers the highlights: Paro arrival & Thimphu, Thimphu culture and Dochula Pass & Punakha Dzong. With 5 days you can add Chimi Lhakhang & Punakha valley and Back to Paro, and a full week adds Tiger's Nest hike and Chele La. Our 3, 5 and 7-day itineraries build on each other, so you can stop at any point.

When is the best time to visit Bhutan?

Mar–May, Sep–Nov. Spring (March–May): rhododendrons in bloom, pleasant days and festivals such as the Paro Tshechu.
