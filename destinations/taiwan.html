Why Visit Taiwan?

Taiwan is one of Asia's most underrated trips: safe, friendly and spotless, with legendary night markets, hot springs, mountain lakes, temples and the skyline of Taipei 101. Public transport is excellent, so it's easy for first-timers and families.

It's also one of the easiest places in East Asia for vegetarians, thanks to its many Buddhist vegetarian restaurants. A first trip combines Taipei with day trips to Jiufen and Shifen, plus Sun Moon Lake on longer itineraries.

Taiwan Itineraries: 3, 5 or 7 Days

Pick 3, 5 or 7 days. Each longer plan builds on the shorter one: the first days stay the same and the extra days add more places. Costs are approximate per person per day; prices and timings change, so check before you book.

1

Taipei 101 & Elephant Mountain

Morning
Arrive at Taoyuan Airport and take the Airport MRT into Taipei (about 40 minutes).
Afternoon
Go up Taipei 101 for views over the city, then explore the Xinyi shopping district.
Evening
Hike the steps of Elephant Mountain for sunset over Taipei 101, then the Raohe Night Market.
NT$2,500–4,500/day
2

Temples, Ximending & Shilin

Morning
Visit Longshan Temple and the grand Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall (watch the changing of the guard).
Afternoon
See the treasures of the National Palace Museum, or wander lively Ximending.
Evening
Eat your way around the huge Shilin Night Market.
NT$2,500–4,500/day
3

Jiufen, Shifen & the Pingxi line

Morning
Take a tour or train to Shifen: write wishes on a sky lantern by the railway tracks and see Shifen Waterfall.
Afternoon
Continue to the hillside town of Jiufen, with lantern-lit lanes and tea houses overlooking the sea.
Evening
Stay for the lanterns at dusk, then return to Taipei.
NT$3,000–5,000/day
4

Beitou hot springs & Yangmingshan

5-day plan
Morning
Visit the Beitou hot-spring area: Thermal Valley and the Hot Spring Museum.
Afternoon
Soak in a public or private hot-spring bath, or hike in Yangmingshan National Park.
Evening
Sunset at Tamsui by the river, with its old fort and street snacks.
NT$3,000–5,000/day
5

High-speed rail to Sun Moon Lake

5-day plan
Morning
Take the High Speed Rail to Taichung (under an hour), then a bus to Sun Moon Lake.
Afternoon
Cruise across the lake by boat and visit the Wenwu Temple.
Evening
Quiet lakeside evening; stay overnight at the lake.
NT$3,500–6,000/day (incl. travel)
6

Sun Moon Lake & Taichung

7-day plan
Morning
Cycle part of the lakeside path or ride the ropeway for views.
Afternoon
Head back to Taichung and see the colourful Rainbow Village.
Evening
Dinner at the Fengjia Night Market, one of Taiwan's biggest.
NT$3,000–5,000/day
7

Back to Taipei & departure

7-day plan
Morning
High Speed Rail back to Taipei or straight to Taoyuan Airport.
Afternoon
Last shopping for pineapple cakes, tea and bubble tea kits.
Evening
Fly home, usually connecting through a Southeast Asian hub or Hong Kong.
NT$2,500–4,500/day

Food to Try in Taiwan

  • Beef noodle soup — Taiwan's comfort dish: braised beef in a rich broth with chewy noodles.
  • Xiaolongbao — Soup dumplings; most dumpling restaurants have vegetable and mushroom versions.
  • Bubble tea — Invented in Taiwan: milk tea with chewy tapioca pearls.
  • Scallion pancake — Flaky, crispy street snack that's usually vegetarian.
  • Stinky tofu — Fermented tofu, fried or in a spicy broth: love it or hate it.
  • Mango shaved ice and pineapple cake — The best dessert in summer, and the classic gift to take home.

For Indian travellers: Look for the character 素 (su), which means vegetarian. Taiwan has thousands of Buddhist vegetarian restaurants and buffets, many with no onion or garlic, which suits Jain travellers too. Taipei also has Indian restaurants.

Best Time to Visit Taiwan

Autumn (October–December): the best time, with comfortable temperatures and clear skies.

Spring (March–April): pleasant weather and cherry and plum blossoms in the mountains.

Plum rain (May–June): frequent showers.

Summer (July–September): hot and humid, with typhoons possible; keep plans flexible.

Winter (January–February): cool and damp in Taipei, but good for hot springs; Lunar New Year makes trains busy.

Getting Around Taiwan

Flights from India: most trips connect through Bangkok, Singapore, Kuala Lumpur or Hong Kong; new direct flights from India have been announced, so check current routes.

In Taipei: the MRT is clean, cheap and easy. Get an EasyCard for the metro, buses, trains and convenience stores.

Around the island: the High Speed Rail runs from Taipei to Kaohsiung in about 1½ hours; regular trains reach the east coast. Buses connect to Sun Moon Lake.

Taxis: plentiful and metered; Uber also works in big cities.

Travel Essentials

Entry for Indians: Indians need permission to visit. If you hold a valid visa or residence permit from the US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea or a Schengen country, you can usually apply online for a free Travel Authorization Certificate for short stays. Otherwise apply for a visitor visa through the Taipei Economic and Cultural Center (TECC) in India. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you travel.

Money: New Taiwan dollar (NT$, TWD). NT$100 is about ₹270. Cards work in most hotels and malls; night markets and small eateries are often cash only.

Daily budget (per person): a mid-range hotel share, food, transport and sightseeing come to about NT$3,000–5,000 a day, plus the High Speed Rail.

SIM card: Chunghwa Telecom, Taiwan Mobile and FarEasTone sell tourist SIMs at the airport with your passport.

Plugs: types A and B (flat pins), 110V. Indian plugs won't fit: bring an adapter, and check that your chargers support 110V (most phone and laptop chargers do).

Etiquette and safety: Taiwan is one of the safest places in Asia. Queue neatly, keep quiet on the MRT (no eating or drinking), and remove shoes in homes and some temples. Taroko Gorge was damaged by a 2024 earthquake, so check which trails are open.

Taiwan FAQs

How much does a Taiwan trip cost per day?

Plan for roughly NT$3,000–5,000 per person per day (about ₹8,100–13,500) for a mid-range trip, covering accommodation, food, local transport and sightseeing. Prices are approximate as of October 2026.

How many days do you need in Taiwan?

3 days covers the highlights: Taipei 101 & Elephant Mountain, Temples, Ximending & Shilin and Jiufen, Shifen & the Pingxi line. With 5 days you can add Beitou hot springs & Yangmingshan and High-speed rail to Sun Moon Lake, and a full week adds Sun Moon Lake & Taichung and Back to Taipei. Our 3, 5 and 7-day itineraries build on each other, so you can stop at any point.

When is the best time to visit Taiwan?

Oct–Dec, Mar–Apr. Autumn (October–December): the best time, with comfortable temperatures and clear skies.
