Why Visit Azerbaijan?

Azerbaijan feels like Europe and Central Asia in one short trip from India: a glittering Caspian Sea capital, a medieval walled city, mud volcanoes, mountains with cable cars and silk-road towns. Flights from Delhi take about five hours, the e-visa is quick, and it's still good value compared with Europe.

There's a surprising Indian connection too: the Ateshgah Fire Temple near Baku was used by Hindu and Zoroastrian worshippers, and Sanskrit and Punjabi inscriptions left by Indian traders are still on its walls. A classic first trip pairs Baku with the green mountains of Gabala and the old silk-road town of Sheki.

Azerbaijan Itineraries: 3, 5 or 7 Days

Pick 3, 5 or 7 days. Each longer plan builds on the shorter one: the first days stay the same and the extra days add more places. Costs are approximate per person per day; prices and timings change, so check before you book.

1

Baku Old City & the Boulevard

Morning
Arrive at Heydar Aliyev International Airport and check in near the Old City or Nizami Street.
Afternoon
Walk the walled Old City (Icherisheher), a UNESCO site: the Maiden Tower, the Palace of the Shirvanshahs and the caravanserais.
Evening
Stroll the seaside Baku Boulevard and watch the Flame Towers light up from Highland Park.
AZN 100–160/day
2

Gobustan & the mud volcanoes

Morning
Tour to Gobustan National Park: rock carvings thousands of years old and a small museum.
Afternoon
Bump over to the bubbling mud volcanoes nearby (usually by local Lada taxi), then the Bibi-Heybat Mosque on the way back.
Evening
Dinner and people-watching around Fountain Square and Nizami Street.
AZN 120–180/day
3

Fire temple, Yanar Dag & modern Baku

Morning
Visit the Ateshgah Fire Temple in Surakhani and look for the Sanskrit and Gurmukhi inscriptions left by Indian traders.
Afternoon
See the curving Heydar Aliyev Center, then the Carpet Museum by the sea.
Evening
Watch the hillside burn at Yanar Dag ("burning mountain"), most dramatic after dark.
AZN 120–180/day
4

To Gabala: mountains & cable cars

5-day plan
Morning
Drive about 3–4 hours to Gabala in the Caucasus foothills, stopping at Shamakhi's Juma Mosque on the way.
Afternoon
Ride the Tufandag cable cars for big mountain views (skiing in winter).
Evening
Relax at Nohur Lake and stay in a mountain resort or guesthouse.
AZN 130–200/day (incl. travel)
5

Sheki: the silk-road town

5-day plan
Morning
Drive about 1½ hours to Sheki and see the painted, stained-glass Sheki Khan's Palace.
Afternoon
Explore the old caravanserai and the Albanian church at nearby Kish village.
Evening
Try Sheki halva and piti (a slow-cooked stew), and stay overnight in Sheki.
AZN 110–180/day
6

Back to Baku via Shamakhi wineries

7-day plan
Morning
Drive back towards Baku through the green hills.
Afternoon
Stop at a winery around Shamakhi or Ismayilli for a tasting (or fresh pomegranate juice).
Evening
Final evening on the Boulevard or a ride on the Baku Eye.
AZN 120–190/day (incl. travel)
7

Shopping & departure

7-day plan
Morning
Shop for carpets, tea glasses (armudu), pomegranate souvenirs and pakhlava on Nizami Street.
Afternoon
Visit the Baku Crystal Hall area or the Old City's small museums if time allows.
Evening
Head to the airport, about 30 minutes from the city.
AZN 80–150/day

Food to Try in Azerbaijan

  • Plov — Saffron rice layered with meat or dried fruit and chestnuts; there are dozens of regional versions.
  • Qutab — Thin stuffed flatbreads; the göy qutab filled with herbs and greens is vegetarian.
  • Dolma — Vine or cabbage leaves stuffed with rice and minced meat; vegetable versions are common in summer.
  • Dushbara — Tiny lamb dumplings in a clear broth.
  • Kebabs — Lyulya (minced) and tikka kebabs grilled over coals, served with lavash and herbs.
  • Pakhlava and Sheki halva — Layered nut sweets, best with black tea in a pear-shaped armudu glass and a spoon of jam.

For Indian travellers: Vegetarian food takes a little planning: ask for qutab with greens, salads, lentil soup (mərci şorbası) and grilled vegetables. Baku has several Indian restaurants around Nizami Street and the Old City.

Best Time to Visit Azerbaijan

Spring (April–June): the best time, with mild weather in Baku and green mountains around Gabala.

Autumn (September–October): warm days, pomegranate season and fewer crowds.

Summer (July–August): hot in Baku (often 35°C) and windy; pleasant in the mountains.

Winter (December–March): cold and windy in Baku, but snow and skiing at Shahdag and Tufandag.

Getting Around Azerbaijan

Flights from India: direct flights run from Delhi (about 5 hours); from other cities, connect through the Gulf or Istanbul. Check current routes when you book.

In Baku: the metro is cheap and fast with a BakuKart card, which also works on buses. Bolt and Uber taxis are inexpensive; avoid unmarked cars.

Day trips and the regions: join a tour or hire a car and driver for Gobustan, Gabala and Sheki; roads are good, and intercity buses leave from Baku's main bus station.

Airport: the Aeroexpress bus runs to the city centre around the clock; a taxi takes about 30 minutes.

Travel Essentials

Entry for Indians: Indians need a visa. Apply for the ASAN e-visa only on the official portal (evisa.gov.az): it's single-entry for up to 30 days and is usually issued within about 3 working days (a faster urgent option costs more). Fees are around US$25–30; avoid look-alike sites. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you travel.

Money: Azerbaijani manat (AZN). 1 AZN is about ₹50. Cards work widely in Baku; carry cash for the regions and markets.

Daily budget (per person): a mid-range hotel share, food, local transport and sightseeing come to about AZN 120–200 a day, plus tours and the drive to the regions.

SIM card: Azercell and Bakcell sell tourist SIMs at the airport with your passport; eSIMs also work.

Plugs: types C and F, 220V. Indian two-pin plugs usually fit type C sockets; bring a universal adapter for three-pin chargers.

Etiquette and safety: Baku is very safe. Dress modestly in mosques (women cover their hair), ask before photographing people, and don't plan travel to the Armenia border areas.

Azerbaijan FAQs

How much does a Azerbaijan trip cost per day?

Plan for roughly AZN 120–200 per person per day (about ₹6,000–10,000) for a mid-range trip, covering accommodation, food, local transport and sightseeing. Prices are approximate as of October 2026.

How many days do you need in Azerbaijan?

3 days covers the highlights: Baku Old City & the Boulevard, Gobustan & the mud volcanoes and Fire temple, Yanar Dag & modern Baku. With 5 days you can add To Gabala: mountains & cable cars and Sheki: the silk-road town, and a full week adds Back to Baku via Shamakhi wineries and Shopping. Our 3, 5 and 7-day itineraries build on each other, so you can stop at any point.

When is the best time to visit Azerbaijan?

Apr–Jun, Sep–Oct. Spring (April–June): the best time, with mild weather in Baku and green mountains around Gabala.
