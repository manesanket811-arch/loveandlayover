Why Visit Sri Lanka?

Sri Lanka packs ancient rock fortresses, misty tea country, wildlife safaris and golden beaches into an island you can cross in a day. It's one of the closest international trips from India, with short flights from Chennai, Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi and other cities, and the food will feel familiar yet different: think hoppers, kottu and coconut-rich curries.

The classic route loops from Colombo through the Cultural Triangle (Sigiriya, Dambulla), the hill country (Kandy, Ella) and down to the south coast (Mirissa, Galle). It's great value, and the scenic train through the tea hills is one of the most beautiful rail journeys in the world.

Sri Lanka Itineraries: 3, 5 or 7 Days

Pick 3, 5 or 7 days. Each longer plan builds on the shorter one: the first days stay the same and the extra days add more places. Costs are approximate per person per day; prices and timings change, so check before you book.

1

Colombo arrival

Morning
Land at Bandaranaike International Airport, about 45 minutes north of Colombo via the expressway. Buy a local SIM at the airport and arrange your driver or train tickets.
Afternoon
Visit Gangaramaya Temple and the floating Seema Malaka on Beira Lake, then the Pettah markets and the red-and-white Jami Ul-Alfar Mosque.
Evening
Sunset on Galle Face Green with isso wade (prawn fritters) from the beach stalls, then dinner at a local rice and curry spot.
LKR 12,000–20,000/day
2

Dambulla cave temple & Sigiriya

Morning
Drive about 4 hours to the Cultural Triangle. Most visitors hire a car and driver for the loop, which is comfortable and good value when shared.
Afternoon
Visit the Dambulla Cave Temple, five caves filled with Buddha statues and murals (dress modestly; remove shoes and hats).
Evening
Climb Pidurangala Rock for sunset with a view of Sigiriya, then stay overnight near Sigiriya.
LKR 15,000–25,000/day
3

Sigiriya Rock & Minneriya safari

Early Morning
Climb Sigiriya, the 5th-century rock fortress, as soon as it opens to beat the heat and crowds (about 1,200 steps; foreign-visitor tickets cost around US$35).
Afternoon
Join a jeep safari in Minneriya or Kaudulla National Park, famous for large gatherings of wild elephants (season dependent; your driver will advise which park).
Evening
Dinner at your guesthouse. If your trip ends here, it's about 4 hours back to the airport.
LKR 20,000–35,000/day
4

Kandy: Temple of the Tooth

5-day plan
Morning
Drive to Kandy (about 2.5–3 hours), stopping at a spice garden in Matale.
Afternoon
Visit the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic, Sri Lanka's most important Buddhist shrine, and walk around Kandy Lake.
Evening
Watch a Kandyan cultural dance show with fire walking, then attend the evening puja at the temple.
LKR 15,000–25,000/day
5

The scenic train to Ella

5-day plan
Morning
Take the famous train from Kandy to Ella through tea plantations and misty hills (about 6–7 hours). Reserved seats sell out, so book in advance or ask your driver to meet you along the route.
Afternoon
Enjoy the views from the open doors and windows; buy snacks from vendors at the stations.
Evening
Arrive in Ella, a laid-back mountain village with cafés and a cool climate.
LKR 12,000–20,000/day
6

Ella to the south coast

7-day plan
Morning
Walk to the Nine Arch Bridge (check the train times to see one cross) and hike up Little Adam's Peak.
Afternoon
Drive down to the south coast (about 4–5 hours), via Ravana Falls. Wildlife lovers can stop for an afternoon safari in Yala or Udawalawe on the way.
Evening
Arrive in Mirissa or Weligama and have a seafood dinner on the beach.
LKR 15,000–25,000/day
7

Mirissa, Galle Fort & departure

7-day plan
Morning
Between November and April, take an early whale-watching trip from Mirissa (choose an operator that keeps its distance from the whales), or relax on the beach.
Afternoon
Explore Galle Fort, a UNESCO-listed Dutch colonial town with ramparts, boutiques and cafés.
Evening
Drive to the airport via the Southern Expressway (about 2.5–3 hours) for your flight home.
LKR 15,000–25,000/day

Sri Lankan Food to Try

  • Rice and curry — Rice with several vegetable, dal and meat or fish curries, plus pol sambol (spiced coconut). The everyday lunch.
  • Kottu roti — Chopped flatbread stir-fried with vegetables, egg or meat; listen for the clanging of the cooks' blades.
  • Hoppers (appa) — Bowl-shaped rice-flour pancakes, often with an egg cooked inside; great for breakfast or dinner.
  • String hoppers — Steamed rice-noodle nests eaten with curry and coconut sambol.
  • Lamprais — Rice and curries baked in a banana leaf, a Dutch Burgher speciality.
  • Watalappan — Coconut and jaggery custard with cardamom.
  • Ceylon tea — Visit a tea factory in the hill country and taste it fresh.

Vegetarian tip: vegetarian food is easy to find, especially rice and curry, dosa and vadai in Tamil areas. Many dishes use Maldive fish flakes, so ask for "no fish" if you're strictly vegetarian.

Best Time to Visit Sri Lanka

West and south coasts and the hill country (Colombo, Galle, Mirissa, Kandy, Ella): December to March is the driest and most popular time.

East coast (Trincomalee, Arugam Bay): April to September, when the south-west monsoon brings rain to the other side of the island.

Year-round: the island has two monsoons, so somewhere is always in season. Expect 26–32°C on the coast and much cooler evenings in Ella and Nuwara Eliya.

Getting Around Sri Lanka

Car and driver: the most popular way to do the classic loop. Agree the route and price in advance; your driver arranges their own accommodation.

Trains: cheap and scenic, especially Kandy to Ella. Book reserved seats in advance on the official railway e-ticketing site or through an agent.

Tuk-tuks and taxis: use the PickMe or Uber apps in Colombo and larger towns, or agree the fare before you get in.

Buses: very cheap and go everywhere, but crowded and fast; fine for short trips with light luggage.

Travel Essentials

Visa for Indians: Indian passport holders need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) for tourism, applied for online on the official Sri Lanka ETA website before you fly. Sri Lanka has at times waived the fee for Indian tourists, so check the current fee and use only the official site. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you travel.

Money: Sri Lankan rupee (LKR). LKR 1,000 is roughly ₹280. Cards work in hotels and bigger restaurants; carry cash for tuk-tuks, small eateries and entry tickets. Indian rupees are not widely accepted.

Daily budget (per person): guesthouse or mid-range hotel share, food, transport and one paid sight usually come to LKR 15,000–25,000 a day. Major sites such as Sigiriya charge foreign-visitor prices in US dollars.

SIM card: Dialog, Mobitel and Hutch sell tourist SIMs at the airport with generous data.

Plugs: types D and G (and M), 230V. Many Indian plugs fit type D sockets, but bring a universal adapter.

Temple etiquette: cover shoulders and knees, remove shoes and hats, and never pose with your back to a Buddha statue. Buddha tattoos can cause problems on entry, so keep them covered.

Sri Lanka FAQs

Do Indians need a visa for Sri Lanka?

Indian passport holders need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) for tourism, applied for online on the official Sri Lanka ETA website before you fly. Sri Lanka has at times waived the fee for Indian tourists, so check the current fee and use only the official site. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you travel.

How much does a Sri Lanka trip cost per day?

Plan for roughly LKR 15,000–25,000 per person per day (about ₹4,200–7,000) for a mid-range trip, covering accommodation, food, local transport and sightseeing. Prices are approximate as of October 2026.

How many days do you need in Sri Lanka?

3 days covers the highlights: Colombo arrival, Dambulla cave temple & Sigiriya and Sigiriya Rock & Minneriya safari. With 5 days you can add Kandy: Temple of the Tooth and The scenic train to Ella, and a full week adds Ella to the south coast and Mirissa, Galle Fort. Our 3, 5 and 7-day itineraries build on each other, so you can stop at any point.

When is the best time to visit Sri Lanka?

Dec–Mar (west & south). West and south coasts and the hill country (Colombo, Galle, Mirissa, Kandy, Ella): December to March is the driest and most popular time.
