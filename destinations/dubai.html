Why Visit Dubai?

Dubai is a city of records and contrasts: the world's tallest building, enormous malls and man-made islands next to old souks, wooden abras on the creek and the open desert. Just 3–4 hours from most Indian cities, it's one of the most popular international trips for Indian families, with a huge Indian community, familiar food everywhere and Hindi widely understood.

It's also an easy base for Abu Dhabi, home to the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, the Louvre Abu Dhabi and the BAPS Hindu Mandir.

Dubai Itineraries: 3, 5 or 7 Days

Pick 3, 5 or 7 days. Each longer plan builds on the shorter one: the first days stay the same and the extra days add more places. Costs are approximate per person per day; prices and timings change, so check before you book.

1

Downtown Dubai & the Burj Khalifa

Morning
Land at Dubai International (DXB) and take the Metro Red Line or a taxi to your hotel. Get a Nol card for the Metro, buses and trams.
Afternoon
Visit the Dubai Mall, its aquarium and the souk-style shops, then go up the Burj Khalifa to At the Top (book a time slot in advance; sunset slots cost more).
Evening
Watch the free Dubai Fountain show by the Burj Khalifa (every 30 minutes in the evening), then dinner in Downtown.
AED 400–700/day
2

Old Dubai: souks & the creek

Morning
Walk through Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood with its wind-tower houses, and visit the Dubai Museum area and the coffee museum.
Afternoon
Cross Dubai Creek on a traditional abra (a ride costs just a couple of dirhams) to the Gold Souk and Spice Souk in Deira. Bargaining is expected in the souks.
Evening
Take a dhow dinner cruise on the creek or Dubai Marina, or eat at the Indian restaurants of Karama and Meena Bazaar.
AED 250–450/day
3

Desert safari

Morning
Visit the Museum of the Future (book ahead) or relax at Jumeirah Beach with a view of the Burj Al Arab.
Afternoon
Pickup for an evening desert safari: dune bashing in a 4x4, sandboarding and camel rides. Tell the driver if you prefer a gentler drive.
Evening
Dinner at a desert camp with a barbecue buffet (vegetarian options are common), tanoura and fire shows. Back in the city around 9–10 PM.
AED 350–600/day
4

Abu Dhabi day trip

5-day plan
Morning
Drive or take an intercity bus to Abu Dhabi (about 1.5 hours). Visit the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque (free, book a slot online; modest dress, women cover their hair).
Afternoon
See the Louvre Abu Dhabi on Saadiyat Island, or visit the BAPS Hindu Mandir at Abu Mureikhah on the way (free; check opening days and dress code).
Evening
Stroll the Corniche, or visit Qasr Al Watan palace, then return to Dubai.
AED 300–500/day
5

Palm Jumeirah, Marina & JBR

5-day plan
Morning
Ride the monorail along Palm Jumeirah or see it from The View at The Palm. Families can spend the day at Atlantis Aquaventure waterpark.
Afternoon
Walk the Dubai Marina promenade and swim at JBR Beach.
Evening
Dinner at The Walk JBR or Bluewaters Island under the Ain Dubai wheel.
AED 400–800/day
6

Dubai Frame, Miracle Garden & Global Village

7-day plan
Morning
See old and new Dubai from the Dubai Frame.
Afternoon
Visit the Dubai Miracle Garden, with millions of flowers in giant displays (open roughly November to May).
Evening
Spend the evening at Global Village, an outdoor festival park with pavilions, shows and food from around the world, including a big India pavilion (open roughly October to May).
AED 250–450/day
7

Shopping & departure

7-day plan
Morning
Shop at Mall of the Emirates (and see Ski Dubai's indoor snow) or Dubai Festival City.
Afternoon
Pick up dates, spices, perfume and gold (the gold souk and malls sell certified jewellery). Keep receipts for VAT refunds at the airport.
Evening
Head to DXB by Metro or taxi, allowing at least 3 hours before your flight.
AED 200–600/day

Food to Try in Dubai

  • Shawarma — Spit-roasted chicken or meat wrapped in flatbread with garlic sauce; available everywhere.
  • Al machboos — Spiced rice with chicken, lamb or fish, a classic Emirati dish.
  • Luqaimat — Crispy dumplings drizzled with date syrup and sesame.
  • Manakish and falafel — Levantine favourites for breakfast or a cheap lunch.
  • Dates and Arabic coffee — Offered as a welcome in hotels and homes.
  • Camel milk treats — Try camel-milk chocolate or ice cream.

For Indian travellers: you'll find every Indian cuisine, from South Indian thalis to Gujarati and Punjabi food, especially in Karama, Bur Dubai and Al Barsha. Vegetarian and Jain options are easy to find.

Best Time to Visit Dubai

Winter (November–March): the best time, with warm days (about 20–30°C), cool evenings and outdoor attractions like Global Village and the Miracle Garden open. It's also the busiest and most expensive season.

Shoulder months (April and October): hotter but manageable, with better prices.

Summer (May–September): extremely hot (often above 40°C). Hotels are much cheaper and life moves indoors to malls and attractions.

Ramadan: dates change each year. Restaurants stay open for visitors, but avoid eating or drinking in public during daylight.

Getting Around Dubai

Dubai Metro: the Red and Green lines connect the airport, Downtown, the Marina and Old Dubai. Use a Nol card; there are carriages reserved for women and children, plus a Gold Class.

Taxis and apps: metered taxis are plentiful; Careem and Uber also operate.

Abras, buses and trams: abras cross Dubai Creek for a couple of dirhams; RTA buses and the Dubai Tram fill the gaps.

Abu Dhabi: intercity buses run from Dubai, or hire a car with driver for a day trip.

Travel Essentials

Visa for Indians: Indian passport holders need a visa for the UAE, usually a tourist e-visa applied for online through airlines such as Emirates or flydubai, licensed travel agents or the official immigration portal. Indians holding a valid US visa or green card, or a UK or EU residence permit or visa, may be eligible for a visa on arrival. Rules as of October 2026; always check the official website before you travel.

Money: UAE dirham (AED). AED 1 is roughly ₹23. Cards and phone payments work almost everywhere.

Daily budget (per person): a mid-range hotel share, food, transport and one big attraction come to about AED 400–700 a day. Many hotels also add a tourism fee per night.

SIM card: du and e& (Etisalat) sell tourist SIMs at the airport and in malls; eSIMs work well.

Plugs: type G (three square pins), 230V.

Local customs: dress modestly in malls, souks and mosques (cover shoulders and knees), avoid public displays of affection, and don't photograph people without permission. The weekend is Saturday and Sunday, and Friday prayers affect some opening times.

How much does a Dubai trip cost per day?

Plan for roughly AED 400–700 per person per day (about ₹9,200–16,000) for a mid-range trip, covering accommodation, food, local transport and sightseeing. Prices are approximate as of October 2026.

How many days do you need in Dubai?

3 days covers the highlights: Downtown Dubai & the Burj Khalifa, Old Dubai: souks & the creek and Desert safari. With 5 days you can add Abu Dhabi day trip and Palm Jumeirah, Marina & JBR, and a full week adds Dubai Frame, Miracle Garden & Global Village and Shopping.
