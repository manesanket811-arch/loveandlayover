What's a Good Salary in Singapore?

Take-home pay, rent and real living costs at every salary level, so you can judge an offer before you accept it.

9 min readUpdated November 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: Frank Schulenburg, CC BY-SA 4.0

"Is S$6,000 a good salary in Singapore?" There's no single answer: it depends on whether you're single or bringing family, and above all on your rent. So instead of a yes or no, here's the maths at each salary level, using what we and our friends actually spend.

As of November 2026Rules and prices in Singapore change. We check official sources (HDB, URA, IRAS, MOM), but always confirm the latest before you sign or pay.

The short answer

SingleS$5,000+comfortable, sharing a flat
Couple, one incomeS$8,000+renting a whole flat
FamilyS$12,000+with international school fees

These are our honest estimates, not official figures. They also match Singapore's family pass rules: you need S$6,000 a month to bring your spouse and children, and about S$12,000 to sponsor your parents.

Step 1: work out your take-home pay

Singapore is kind on take-home pay. Foreigners don't pay into CPF (the local pension scheme), and income tax is low. Once you're a tax resident, here's roughly what you keep:

Tax is approximate, before reliefs, using resident rates. It's paid once a year, so set it aside monthly. Annual bonuses are extra and taxed too.

Salary by salary: what life looks like

S$4,000 a month Tight but workable for a single person

This is around S Pass level. Sharing a room, you could save S$500–2,300 a month. Supporting a family on this salary isn't realistic, and it's below the Employment Pass minimum.

S$5,000 a month Comfortable for a single person

Sharing a flat, you can live well and save roughly S$1,400–3,200 a month. Renting a whole flat alone would take most of your savings. Note that it's below the Employment Pass minimum for new applications (S$5,600, rising to S$6,000 in 2027).

S$6,000 a month Good for a single, tight for a couple

A single person can save S$2,400–4,200. A couple on one income in a whole HDB flat would save only a little, or nothing in a pricier area. S$6,000 is also the minimum to bring your spouse and children on a Dependant's Pass.

S$8,000 a month Comfortable for a couple

A couple renting a whole HDB flat can save about S$1,300–3,700 a month; a condo cuts that to S$0–3,300. This is where most couples we know feel comfortable on one income.

S$10,000 a month Very comfortable for a couple

A couple can rent a condo, travel and still save S$1,500–5,000 a month. A family with young children (no school fees yet) can manage well.

S$12,000+ a month Workable for a family

With international school fees, a family can still be tight: savings range from almost nothing to about S$4,600 a month. Many families negotiate a housing or education allowance. This is also about the level needed to sponsor your parents on a Long-Term Visit Pass.

How to judge a job offer

  • Compare after rent, not before. S$6,000 sounds like ₹4.2 lakh a month, but rent alone can take S$2,000–4,000.
  • Ask what's included: annual bonus, housing allowance, relocation support, medical insurance for your family, and annual leave.
  • Check the pass rules: the offer must meet the Employment Pass minimum for your age, and higher salaries also score better under COMPASS. See our guide to getting a job in Singapore.
  • Think about one income vs two: spouses on a Dependant's Pass need their own work pass to work, which isn't easy. Plan on one income at first.
  • Plan your savings yourself: without CPF, your retirement savings and investments are up to you.

FAQs

Is S$5,000 a good salary in Singapore?

For a single person sharing a flat, yes: you can live comfortably and save around S$1,400–3,200 a month. It's tight for a couple renting a whole flat, and it's below the Employment Pass minimum for new applications.

Is S$6,000 a good salary in Singapore?

It's good for a single person, who can save S$2,400–4,200 a month. For a couple on one income renting a whole flat, it's tight. S$6,000 is also the minimum salary to bring your spouse and children on a Dependant's Pass.

Is S$8,000 a good salary in Singapore for a family?

For a couple, S$8,000 is comfortable. For a family paying international school fees, it's usually not enough unless your employer helps with housing or education.

How much tax do foreigners pay in Singapore?

Once you're a tax resident (usually 183 days or more in a year), you pay progressive rates. On S$6,000 a month that's roughly S$2,800 a year before reliefs. Foreigners don't contribute to CPF.

What salary do I need to bring my family to Singapore?

At least S$6,000 a month fixed salary for a Dependant's Pass for your spouse and children, and about S$12,000 to sponsor your parents on a Long-Term Visit Pass.
