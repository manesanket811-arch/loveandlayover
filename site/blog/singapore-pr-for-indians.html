Singapore PR for Indians: How It Works

Who can apply, how the ICA process works, what helps, and what to plan for, including National Service and CPF.

8 min readUpdated November 2026By Sanket & Nikita, living in Singapore
Photo: Mustang Joe, CC0

After a year or two on an Employment Pass, many Indians start thinking about Singapore Permanent Residence (PR). It brings stability, as you're no longer tied to a work pass. But the criteria aren't published and there's no points calculator, so here's what's actually known, from official sources and the experience of people around us.

As of November 2026Rules change, and this is a general guide from our experience, not legal, tax or immigration advice. Always confirm with the Immigration & Checkpoints Authority (ICA) at ica.gov.sg for your own case.

Who can apply

  • Employment Pass and S Pass holders, under ICA's Professionals, Technical Personnel and Skilled Workers (PTS) scheme. Most people apply after working here for at least six months, often longer.
  • Spouses and unmarried children (under 21) of Singapore citizens or PRs.
  • Aged parents of Singapore citizens.
  • Investors under the Global Investor Programme, and some students.

Your spouse and children can be included in your application.

How to apply

  1. Apply online through ICA's e-PR system, logging in with Singpass.
  2. Upload documents: passport, work pass, recent payslips and employment letter, educational certificates, and for family members, marriage and birth certificates.
  3. Pay the application fee per applicant.
  4. Wait for the outcome: processing usually takes several months, and it can take longer for complex cases.
  5. If approved, you complete the formalities with ICA and receive your PR card and a Re-Entry Permit.

What ICA looks at

ICA assesses each application as a whole and doesn't publish a checklist. Factors commonly understood to matter include:

  • Your job and salary, and how stable your employment is
  • Your qualifications
  • How long you've lived and worked here
  • Family ties, for example a spouse or children who are citizens or PRs
  • Integration: community involvement, volunteering and ties to Singapore
  • Age and the overall family profile
Be wary of "guaranteed PR" servicesNo agent can guarantee approval or speed up ICA. Some consultants help prepare applications, but you can apply yourself, and the decision is ICA's alone.

Things to plan for before you apply

  • National Service for sons: male children who become PRs are generally liable for National Service when they turn 18. This is a big decision for families; read the rules carefully before including sons.
  • CPF contributions: as a PR you and your employer contribute to CPF, so your take-home pay falls, but you build retirement and housing savings.
  • Re-Entry Permit: you need a valid one to keep your PR when you travel.
  • Indian citizenship: PR doesn't affect your Indian citizenship. Becoming a Singapore citizen later would mean giving up Indian citizenship, as India doesn't allow dual citizenship (OCI is an option afterwards).
  • Housing: PRs can buy resale HDB flats under certain conditions, and pay different stamp duties from foreigners on private property.

If you're rejected

ICA doesn't usually give reasons. Many people reapply later, after a pay rise, a promotion or more time in Singapore. Make sure every document is complete and up to date when you reapply.

FAQs

Can Indians get PR in Singapore?

Yes. Indians on an Employment Pass or S Pass can apply under ICA's PTS scheme, usually after working in Singapore for at least six months. Approval isn't guaranteed and criteria aren't published.

How long does Singapore PR take?

Processing usually takes several months, and can be longer for complex cases. Check ICA's website for current timelines.

Do sons of PRs have to do National Service in Singapore?

Male children who become Singapore PRs are generally liable for National Service when they reach 18. Families should understand this before including sons in an application.

Do I lose Indian citizenship if I become a Singapore PR?

No. PR doesn't affect your Indian citizenship. Becoming a Singapore citizen would require giving up Indian citizenship, since India doesn't allow dual citizenship.
